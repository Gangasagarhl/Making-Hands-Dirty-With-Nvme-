PART XXVI

Debugging Methodology

Protocol knowledge tells you what should happen. Methodology is what gets you from "it hangs sometimes" to a fix that provably works. This Part makes one habit automatic: separate what you observed from what you inferred from what you merely suspect, and do not call anything a root cause until evidence has eliminated the alternatives.

79 The Debugging Framework: Observation, Inference, Hypothesis, Evidence, Root Cause

79.1 Why this chapter exists

The most expensive debugging mistakes are not technical. They are: fixing the first plausible cause and declaring victory; confusing "happened before" with "caused"; reasoning from a trace whose coverage nobody checked; and changing three things at once. A framework is a defence against your own cleverness.

  • Beginner: label every statement in a bug discussion as observation, inference or hypothesis.
  • Intermediate: walk the 12-step framework from symptom to regression test.
  • Advanced: design eliminating experiments that change exactly one variable.
  • Senior: grade confidence explicitly and write reports others can audit.
  • Debugging / validation: every fix ships with a regression test that failed before the fix.

Prerequisites: Trace Academy rules, kernel logs.

79.3 The five habits

HabitDefinitionExample statement
ObservationWhat we actually know: directly recorded, with source and scope"dmesg at 931.100 s: I/O tag 23 QID 3 timeout." "Trace (both directions, no filters, 0–2 s): no CplD for MRd tag 0x2a."
InferenceWhat logically follows from observations and protocol rules"Since the SQE was never delivered, the controller could not have executed CID 0x17."
HypothesisWhat might be happening; must predict something testable"The switch drops completions under mixed load — predicts the CplD is visible upstream but not downstream."
EvidenceWhat would prove or disprove the hypothesis"A second probe on the upstream link during the next failure."
Root causeThe most likely explanation after elimination, with confidence"Switch firmware drops CplD when … (high confidence: reproduced 5/5, fixed with FW x.y, regression passes)."
🧒 Kid analogy — the detective notebook

A good detective's notebook has three columns: "I saw" (muddy boots by the door), "So" (someone came in from the garden), "Maybe" (the gardener? the dog-walker?). She never writes "the gardener did it" in the first column. And before arresting anyone she checks: did the dog-walker have muddy boots too?

🧩 Where the analogy breaks

In engineering you can usually re-run the crime: reproduce the failure under controlled changes. That makes elimination far stronger than in detective stories — and makes skipping it inexcusable.

79.4 Worked exercise: the slow Gen5 drive

Symptom: "New Gen5 SSD reaches only 6.8 GB/s sequential read; datasheet says ~14 GB/s."

CategoryStatements
Observationfio seqread 128k QD32 numjobs=1: 6.8 GB/s. lspci -vv on the SSD: LnkCap Speed 32GT/s, Width x4, LnkSta Speed 16GT/s (downgraded), Width x4; on the root port: LnkCap Speed 16GT/s. CPU usage of the fio thread 100 %.
InferenceThe link is Gen4 x4 because the root port only supports 16 GT/s → payload ceiling ~7 GB/s (Chapter 90). The observed 6.8 GB/s is consistent with the link ceiling. CPU 100 % is a second potential limit.
HypothesesH1 platform Gen4 port limits link (strong); H2 single fio job CPU-bound (possible); H3 drive thermally throttling (weak: no data).
EvidenceMove drive to a Gen5-capable port (H1); run numjobs=4 with the same total QD (H2); log temperature and throughput over 10 min (H3).
Root causeAfter moving to a Gen5 port: 13.1 GB/s with numjobs=4, 9.5 GB/s with numjobs=1 → H1 primary, H2 secondary. Confidence high (two one-variable experiments).

79.5 Correlation is not causation (performance causality)

Latency rose after a PCIe Recovery event. Did the Recovery cause it? Possible structures: (a) Recovery → stall → latency (causal); (b) a third factor causes both — e.g. temperature raises both link errors and NAND read-retry rates; (c) coincidence in a noisy system. Evidence for causality: temporal precision (latency spike begins within microseconds of the Recovery, not seconds); mechanism (Recovery duration explains the spike's magnitude); intervention (removing Recovery — lower speed, better channel — removes spikes); dose-response (more Recoveries → proportionally more spikes). Require at least intervention before claiming cause.

79.6 Device-specific limitations

🏭 Implementation-dependent

Two NVMe SSDs that expose the same standard interface may differ in firmware, NAND type and generation, controller architecture, DRAM or HMB-based mapping caches, internal scheduling, garbage-collection policy, thermal behavior and power management. Standard-visible behavior (latency distributions, throughput curves) is the output of all of these. Do not infer internal architecture — "it must be GC", "the cache is 8 GB" — from external behavior without evidence such as vendor telemetry, controlled preconditioning experiments, or vendor confirmation. Say "consistent with" rather than "is".

79.7 Analyzer and log limitations as evidence rules

  • A trace is evidence with scope: filtering, capture gaps, dropped packets, timestamp limits, decoder limits, version-dependent UI, proprietary annotations (Chapter 77).
  • A log is evidence of what was detected and printed, not of everything that happened (Chapter 71).
  • A counter is evidence of how many, not when — sample it over time.
  • Absence of evidence counts only within proven coverage.

79.8 Bug report template

TITLE        : <layer>: <observable failure> when <condition>

REPRODUCTION : workload command, rate (k/N runs), time to failure

OBSERVATIONS : numbered, each with source + timestamp
FIRST DEVIATION : which observation, why it is the first
INFERENCES   : each citing observation numbers and the rule applied
HYPOTHESES   : ranked; for each: prediction, evidence for/against, status (open/eliminated)

79.9 Debugging exercises

🎯 Exercise 79.1 — Classify the statements SAFE

Label each: (a) "The SSD is overheating." (b) "smart-log temperature: 352 K at 10:02, 341 K at 09:50." (c) "Throughput dropped 40 % at 10:01." (d) "Thermal throttling would reduce throughput." (e) "The drive firmware throttles at 350 K."

Answer

(a) hypothesis; (b) observation (352 K ≈ 79 °C); (c) observation (needs source: fio log?); (d) inference from general knowledge — but becomes a hypothesis about this case; (e) a claim about implementation — unverified unless from vendor documentation or WCTEMP + throttling counters (impl).

🎯 Exercise 79.2 — Full framework on a reset loop SAFE

Symptom: after a firmware update on a lab drive, the host logs a controller reset every ~2 minutes. dmesg shows I/O ... timeout, reset controller each time, no AER. smart-log: num_err_log_entries unchanged. Error log: empty. Write Observation / Inference / Hypothesis / Evidence / Root cause.

Model answer

Observation: periodic timeouts (≈120 s), resets succeed, no AER, no new error-log entries, began after FW update. Inference: link-level errors unlikely to be reported (no AER); controller did not consider any command errored; commands are not completing within io_timeout (30 s), i.e. something stalls ≥30 s every 2 min — or completions are not noticed. Hypotheses: H1 new FW stalls during a periodic internal task (e.g. background operation) >30 s; H2 interrupt delivery broken for one vector after FW change (CQEs posted, not signalled); H3 APST/power-state transition in new FW has long exit latency; H4 host-side change coincident with FW update. Evidence: kernel message "completion polled" would support H2; disable APST (nvme_core.default_ps_max_latency_us=0, lab only) for H3; analyzer: does the CQE exist before the timeout? (H1 vs H2); roll back FW keeping host identical (H4). Root cause: chosen only after these; report confidence.

🎯 Exercise 79.3 — Spot the causality error SAFE

"We saw 12 corrected Receiver Errors last night and the database had 3 latency spikes; therefore the link causes the spikes." Critique.

Answer

No temporal alignment shown (do the spikes coincide within microseconds–milliseconds with errors?), no mechanism magnitude (a corrected error costs a replay of ~µs, not ms), no intervention. Likely coincidence or common cause; collect per-event timestamps and test by eliminating errors.

Quiz

  1. At which step do hypotheses first appear?

    Answer

    Step 7, after the first deviation, layer and available evidence are established.

  2. What makes a hypothesis useful?

    Answer

    It predicts an observation that an experiment can confirm or refute.

  3. Why must the regression test fail before the fix?

    Answer

    Otherwise it does not exercise the failure condition and proves nothing.

Interview questions

  1. Intermediate How do you avoid changing multiple variables?
    Model answer

    Plan experiments as a table: baseline, one change per run, repeat enough to beat variance; record everything else as fixed.

  2. Senior A manager wants a root cause today; you have two surviving hypotheses. What do you report?
    Model answer

    Both, ranked, with the evidence for each, the experiment and time needed to decide, and a safe interim mitigation that works under both.

80 Decision Tree, Debugging by Layer, Evidence Escalation and the Cross-Layer Lab

80.1 Why this chapter exists

With twelve layers between an application and a NAND die, knowing where to look next is half the job. This chapter gives a diagnostic heuristic (decision tree), a layer-by-layer table, an evidence-escalation ladder, and a cross-layer capstone.

  • Intermediate: use the decision tree to choose the next check.
  • Advanced: map symptoms to layers with typical evidence and next checks.
  • Senior: plan evidence escalation: what each additional source makes possible, and its cost.

80.2 The debugging decision tree

⚠️ A heuristic, not a law

This tree orders checks from cheapest/most fundamental to most specific. Real failures can involve several layers simultaneously, and some evidence (e.g. a lost interrupt) appears healthy at lower layers. Use it to choose the next check, not to conclude.

80.6 Cross-layer lab: "Read occasionally takes 10 seconds"

🧪 Lab 80.1 — Which layer owns the 10-second read? SAFE

Classification: offline analysis of synthetic evidence; non-destructive.

Scenario. A database reports that roughly once an hour a single 16 KiB read takes about 10 s. No application errors. You have four evidence sources (synthetic).

1. OS log (journalctl -k, the hour around one event at 14:02:11):

13:41:07 nvme 0000:01:00.0: AER: Corrected error received: 0000:01:00.0
13:41:07 nvme 0000:01:00.0: PCIe Bus Error: severity=Corrected, type=Physical Layer, (Receiver ID)
13:41:07 nvme 0000:01:00.0:    [ 0] RxErr

Questions. (1) Which layer owns the delay? (2) What role does the 13:41 RxErr play? (3) What is the most likely mechanism, and how confident can you be? (4) What experiment would raise confidence? (5) What mitigation and regression test do you propose?

Hints

Use blktrace phases to split host from device. Use the trace to split link from controller. Use the LBA pattern.

Solution

Observations: host queueing 4 µs, device time 9.97 s (blktrace D→C); on the link, the SQE was fetched within ~1 µs, data appeared after 9.97 s, no link-level errors in the capture window, other queues progressed; CQE status success; no media_errors increment; slow reads cluster on old, rarely-read LBAs; one corrected RxErr 21 minutes earlier.

Inferences: host software and PCIe transport are not the owners (the command reached the controller promptly; the link carried other traffic normally). The delay lies inside the controller between SQE receipt and data transfer: controller firmware / media layer. Success status with no media-error increment means the data was eventually recovered.

(2) The RxErr is a distractor: 21 minutes earlier, corrected, and the slow command's capture shows no link errors. Correlation without temporal or mechanistic link.

(3) Hypothesis (medium confidence): media-level error recovery on aged data (e.g. extensive read-retry / soft-decode steps on cells whose charge drifted), allowed to run long because no time-limited error recovery is configured. This is consistent with the evidence but the internal mechanism is not observable via the standard interface impl. Alternatives: firmware internal lock/contention affecting that LBA range; background operation on the same die.

(4) Experiments: on a lab copy (never production): read the slow range repeatedly — is it consistently slow? Rewrite the range and re-read — does latency normalize? Request vendor telemetry / read-retry counters (vendor-specific logs). If supported, set a time-limited error recovery value on a lab drive and observe whether the command now fails fast with an error instead of succeeding late.

(5) Mitigation: application/storage-level: background scrubbing (periodic reads) or vendor-recommended data refresh; configure time-limited error recovery where appropriate for redundant systems (fail fast, read from replica) impl. Regression test: a scrub job plus a latency SLO monitor on p99.99 per LBA region; in qualification, an aging test (retention bake per vendor guidance) followed by full-range read latency histogram with a maximum-latency threshold.

Owner layer: NVMe controller firmware/media.

Quiz

  1. In the decision tree, what comes after "command status is successful"?

    Answer

    Interrupt/completion delivery to the host, then software processing.

  2. blktrace shows Q→D = 9 s, D→C = 80 µs. Which half owns the delay?

    Answer

    The host side before dispatch (block layer queueing, scheduler, tag exhaustion) — not the device.

  3. What new knowledge does controller firmware logging add over a PCIe analyzer?

    Answer

    Internal state: why the controller was slow or failed (media operations, internal errors), which the link cannot show.

Interview questions

  1. Advanced Walk the decision tree for "device occasionally disappears".
    Model answer

    Link: does it go down (pciehp Link Down, DPC, Surprise Down)? If yes, physical/power/reset investigation with analyzer training capture and scope on power/PERST#. If the link stays up but CSTS reads all-ones, the device stopped responding — firmware/hardware hang. Escalate accordingly.

  2. Senior Which evidence would you collect first for a fleet-wide intermittent issue and why?
    Model answer

    Cheap, scalable evidence: kernel logs, AER counters, SMART/error-log deltas, FW versions, platform versions — to find correlations by population; then targeted analyzer captures on a reproducing unit.
