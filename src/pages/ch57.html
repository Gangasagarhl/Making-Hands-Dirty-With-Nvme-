An NVMe SSD keeps a running account of its own health: how hot it is, how much spare flash it has left, how much of its rated life it has used, how much data it has moved, how often it lost power without warning, and how many errors it has seen. This chapter teaches every field of the SMART / Health Information log page, the logs around it (error information, endurance group, telemetry, persistent events), how the host is alerted, and how to read the numbers without fooling yourself.

Why this chapter exists

Every fleet operator, every validation engineer and every support engineer reads SMART data. It decides when drives are replaced, whether a warranty claim is accepted, whether a field failure was caused by heat or by wear, and whether a platform shut drives down cleanly. Yet SMART fields are among the most misread numbers in storage: data units that are not bytes, a "percentage" that can exceed 100, a power-on-hours counter that may not count every hour, and an "unsafe shutdowns" counter that blames the drive for a platform's mistakes.

Part XVIII moves from the PCIe transport to NVMe features above it. Health reporting is the natural first stop: it uses only commands you already know (Get Log Page, Asynchronous Event Request, Get/Set Features), and it connects directly to the errors, resets and power topics of Parts XV and XVI.

Learning objectives

  • Retrieve the SMART / Health Information log (log identifier 02h) and decode every field, including units and rounding.
  • Interpret Critical Warning bits and know which host actions each should trigger.
  • Explain composite temperature, temperature sensors, thresholds and thermal management counters.
  • Explain available spare, its threshold, and percentage used, and why percentage used is an estimate.
  • Convert data units read/written (units of 1000 × 512 bytes) to bytes, and relate them to host commands and write amplification.
  • Interpret power cycles, power-on hours, unsafe shutdowns, media and data integrity errors, and error log entry counts, including their common misreadings.
  • Use the Error Information log, Endurance Group Information log, Persistent Event log and Telemetry logs alongside SMART.
  • Configure and handle asynchronous SMART/health events.

The story

Think of a bicycle with a little notebook tied to it. Every day the bicycle writes in its notebook: how hot its tyres got, how many kilometres it rode, how many times somebody fell off it, how worn its brake pads are, and how many spare inner tubes are left in the saddlebag. If the bicycle notices something really bad — the last spare tube is nearly used, or the brakes are hot — it rings its bell to tell you right away.

When you want to know if the bicycle is healthy, you read the notebook. But you have to know how it counts: it writes kilometres in "thousands of half-metres," and when it says "worn 80%," that is its own guess, not a measurement.

A car's dashboard and service record: an odometer (data written), an engine hours meter (power-on hours), a temperature gauge and warning lights (critical warnings), a log of engine starts (power cycles), a record of "ignition cut while engine running" events (unsafe shutdowns), and a manufacturer's estimate of remaining tyre life (percentage used). The dashboard is standardized; the mechanic's diagnostic dump (telemetry) is in the manufacturer's own format.

What it hides: an SSD's "odometer" counts only host data, not the extra writes the drive makes internally (garbage collection, wear levelling). Real flash wear depends on those internal writes. And there is no single "engine": a drive may have several temperature sensors and an internal composite figure that is a weighted judgement, not one reading.

Where it misleads: a car's warning light usually means "a part is failing." Several NVMe critical warnings mean "the drive has protected itself" — for example, going read-only to preserve data — which is a designed safe state, not a malfunction. And "unsafe shutdown" is often the platform's fault, not the drive's.

Beginner explanation

"SMART" originally meant Self-Monitoring, Analysis and Reporting Technology, a scheme from ATA hard drives with vendor-defined attributes. NVMe standardized the idea into one fixed-format, 512-byte SMART / Health Information log page, log identifier 02h. Every NVMe controller must support it, and every field has a defined meaning and unit.

The host reads it with the admin command Get Log Page (opcode 02h), asking for log identifier 02h. The controller copies the 512 bytes into host memory by DMA and posts a completion. Tools such as nvme smart-log and smartctl do exactly this and print the fields.

The fields fall into five groups:

Besides being polled, the controller can tell the host when something becomes critical, through an Asynchronous Event Request (AER in NVMe terminology — not to be confused with PCIe Advanced Error Reporting) that completes with a SMART/Health event type.

Two different "AER"s meet in this course. PCIe AER is Advanced Error Reporting (Chapter 46). NVMe's Asynchronous Event Request is an admin command (opcode 0Ch) that the host leaves outstanding so the controller can complete it when an event occurs. Always say which one you mean.

Engineering explanation

Retrieving the log

Get Log Page carries the log identifier in Command Dword 10 bits 07:00 (LID), a Log Specific Parameter, a Retain Asynchronous Event (RAE) bit, and the number of dwords to return (0's based, split into NUMDL in CDW10 bits 31:16 and NUMDU in CDW11). For the full 512-byte SMART log, NUMD = 128 dwords → NUMDL = 127 (7Fh), so CDW10 = 007F0002h when RAE = 0. The data pointer (PRP1, or an SGL) gives the destination buffer.

The Namespace Identifier (NSID) selects scope:

  • NSID = FFFFFFFFh (or 0 on many controllers; check the rules for your revision): controller-wide or NVM-subsystem-wide information.
  • A specific NSID: per-namespace information, only if the controller reports support in the Log Page Attributes (LPA) field of Identify Controller. Many fields are not meaningful per namespace; the specification says which.

Version-dependent: the scope rules for SMART (controller vs NVM subsystem vs namespace, and the meaning of NSID 0 vs FFFFFFFFh) have been refined across NVMe 1.x and 2.x. Check the "SMART / Health Information" section of the NVMe Base Specification 2.4 — Log Page Identifiers.

The Asynchronous Event Configuration feature (Feature Identifier 0Bh) has matching enable bits: the host chooses which critical warnings generate an asynchronous event. A bit can be set in the log even if its event is disabled.

Critical Warning bits reflect current conditions (temperature) or latched conditions (read-only, reliability degraded). A temperature warning clears when the drive cools; a read-only warning does not go away. Monitoring should store the history of the byte, not just alert on the present value.

Temperature

Composite Temperature is a single value in Kelvin that the controller derives from its sensors to represent the device's thermal state — the value the host should compare with the thresholds. Its derivation is Implementation-dependent. Up to eight raw Temperature Sensor values are reported separately (0 means not implemented). Subtract 273 (more precisely 273.15) for degrees Celsius.

Thresholds come from two places:

  • Identify Controller reports WCTEMP (Warning Composite Temperature Threshold) and CCTEMP (Critical Composite Temperature Threshold), in Kelvin. The SMART fields "Warning/Critical Composite Temperature Time" count minutes spent at or above these.
  • Temperature Threshold feature (Feature Identifier 04h) lets the host read and set over- and under-temperature thresholds for the composite temperature and for each sensor. Crossing them sets Critical Warning bit 1.

The Host Controlled Thermal Management feature lets the host set two thermal management temperatures (TMT1 for light throttling, TMT2 for heavy throttling); the SMART log counts transitions into each and the time spent in each. Throttling is the drive reducing performance to cool down — a common, silent cause of "the SSD got slow."

Available spare and percentage used

Flash blocks wear out. SSDs keep spare blocks to replace blocks that fail. Available Spare is the remaining spare capacity as a normalized percentage (100 = all spare left). The vendor sets the Available Spare Threshold; when spare falls below it, Critical Warning bit 0 sets. Spare usually stays at 100% for most of a drive's life and then falls quickly near the end — or after a cluster of block failures.

Percentage Used is the vendor's estimate of how much of the device's rated life has been consumed, based on actual usage and the manufacturer's prediction of life. 100 means the rated endurance is reached, not that the drive fails at once; values above 100 are allowed, saturating at 255. The specification asks for it to be updated about once per power-on hour. It is the field closest to "wear," but its formula is the vendor's.

Percentage Used, the composite temperature algorithm, the spare threshold, which events count as media errors, and how Controller Busy Time is measured are all vendor decisions inside the specification's definitions. Compare these fields across drives of the same model and firmware, not across vendors.

Data units, commands and busy time

Bytes ≈ DU × 1000 × 512
Bytes
approximate host bytes read (or written)
DU
Data Units Read (or Written) from the SMART log; one unit is 1000 × 512 = 512,000 bytes, and the controller rounds up

The unit is always 512 bytes, even for namespaces formatted with 4 KiB logical blocks: a 4 KiB write counts 8 units of 512 bytes toward the "thousands." Metadata is not counted. Data units count host data transferred by I/O commands (for example Read, Write, Compare for the NVM command set); the specification defines which commands contribute.

Host Read Commands and Host Write Commands count completed commands. Dividing data by commands gives the average transfer size — a quick check of the workload's shape. Controller Busy Time counts minutes during which the controller had I/O outstanding; on a lightly loaded system it is far smaller than power-on time.

Write amplification

Data Units Written counts only host writes. The flash also absorbs internal writes (garbage collection, wear levelling, metadata). The ratio is the write amplification factor (WAF):

WAF = MediaUnitsWritten ÷ DataUnitsWritten
WAF
write amplification factor (≥ 1 in normal operation)
MediaUnitsWritten
units written to the media, reported in the Endurance Group Information log (where supported)
DataUnitsWritten
host data units written for the same scope and period, in the same units

The SMART log alone cannot give WAF; the Endurance Group Information log can (see below), and some vendors report it in vendor-specific logs.

History counters

  • Power Cycles: number of power cycles. Compare with the platform's boot count: a drive with far more cycles than the server has boots is being power-cycled by something else (hot-plug, backplane faults, power capping).
  • Power On Hours: hours powered on. Whether time in non-operational power states counts can differ (the specification has notes on this; check your revision). Not a wall clock.
  • Unsafe Shutdowns: incremented when power is lost without a shutdown notification having been completed (CC.SHN written and CSTS.SHST reporting completion; see Chapter 48). Often a platform or driver problem: the OS powered off before the drive finished, the BMC cut slot power, or a hot-unplug occurred.
  • Media and Data Integrity Errors: unrecovered data integrity errors the controller detected — uncorrectable ECC, CRC or end-to-end protection (Protection Information) check failures, LBA tag mismatch. Each normally caused a command to fail. Non-zero values deserve attention; a rising count is serious.
  • Number of Error Information Log Entries: lifetime count of entries created in the Error Information log (01h). Many are harmless (an invalid field in a vendor tool's command, for example). The count says "something was logged," the Error Information log says what.

Endurance groups

An endurance group is a collection of NVM sets (and their namespaces) whose wear is managed together. Drives with one endurance group report the same wear in SMART and in the Endurance Group Information log; drives with several (for example, separate groups for different media regions or tenants) report each separately, and the SMART log's Endurance Group Critical Warning Summary tells the host that at least one group needs attention. The Endurance Group Information log also reports Endurance Estimate (an estimate of total data units writable over the group's life) and Media Units Written, which gives write amplification.

Telemetry

Telemetry logs carry vendor-defined diagnostic data inside a standardized container: a header (with identifiers, reason information and generation numbers) and data areas of increasing size (Data Area 1, 2, 3 and, with extended telemetry, Data Area 4). The host requests a host-initiated capture by setting the Create Telemetry Host-Initiated Data flag in the Log Specific Parameter on Get Log Page; the controller may capture controller-initiated data on its own and signal it with an asynchronous event. The content is opaque to the host — it is for the vendor's tools — but collecting it at the right moment is the host's job.

Collect telemetry before resetting, power-cycling or updating firmware on a misbehaving drive. Resets can discard the volatile state the vendor needs. A controller-initiated telemetry generation number that changed since the last check means the drive captured something on its own.

Asynchronous events for health

The host keeps one or more Asynchronous Event Request commands (admin opcode 0Ch) outstanding. When an enabled health condition occurs, the controller completes one with an event type of SMART / Health Status and event information (for example reliability, temperature threshold, or spare below threshold) and identifies the associated log page (02h). The host must then read that log page (with RAE = 0) to clear the condition and re-arm the event; until it does, the controller does not report the same event type again. A new Asynchronous Event Request must also be submitted to replace the one that completed.

The central concept on the ladder

The bicycle's notebook records heat, kilometres, falls and worn brakes, and the bicycle rings its bell when something is really wrong.

The SSD keeps standardized counters and warnings in a 512-byte log; the host reads it, and the SSD can raise an event when a critical condition starts.

Controller firmware maintains counters in its RAM and persists them to flash periodically; temperature sensors feed a composite value; the flash translation layer tracks spare blocks and erase counts.

Get Log Page (02h) with LID 02h and NUMD 128 dwords; the controller DMAs 512 bytes; CQE; Asynchronous Event Request completions for health events.

On PCIe: doorbell MWr, SQE fetch MRd, two 256-byte MWr (MPS 256) carrying the log, CQE MWr, MSI-X MWr. The analyzer's NVMe decode shows LID and field values.

The 16 questions, for the SMART / Health log

1. What is it?
A mandatory, standardized 512-byte log of device health and lifetime counters.
2. Why was it created?
To give hosts a vendor-independent way to monitor wear, temperature, errors and usage.
3. What problem does it solve?
Predicting failure and end of life, diagnosing environment and platform issues, fleet management.
4. What existed before?
ATA SMART with vendor-specific attribute IDs, thresholds and raw-value formats.
5. Why is it better?
Fixed fields, fixed units and standardized warnings; one decoder works for every NVMe device.
6. Who creates it?
The controller's firmware.
7. Who consumes it?
Host drivers (for events), monitoring tools, fleet management, validation and support engineers, NVMe-MI management controllers.
8. Where does it live?
Inside the controller, persisted to non-volatile storage; delivered into host memory on request.
9. Memory/registers?
No registers; a host buffer described by PRP/SGL; Identify Controller fields (LPA, WCTEMP, CCTEMP, ELPE); features 04h and 0Bh.
10. Before it?
Controller ready, admin queue running; for events, Asynchronous Event Requests outstanding and Asynchronous Event Configuration set.
11. After it?
Host decides: alert, throttle, migrate data, replace drive, or collect telemetry.
12. If wrong?
Misread units cause wrong endurance planning; unhandled warnings lead to data loss; misattributed unsafe shutdowns blame the wrong party.
13. Analyzer shows?
Get Log Page SQE (CDW10 = 007F0002h for the full log), 512 bytes of data MWr, CQE.
14. Linux shows?
nvme smart-log, smartctl -a, hwmon temperature for NVMe, kernel messages for AER-driven events.
15. Validation tests?
Field units and rounding, counter persistence across power cycles, thresholds and events, unsafe shutdown detection, per-namespace support, telemetry capture.
16. Senior suspects?
Platform power sequencing (unsafe shutdowns), cooling (throttling), workload shape (WAF), firmware version changes in counter behaviour.

Hardware view

  • Counters are persisted periodically. After an unsafe power loss, the last few minutes of counting may be lost (an implementation choice); expect small inconsistencies after power failures.
  • Spare and wear are tracked by the flash translation layer (FTL) per block; the SMART values are summaries.
  • Power-loss detection uses supply monitoring; drives with power-loss protection capacitors can finish in-flight work after power loss, but still count an unsafe shutdown if no shutdown notification was completed.
  • Thermal throttling is enforced by firmware reducing NAND parallelism, clock rates or link activity.

Specification view

The SMART / Health Information log, Critical Warning, the related logs, features and the Asynchronous Event mechanism are defined in the NVMe Base Specification 2.4 — Log Page Identifiers; SMART / Health Information; Error Information; Endurance Group Information; Telemetry; Persistent Event Log; Asynchronous Event Request; Features (Temperature Threshold, Asynchronous Event Configuration, Host Controlled Thermal Management). Command-set-specific rules for which commands count toward Data Units and Host Commands are in the NVM Command Set Specification 1.3 (and the ZNS and KV command set specifications for those command sets). Out-of-band access to health data is in the NVMe Management Interface Specification 2.2 (see Chapter 63).

What is verified? LID 02h for SMART / Health; the field list and byte layout above; Kelvin temperatures; data unit = 1000 × 512 bytes rounded up; Percentage Used may exceed 100 and saturates at 255; Critical Warning bits 0–5; Get Log Page opcode 02h; Asynchronous Event Request opcode 0Ch; Feature Identifiers 04h (Temperature Threshold) and 0Bh (Asynchronous Event Configuration); log identifiers 01h, 03h, 06h, 07h, 08h, 09h, 0Dh.

What is derived? Bytes from data units; average I/O size; TB per day; linear life projection; WAF from Endurance Group log fields; all the interpretation rules of thumb in this chapter.

What is implementation-dependent? Composite temperature derivation; Percentage Used formula; spare threshold value; which internal events count as media errors; persistence interval of counters; telemetry content; throttling behaviour; whether per-namespace SMART is supported.

What is version-dependent? Thermal management fields and the Endurance Group Critical Warning Summary were added in 1.3/1.4; PMR warning bit with PMR support; Endurance Group, Persistent Event and telemetry extensions over 1.3–2.x; SMART scope rules refined in 2.x; voltage monitoring added in 2.4.

What must be checked in the specification? Exact NSID scope rules; which commands increment Host Read/Write Commands and Data Units for each command set; Power On Hours treatment of non-operational states; event information codes for SMART/Health asynchronous events; any new fields in reserved bytes in 2.4; voltage monitoring identifiers.

What the specification does not say: when a drive should be replaced; what Percentage Used value means "failing"; how to weigh spare against percentage used.

What should never be assumed? That Data Units are bytes, sectors or 4 KiB blocks; that Percentage Used is linear in time; that Unsafe Shutdowns are the drive's fault; that a zero Critical Warning means a healthy drive (a drive can fail suddenly with no warning); that values are comparable across vendors.

Protocol view

Reading the SMART log is an ordinary admin command. On the lab system (admin SQ at 0x1_0000_0000, admin CQ at 0x1_0000_1000, MPS 256 bytes), the 512-byte log arrives as two 256-byte memory writes.

Direction and order: host writes SQE into host memory; host MWr to the admin SQ tail doorbell (BAR0 + 1000h); controller MRd fetches the SQE; controller MWr writes the log into the buffer (posted, so it is ordered before the CQE that follows in the same direction); controller MWr writes the CQE; controller MWr sends MSI-X vector 0 (admin). The host consumes the CQE and writes the admin CQ head doorbell (BAR0 + 1004h).

Status codes to expect: Successful Completion; Invalid Log Page (if the LID is not supported); Invalid Field in Command (for example a per-namespace request on a controller that does not support it, or an offset/length combination it does not allow). See Chapter 44 for the status code types.

5—41Get Log 02hSpare 8 (< threshold 10), CW=0x05, Media errs 19Bits 0 and 2: spare low and reliability degraded

Reasoning: Percentage Used barely moved (wear-out is not the cause); spare fell fast with rising media errors — a pattern consistent with a failing NAND die or channel rather than normal end of life. The Error Information log entries for days 29–41 (LBAs, namespaces, status) and a telemetry capture belong in the vendor report. The warning signal was available on day 29, twelve days before the critical warning — which is why fleets track deltas, not thresholds.

Code / pseudocode

SMART log decoder (runnable)

This decoder is executable with the standard library. Without arguments it decodes a synthetic log; with a file argument it decodes 512 raw bytes saved from a real device.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""smart_decode.py - decode an NVMe SMART / Health Information log page (02h).

Usage:
  python3 smart_decode.py               decode a built-in SYNTHETIC example
  python3 smart_decode.py smart.bin     decode 512 raw bytes, e.g. saved with
      sudo nvme smart-log /dev/nvme0 --raw-binary > smart.bin
Byte offsets follow the SMART / Health Information log layout of the NVMe Base
Specification; confirm against the revision your device implements.
"""
import struct
import sys

WARN_BITS = {
    0: "available spare below threshold",
    1: "temperature above an over-temperature or below an under-temperature threshold",
    2: "NVM subsystem reliability degraded",
    3: "media placed in read-only mode",
    4: "volatile memory backup device failed",
    5: "Persistent Memory Region read-only or unreliable",
}

def u128(b, off):
    lo, hi = struct.unpack_from("<QQ", b, off)
    return lo | hi << 64

def synthetic():
    b = bytearray(512)
    b[0] = 0x00                                   # critical warning
    struct.pack_into("<H", b, 1, 311)             # composite temp, kelvin (~38 C)
    b[3], b[4], b[5] = 100, 10, 7                 # spare %, threshold %, % used
    for off, val in [(32, 61_440_000), (48, 98_304_000), (64, 1_830_000_000),
                     (80, 2_410_000_000), (96, 21_300), (112, 412), (128, 14_260),
                     (144, 9), (160, 0), (176, 37)]:
        struct.pack_into("<QQ", b, off, val, 0)
    struct.pack_into("<II", b, 192, 12, 0)        # warning / critical temp time (min)
    for i, k in enumerate([309, 318, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0]):
        struct.pack_into("<H", b, 200 + 2 * i, k) # sensors 1..8 (0 = not implemented)
    struct.pack_into("<IIII", b, 216, 3, 0, 840, 0)
    return bytes(b)

def decode(b):
    if len(b) < 512:
        raise SystemExit("need 512 bytes")
    cw = b[0]
    print(f"Critical Warning           : 0x{cw:02x}")
    for bit, text in WARN_BITS.items():
        if cw >> bit & 1:
            print(f"   bit {bit}: {text}")
    if cw & 0xC0:
        print("   reserved bits set: check your specification revision")
    t = struct.unpack_from("<H", b, 1)[0]
    print(f"Composite Temperature      : {t} K ({t - 273} C)")
    print(f"Available Spare            : {b[3]} %  (threshold {b[4]} %)")
    print(f"Percentage Used            : {b[5]} %  (may exceed 100, saturates at 255)")
    print(f"Endurance Grp Crit Summary : 0x{b[6]:02x}")
    dur, duw = u128(b, 32), u128(b, 48)
    # 1 data unit = 1000 x 512 bytes = 512,000 bytes (counter rounds up)
    print(f"Data Units Read            : {dur:,}  (~{dur * 512_000 / 1e12:.2f} TB)")
    print(f"Data Units Written         : {duw:,}  (~{duw * 512_000 / 1e12:.2f} TB)")
    hr, hw = u128(b, 64), u128(b, 80)
    print(f"Host Read Commands         : {hr:,}")
    print(f"Host Write Commands        : {hw:,}")
    print(f"Controller Busy Time       : {u128(b, 96):,} min")
    print(f"Power Cycles               : {u128(b, 112):,}")
    poh = u128(b, 128)
    print(f"Power On Hours             : {poh:,}  (~{poh / 24 / 365:.2f} years)")
    print(f"Unsafe Shutdowns           : {u128(b, 144):,}")
    print(f"Media and Data Integ Errors: {u128(b, 160):,}")
    print(f"Error Info Log Entries     : {u128(b, 176):,}")
    wt, ct = struct.unpack_from("<II", b, 192)
    print(f"Warning / Critical Temp Time: {wt} / {ct} min")
    for i in range(8):
        k = struct.unpack_from("<H", b, 200 + 2 * i)[0]
        if k:
            print(f"Temperature Sensor {i + 1}       : {k} K ({k - 273} C)")
    c1, c2, s1, s2 = struct.unpack_from("<IIII", b, 216)
    print(f"Thermal Mgmt T1/T2 count   : {c1} / {c2}   time: {s1} s / {s2} s")
    # Derived figures (not reported by the device)
    if hw:
        print(f"[derived] average write size ~ {duw * 512_000 / hw / 1024:.1f} KiB per command")
    if poh:
        tb_day = duw * 512_000 / 1e12 / (poh / 24)
        print(f"[derived] host writes ~ {tb_day:.3f} TB per power-on day")
    if b[5] and poh:
        print(f"[derived] linear projection to 100% used: ~{poh * 100 / b[5] / 24 / 365:.1f} "
              "power-on years (a rough guide only)")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    data = open(sys.argv[1], "rb").read() if len(sys.argv) > 1 else synthetic()
    decode(data)

Worked example

Synthetic example for teaching, using the decoder's built-in log for a 1 TB Lab SSD.

Example 1 — How much has the host written?

Data Units Written = 98,304,000. Bytes ≈ 98,304,000 × 512,000 = 5.033 × 1013 ≈ 50.3 TB. Host Write Commands = 2.41 × 109 → average ≈ 50.3 TB ÷ 2.41 × 109 ≈ 20.9 kB (≈ 20.4 KiB) per write: a mixed workload of small random and larger sequential writes.

Example 2 — Is the drive on track for its warranty?

Suppose the drive is rated for 600 TB written (TBW) over 5 years. Power On Hours = 14,260 (≈ 594 days). Host writes per day ≈ 50.3 TB ÷ 594 ≈ 0.085 TB/day. Rated TBW per day = 600 ÷ (5 × 365) ≈ 0.33 TB/day. Using ~26% of the rated daily rate. Percentage Used = 7% after 1.63 years; a linear projection gives about 23 power-on years to reach 100%. The two views agree roughly: this drive is lightly used. Derived; Percentage Used is the vendor's estimate and need not be linear.

Example 3 — Write amplification

From the Endurance Group Information log (synthetic): Data Units Written = 98,304,000, Media Units Written = 235,900,000. WAF ≈ 235.9 ÷ 98.3 ≈ 2.4. A random-write-heavy workload on a fairly full drive commonly shows WAF above 2; sequential workloads approach 1. If the operator over-provisions (leaves part of the drive unallocated), WAF often falls. Implementation-dependent.

Example 4 — Temperature conversion

Composite Temperature bytes 1–2 = 37h 01h (little-endian) → 0x0137 = 311 K → 311 − 273 = 38 °C. WCTEMP from Identify Controller = 0x0161 = 353 K = 80 °C.

Common mistakes

  • Treating Data Units as 512-byte sectors (off by 1000×) or as 4 KiB blocks.
  • Reading Percentage Used as remaining life. It is life used, an estimate, and can exceed 100.
  • Blaming the SSD for Unsafe Shutdowns without checking shutdown sequencing and RTD3E.
  • Ignoring throttling counters when investigating performance drops.
  • Treating every error log entry as a device fault. Many are invalid commands sent by tools.
  • Resetting or updating firmware before collecting telemetry on a misbehaving drive.
  • Polling only for Critical Warning and missing trends (spare drop, media errors rising).
  • Comparing vendors' Percentage Used or composite temperatures as if they used one formula.
  • Forgetting the Asynchronous Event re-arm: not reading the log with RAE cleared, so later warnings never arrive.

Senior engineer insight

  • Deltas beat thresholds. The critical warning arrives late; changes in spare, media errors, and throttling counters arrive early.
  • SMART is evidence about the environment as much as the drive. Unsafe shutdowns and temperature history are audits of the platform.
  • Collect telemetry first, ask questions later. It is the only artifact a vendor can use to root-cause internal failures, and it is fragile.
  • Correlate across layers. A media error in SMART should match an Error Information log entry, an I/O error in the kernel log, and possibly a Persistent Event entry; inconsistencies point to tool or identity mistakes.
  • Validate counters like any other feature: units, rounding, persistence across power cycles (clean and unclean), behaviour across firmware updates, per-namespace scope.
  • Watch the 2.4 additions. Voltage monitoring will make "drive vs platform power" arguments evidence-based on devices that support it.

Lab

Lab 57 — Build a health report from SMART and its companion logs

Objective
Decode the SMART / Health log, compute derived figures correctly, and write a health report that separates device evidence from platform evidence.
Skills
Field decoding and units; derived metrics; correlating SMART with error, endurance and persistent event logs; telemetry discipline.
Safety
Safe lab for Steps 1–4 (reading logs only). Optional self-test and telemetry capture: Caution lab (no data change, but background activity).
Hardware required
Optional: any Linux system with an NVMe SSD.
Software required
Python 3.8+; nvme-cli; optionally smartmontools.
Input
smart_decode.py; your device's SMART log (raw binary) if available.
Expected output
A one-page health report: identity, current state, wear, workload, history, risks, and actions.

Background

A health report must state units and derivations explicitly, and must say which conclusions are the vendor's estimates.

Step 1 — Decode the synthetic log

Run smart_decode.py. Verify by hand the TB written, the average write size and the temperature conversion.

Step 2 — Decode a real log (optional)

Save your drive's log with nvme smart-log --raw-binary and decode it with the script; compare with nvme smart-log output field by field. Record the serial number and firmware revision.

Step 3 — Correlate

Read the Error Information log. For each entry, note SQID (0 = admin queue), status and LBA. Decide whether the entries indicate device problems or invalid commands from tools. Compare the count with SMART's Number of Error Information Log Entries.

Step 4 — Platform evidence

Compare Power Cycles and Unsafe Shutdowns with your system's boot history (last -x reboot shutdown or the journal). Read RTD3E from Identify Controller.

Observation

Note any counter that disagrees with platform history and any field your drive does not implement (zeros in sensors, unsupported endurance log).

Questions

  1. Why might Unsafe Shutdowns exceed the number of unexpected power losses you know about?
  2. How many bytes does a Data Units Read value of 1 represent? Of 1,000,000?
  3. Your drive shows Percentage Used = 103 and Available Spare = 100. Is it failing?
  4. Why is Media and Data Integrity Errors more serious than Number of Error Information Log Entries?

Challenge

Extend the decoder to compare two snapshots and print deltas per day for data units, commands, media errors, unsafe shutdowns and thermal counters, flagging any counter that decreased.

Hints

Use the Power On Hours delta for "per power-on day." A counter that decreased means a different drive, a firmware change in counting, or a decoding mistake.

Solution

Step 1: 98,304,000 × 512,000 B ≈ 50.33 TB; 50.33 × 1012 ÷ 2.41 × 109 ≈ 20,880 B ≈ 20.4 KiB; 311 K − 273 = 38 °C.

Q1: Clean-looking shutdowns where the platform removed power before the drive reported shutdown complete; hot-unplugs; BMC power actions; driver shutdown timeouts shorter than RTD3E.

Q2: 1 unit = 512,000 bytes (rounded up — any read from 1 byte to 512,000 bytes of 512-byte units moves it). 1,000,000 units = 512 GB.

Q3: Not necessarily. It has exceeded the vendor's rated endurance estimate, but spare is intact and no critical warning is set. Plan replacement per policy; watch spare and media errors closely.

Q4: Media errors are unrecovered integrity failures (data could not be returned correctly). Error log entries include harmless command errors such as invalid fields.

What a senior engineer notices

Whether the snapshot identity (serial, firmware, NSID) is recorded; whether unsafe shutdowns correlate with a platform event; whether throttling explains performance complaints; and whether telemetry was captured before anyone touched a failing drive.

Failure variants

  • A truncated raw file (< 512 bytes): the decoder stops — a reminder to check transfer length (NUMD).
  • Per-namespace request on a controller without LPA support: Invalid Field in Command.
  • Big-endian decoding mistake: temperatures like 0x3701 = 14081 K — obviously wrong; multi-byte fields are little-endian.

Exercises

  1. Compute CDW10 for a Get Log Page that reads only the first 64 bytes of the SMART log with RAE = 1.
  2. A drive reports Critical Warning = 0x0A. Decode it and state the actions.
  3. Data Units Written rose by 2,000,000 in 24 power-on hours. How many TB/day is that?
  4. A namespace uses 4 KiB LBAs. The host writes one 4 KiB block. By how many 512-byte units does the internal count rise, and when does Data Units Written increment?
  5. Why should Asynchronous Event Configuration enable the temperature warning on every production system?
Answers

1. 64 bytes = 16 dwords → NUMDL = 15 = 000Fh; RAE = bit 15 = 1; LID = 02h → CDW10 = 0x000F8002.

2. 0x0A = bits 1 and 3: temperature threshold condition and media read-only. Check cooling; copy data off — writes will fail; plan replacement.

3. 2,000,000 × 512,000 B = 1.024 × 1012 B ≈ 1.02 TB/day.

4. 8 units of 512 bytes. Data Units Written counts thousands of 512-byte units, rounded up, so the reported value increases each time another 1,000 units (512,000 bytes) accumulate — after about 125 such writes.

5. Overheating causes throttling (performance loss) and, at critical levels, protective shutdown; an event lets software act before damage or outage, instead of discovering it during a later poll.

Quiz

  1. What is the log identifier of the SMART / Health Information log?

    • 01h
    • 02h
    • 03h
    • 0Dh
    Answer and reasoning

    02h. 01h is Error Information, 03h Firmware Slot Information, 0Dh Persistent Event.

  2. One Data Unit equals:

    • 512 bytes
    • 4,096 bytes
    • 1000 × 512 bytes
    • 1 MiB
    Answer and reasoning

    Thousands of 512-byte units: 512,000 bytes, rounded up.

  3. In which unit is Composite Temperature reported?

    • Kelvin
    • Celsius
    • Fahrenheit
    • Vendor-specific
    Answer and reasoning

    Kelvin; tools convert to Celsius.

  4. Percentage Used = 120 means:

    • Invalid value; the drive is broken
    • The drive has exceeded the vendor's rated life estimate by 20%
    • 120% of spare remains
    Answer and reasoning

    Values above 100 are allowed (saturating at 255); the drive may still work.

  5. Which Critical Warning bit indicates the media is read-only?

    • Bit 0
    • Bit 1
    • Bit 2
    • Bit 3
    Answer and reasoning

    Bit 3. Bit 0 spare, bit 1 temperature, bit 2 reliability degraded.

  6. What increments Unsafe Shutdowns?

    Model answer

    Loss of power without a completed shutdown notification (the host did not write CC.SHN and wait for CSTS.SHST to report completion before power was removed).

  7. Which log gives Media Units Written for computing write amplification?

    • SMART / Health Information (02h)
    • Error Information (01h)
    • Endurance Group Information (09h)
    • Firmware Slot Information (03h)
    Answer and reasoning

    The Endurance Group Information log reports Media Units Written alongside Data Units Written.

  8. After a SMART/Health asynchronous event, what must the host do to receive future events of that type?

    Model answer

    Read the associated log page (02h) with Retain Asynchronous Event cleared, and submit a new Asynchronous Event Request to replace the one that completed.

  9. Why collect telemetry before resetting a misbehaving drive?

    Model answer

    Telemetry captures internal, often volatile, state for vendor analysis; resets, power cycles or firmware updates can discard it, removing the only evidence of the internal failure.

Interview questions

  1. Beginner: How do you read SMART data from an NVMe drive, and what command does it use?

    Model answer

    nvme smart-log /dev/nvme0 or smartctl -a; both issue the admin Get Log Page command (opcode 02h) for log identifier 02h, 512 bytes.

  2. Beginner: What is the difference between Available Spare and Percentage Used?

    Model answer

    Available Spare is the remaining replacement capacity for failed blocks (normalized percent); Percentage Used is the vendor's estimate of rated life consumed. Spare can stay 100 while Used climbs, or drop suddenly with failures.

  3. Intermediate: Convert Data Units Written to terabytes and explain the pitfalls.

    Model answer

    TB = DUW × 512,000 ÷ 1012. Pitfalls: units are thousands of 512-byte units regardless of LBA size; rounded up; host data only, not media writes; tools may print different conversions.

  4. Intermediate: How do asynchronous health events work?

    Model answer

    Host enables warnings with Asynchronous Event Configuration (FID 0Bh) and keeps Asynchronous Event Requests (0Ch) outstanding; the controller completes one with event type SMART/Health and the log page; host reads the log with RAE cleared to re-arm, and submits a new request.

  5. Advanced: A fleet shows Unsafe Shutdowns rising on one server model only. Investigate.

    Model answer

    Correlate increments with reboots/power events; compare the kernel's shutdown path and nvme driver shutdown timeout with RTD3E; check BMC power sequencing and slot power control; check Persistent Event logs for power-off without shutdown completion; test with longer shutdown wait. Likely a platform or driver configuration issue.

  6. Advanced: How would you estimate remaining life more robustly than reading Percentage Used?

    Model answer

    Trend Percentage Used and media writes over time, compute WAF from the Endurance Group log, compare host write rate with rated endurance, watch spare and media error deltas, and account for workload changes; still treat the result as an estimate.

  7. Senior: Design a fleet health-monitoring policy for NVMe drives.

    Model answer

    Event-driven plus periodic snapshots; store full snapshots with identity and firmware; alert on critical warnings immediately and on deltas (spare drop, media errors, thermal counters, unsafe shutdowns) with rates; automatic telemetry capture on degraded or read-only states before remediation; correlate with platform power and cooling telemetry; replacement policy combining spare, media errors and used; firmware-aware baselines; on 2.4-capable drives, include voltage monitoring alerts.

  8. Senior: What would you validate about SMART in a new SSD firmware release?

    Model answer

    Field units and rounding against known workloads; counter persistence across clean and unclean power cycles; monotonicity across firmware update; threshold behaviour and asynchronous events; per-namespace and endurance group consistency; error log and media error coupling; telemetry capture and generation numbers; thermal counters under controlled heating; no regressions in Get Log Page offsets and partial reads.

Chapter summary

The SMART / Health Information log (02h) is a mandatory 512-byte, little-endian structure read with Get Log Page. It reports Critical Warning bits (spare, temperature, reliability, read-only, volatile backup, PMR), composite and per-sensor temperatures in Kelvin with warning/critical time and thermal management counters, Available Spare with its threshold, the vendor-estimated Percentage Used (which can exceed 100), Data Units Read/Written in thousands of 512-byte units, host command counts, controller busy time, power cycles, power-on hours, unsafe shutdowns, media and data integrity errors, and the number of error log entries. The Error Information, Endurance Group, Persistent Event and Telemetry logs complete the picture; asynchronous events deliver warnings promptly. Read the numbers with their units, as trends, together with platform evidence, and collect telemetry before touching a failing drive.

Do not move on until you can:

  • build the Get Log Page command for the SMART log and predict its PCIe traffic
  • decode every SMART field with correct units, including temperatures and data units
  • decode Critical Warning bits and choose host actions
  • compute TB written, average I/O size and WAF (with the Endurance Group log)
  • explain why Unsafe Shutdowns and Percentage Used are commonly misread
  • configure and handle SMART/Health asynchronous events, including re-arming
  • write a health report that separates device evidence, platform evidence and vendor estimates
