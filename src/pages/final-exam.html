This page holds two things. The first is the nine-level mastery rubric (0–8) the course uses to judge engineering ability, with behaviours you can observe at each level. The second is a six-hour, twelve-part certification exam with a hidden answer key and a grading guide. Certification requires level 7 or higher: you can investigate ambiguous failures, not just recite how things work.

How the rubric and the exam fit together

Most exams measure what you remember. This one measures what you can do with what you remember: decode, correlate, reason under uncertainty, design, and communicate. The rubric describes those abilities as levels; the exam gives you tasks where each level produces a visibly different answer.

Learning objectives

  • Place an answer (yours or someone else's) on the 0–8 rubric using observable behaviours, not impressions.
  • Know what separates each level from the next, so you know what to practise.
  • Sit the six-hour exam under realistic conditions and grade it with the key and the guide.

Prerequisites

The whole course. In particular: Transaction Layer Packet (TLP) anatomy (Chapter 11), NVM Express (NVMe) command and completion structure (Chapter 29), queues (Chapter 25), PRPs (Chapter 36), errors and AER (Chapter 44–Chapter 47), performance (Performance Engineering), the 2026 updates (2026 NVMe 2.4 Updates), and practice with Interview Academy, Scenario Interviews and the Final Capstone.

Learning to ride a bike has levels too. First you can name the parts. Then you can explain how pedals turn the wheel. Then you can ride on a flat path. Then you can fix the chain when it falls off. Much later you can ride in traffic, in the rain, on a road you have never seen, and decide on the fly which way is safest.

Bike skills mostly grow on their own with practice. Engineering levels do not: many people stay at "can fix the chain" for years, because reaching "can handle a road never seen before" needs deliberate practice with ambiguous problems, written reasoning and feedback. That is why the exam's last part is an ambiguous incident.

The senior engineer rubric (levels 0–8)

Each level includes the abilities of the levels below it. A level is awarded only when its behaviour is observed in the answer, in writing or out loud. Intentions ("I would look at the trace") do not count unless the answer also says what the trace would show and why it matters.

One question, six answers

The fastest way to learn the rubric is to see the same question answered at different levels. Question: "The root port logs a Completion Timeout and then the NVMe driver resets the controller. What happened?"

Answers at different levels
LevelAnswer (condensed)Why it is at that level
0"A Completion Timeout is an error where a completion times out."Definition only, circular
2"A non-posted request did not get a completion in time. The solid-state drive (SSD) probably did not answer, so the driver reset it."Explains the mechanism; assumes the cause
4"The root port is the requester, so the central processing unit (CPU) sent a non-posted request (likely a memory-mapped I/O (MMIO) read) that was not answered. I would check the analyzer for the read and whether the SSD ACKed it."Correct detector reasoning and a specific evidence request
5"…and I would line up the kernel log: did NVMe I/O time out before the CTO? If so, the read may have come from the driver's recovery path, and the CTO is a consequence. Also check for receiver errors or link events before it."Cross-layer ordering
7"Three main hypotheses: SSD did not answer (firmware stall), request or completion lost on the link, or the read was issued at a moment the SSD is allowed to be slow (reset, activation). I'd also check for an Unexpected Completion later, which would mean the SSD answered late. Confidence depends on that capture; I'd report it as medium until then."Ranked hypotheses, discriminating evidence, a distractor named, confidence

How to assess

  1. Score the evidence in the answer, not the person. Quote the sentence that shows the behaviour.
  2. Find the highest level fully shown, then check that the lower levels are not contradicted (a level-7 answer containing a factual error that changes the conclusion drops to the level of the error's consequence).
  3. Use two raters for certification. If they differ by more than one level, they discuss with quotes; if they still differ, a third rater decides.
  4. Record what was missing to reach the next level. That note is the most useful feedback the candidate gets.
  • Confidence is not level. A fluent, confident wrong answer is level 0–2.
  • Vocabulary is not level. "Equalization, FLIT, four-level Pulse Amplitude Modulation (PAM4)" in an answer that does not use them correctly earns nothing.
  • Saying "I would check the specification" is good when it marks a real uncertainty; it does not replace knowing the well-established basics.

The certification rule

The final certification requires level 7 or higher. In exam terms (details in the grading guide):

  • total score at least 225 of 300 (75%), and
  • Part L (senior incident investigation) assessed at level 7 or higher by two raters, and
  • no part below 50% of its points.

Part A — PCIe architecture (20 points)

  1. (3 points) Which statement about the PCIe layers is correct?

    • The Data Link Layer creates TLP headers and the Transaction Layer adds the LCRC.
    • Flow control credits are managed by the Physical Layer.
    • The Transaction Layer creates TLPs; the Data Link Layer adds sequence numbers and LCRC and handles ACK/NAK; the Physical Layer handles encoding, framing and link training.
    • DLLPs carry memory read data between devices.
    Answer key

    Option 3. Flow control credits are exchanged with DLLPs, but credits account for Transaction Layer buffer space. DLLPs never carry memory data. Learn it in Chapter 7.

  2. (4 points) Explain why a root port and an endpoint must agree on Max Payload Size, and what happens if the endpoint sends a TLP whose payload exceeds the root port's setting.

    Answer key

    MPS bounds receive buffers and is checked by the receiver. A TLP with a payload larger than the receiver's MPS is a Malformed TLP, an uncorrectable error that is fatal by default, typically leading to error recovery and link reset. Software sets MPS consistently along a path (no larger than the smallest supported along it). 2 points for the receiver check and Malformed TLP; 1 for severity; 1 for software's role. Chapter 18, Chapter 45.

  3. (4 points) Name the Link Training and Status State Machine (LTSSM) states a link passes through from reset to normal operation at Gen4, and explain where equalization happens.

    Answer key

    Detect → Polling → Configuration → L0 at 2.5 GT/s; then a speed change through Recovery, with Recovery.Equalization phases for 8.0 GT/s and again for 16.0 GT/s, returning to L0. 2 points for the base path, 2 for equalization in Recovery per speed. Chapter 8, Chapter 54.

  4. (4 points) What is the difference between posted and non-posted requests, and why does that difference matter for error detection?

    Answer key

    Posted requests (memory writes, messages) receive no completion; non-posted (memory reads, configuration and I/O requests) require one. Consequently, Completion Timeout can only occur for non-posted requests and is detected by the requester; failures of posted writes are detected, if at all, by the receiver (for example Unsupported Request logged at the completer side, or an IOMMU fault), and the sender never learns about them through a completion. Chapter 12.

  5. (5 points) A Gen5 x4 link reports Link Status of 16 GT/s x4. List three distinct causes and the evidence that distinguishes them.

    Answer key

    (1) One end supports only 16 GT/s (check Link Capabilities / Link Capabilities 2 on both ends). (2) Target Link Speed in Link Control 2 limited to 16 GT/s by software or platform policy (check Link Control 2). (3) 32 GT/s equalization failed and the link fell back (check equalization status bits in the 32 GT/s capability, LTSSM history, and whether the problem follows the slot or riser). Also acceptable: retimer limited, platform setting. 1 point per cause, up to 3, plus 2 for correct discriminating evidence. Chapter 9, Chapter 69.

Part B — NVMe architecture (20 points)

  1. (3 points) Which register does the host write to tell the controller where the Admin Submission Queue is?

    • AQA
    • ASQ
    • CC
    • CAP
    Answer key

    ASQ (offset 28h) holds the base address; AQA (24h) holds the sizes; ACQ (30h) the Admin CQ base. Chapter 24.

  2. (4 points) Describe the controller initialisation sequence from the driver's point of view, up to the first I/O command.

    Answer key

    Read CAP (and VS); ensure CC.EN = 0 and CSTS.RDY = 0; allocate admin queues and program AQA, ASQ, ACQ; set CC (memory page size, command set, entry sizes) and CC.EN = 1; wait for CSTS.RDY = 1 within CAP.TO, checking CSTS.CFS; Identify Controller (and namespaces); Set Features Number of Queues; set up interrupts (MSI-X); Create I/O CQs, then Create I/O SQs (each referencing its CQ); optional features; Asynchronous Event Requests; then I/O. Deduct 1 point if SQs are created before their CQs. Chapter 26, Chapter 39.

  3. (4 points) Explain the relationship between an NVM subsystem, a controller and a namespace, and give a configuration where one namespace is reachable through two controllers.

    Answer key

    An NVM subsystem contains one or more controllers and namespaces (and the media). A controller is the interface the host talks to (registers, queues); a namespace is addressable storage attached to zero or more controllers. A dual-port SSD with one controller per port, both attached to a shared namespace, lets two hosts or two paths reach it (multipath). Chapter 22, Module 22.

  4. (4 points) Why does NVMe put queues in host memory rather than in controller memory, and what optional mechanism allows queues in controller memory?

    Answer key

    Host memory is plentiful and cheap; the host writes commands with ordinary stores and reads completions without crossing the link; the controller fetches commands efficiently with DMA. The Controller Memory Buffer (CMB) is an optional feature that lets SQs (and other structures) live in controller memory, saving the Submission Queue Entry (SQE) fetch round trip at the cost of MMIO writes across the link. Chapter 25, Module 21.

  5. (5 points) The specification set now has a Base specification, several command set specifications and several transport specifications. Explain why it was split and how that split helps an engineer debugging an NVMe over PCIe problem.

    Answer key

    Since NVMe 2.0 the material is split so that common architecture (Base), each command set (NVM, ZNS, KV, and others) and each transport (PCIe, RDMA, TCP) can evolve independently. When debugging NVMe over PCIe, you read the Base specification for queues, admin commands and status; the NVM Command Set for Read/Write semantics; the PCIe Transport specification for how registers, doorbells and interrupts map onto PCIe; and the PCI Express Base Specification for the link itself. Knowing which document owns a behaviour prevents reading the wrong one and helps cite correctly. Chapter 22, Specification Reading School.

Part C — TLP decoding (25 points)

Decode each header. Give type, length in bytes, requester or completer, tag, address or routing, and what the TLP means on the reference system. Header DWs are given in transmission order, as 32-bit values.

  1. (3 points) 0x4000_0001 0x0000_000F 0xFE60_1008, payload 0x0000_0005

    Answer key

    Fmt 010, Type 00000: MWr, 3DW header (32-bit address). Length 1 DW = 4 bytes. Requester 0x0000 (root complex), tag 0x00 (not meaningful for posted), First DW BE 1111, Last DW BE 0000. Address 0xFE60_1008 = BAR0 + 0x1008 = SQ1 tail doorbell; value 5. Chapter 11.

  2. (4 points) 0x2000_0010 0x0100_21FF 0x0000_0001 0x2340_0100

    Answer key

    Fmt 001, Type 00000: MRd, 4DW header (64-bit address). Length 0x010 = 16 DW = 64 bytes. Requester 0x0100 (the SSD), tag 0x21, BE 1111/1111. Address 0x1_2340_0100: SQ1 base + 0x100 = slot 4 (0x100 ÷ 64). Meaning: the controller fetches the SQE in SQ1 slot 4. 1 point for noting the 64-bit format is required because the address is above 4 GB (addresses below 4 GB must use the 32-bit format).

  3. (4 points) 0x4A00_0010 0x0000_0040 0x0100_2100, 64 bytes of payload

    Answer key

    Fmt 010, Type 01010: CplD. Length 16 DW = 64 bytes. Completer ID 0x0000, Status 000 (Successful Completion), BCM 0, Byte Count 0x040 = 64. Requester ID 0x0100, Tag 0x21, Lower Address 0x00. It completes the request in question 2 in one piece. Chapter 52.

  4. (5 points) 0x0A00_0000 0x0100_2004 0x0000_051C. Decode it and give two possible reasons the device returned it.

    Answer key

    Fmt 000, Type 01010: Cpl (no data). Completer 0x0100 (the SSD), Status 001 = Unsupported Request, Byte Count 4. Requester 0x0000 (root complex), Tag 0x05, Lower Address 0x1C: a 4-byte read whose address ends in 0x1C, consistent with a read of CSTS (BAR0 + 0x1C). Reasons: the function is in D3hot (memory requests are not serviced there), or Memory Space Enable is clear (for example after a reset before configuration was restored). Either way, the CPU sees all ones. 3 points for decode, 2 for reasons. Chapter 45, Chapter 50.

  5. (4 points) 0x0400_0001 0x0000_010F 0x0100_0010

    Answer key

    Fmt 000, Type 00100: Configuration Read Type 0. Length 1 DW. Requester 0x0000, Tag 0x01, First BE 1111. Bus 0x01, Device 0, Function 0; Extended Register 0, Register Number 4 → byte offset 0x10: BAR0. Meaning: enumeration software reads BAR0 of 01:00.0 (for example during sizing). Chapter 17.

  6. (5 points) The SSD sends 0x6000_0080 … to the root port. What is it, and what happens on the reference system?

    Answer key

    Fmt 011, Type 00000: MWr with a 4DW header. Length 0x080 = 128 DW = 512 bytes. With MPS 256 on the reference system, the root port must treat it as a Malformed TLP (payload exceeds its MPS): uncorrectable, fatal by default, logged with this header in the root port's AER Header Log, followed by error recovery. A correct SSD would have split the data into two 256-byte writes. The likely cause is an MPS mismatch (SSD programmed with 512) or a device bug. Chapter 18, Chapter 46.

Part D — NVMe command decoding (25 points)

SQE dwords are given as 32-bit values; 64-bit fields are written whole. Completion Queue Entry (CQE) Dword 3 is given as one value.

  1. (5 points) An I/O SQE: CDW0 = 0x002A_0002, Namespace Identifier (NSID) = 0x0000_0001, PRP1 = 0x0000_0002_0000_3000, PRP2 = 0x0000_0001_5000_0000, CDW10 = 0x0010_0000, CDW11 = 0x0000_0000, CDW12 = 0x0000_001F, namespace with 512-byte LBAs. Decode it and describe the data pointer.

    Answer key

    Opcode 02h = Read; FUSE 0 (normal); PSDT 0 (PRPs); Command Identifier (CID) 0x002A. NSID 1. SLBA = 0x0010_0000. NLB field 0x1F is zero-based: 32 blocks × 512 B = 16 KiB. PRP1 has offset 0, so the transfer covers four 4 KiB pages; PRP2 therefore points to a PRP list (at 0x1_5000_0000) holding three entries for pages 2–4. 1 point each: opcode, CID, SLBA, length (zero-based), PRP2 role. Chapter 32, Chapter 36.

  2. (4 points) CDW0 = 0x002B_0001, CDW12 = 0x4000_000F, PRP1 = 0x0000_0002_0000_8000, PRP2 = 0x0000_0002_0000_9000, 512-byte LBAs. Decode and explain PRP2.

    Answer key

    Opcode 01h = Write, CID 0x002B. CDW12: bit 30 set = Force Unit Access; NLB 0x0F → 16 blocks = 8 KiB. PRP1 offset 0, so exactly two pages: PRP2 is a direct pointer to the second data page, not a list. FUA means the data must be on non-volatile media before completion. Chapter 33, Chapter 34.

  3. (4 points) A CQE has DW2 = 0x0001_0005 and DW3 = 0x0005_002A. Decode it.

    Answer key

    DW2: SQ Identifier 1, SQ Head Pointer 5. DW3: CID 0x002A, Phase Tag 1, Status Field 0x0002 → Status Code Type 0 (generic), Status Code 02h = Invalid Field in Command; More 0; Do Not Retry 0. The Read in question 1 was rejected; the host should check which field (the Error Information log may say), and may retry only after fixing the command. Chapter 44, NVMe Command Decoder.

  4. (4 points) Same command, but DW3 = 0x8005_002A. What changed and what should the host do?

    Answer key

    Bit 31 of DW3 is the Do Not Retry bit (Status Field bit 14): set. Same status (Invalid Field in Command), but the controller says retrying the same command is expected to fail again. The host should fail the I/O upward (or fix the command), not loop. Chapter 44.

  5. (4 points) DW3 = 0x0503_002A. Decode the status and name the Linux-level consequence you expect.

    Answer key

    DW3 bits 31:16 = 0x0503: Phase Tag 1; Status Field = 0x0281 → SCT 2h (Media and Data Integrity Errors), SC 81h = Unrecovered Read Error; DNR 0, More 0. Linux reports an I/O error on the read (the block layer logs a critical medium error for the sector range); the Self-Monitoring, Analysis and Reporting Technology (SMART) media error count and the Error Information log should record it. If DNR is clear, the driver may retry. Confirm status values in the NVM Express Base Specification's status tables when citing them. Chapter 44, Chapter 57.

  6. (4 points) Admin SQE: CDW0 opcode 01h, CDW10 = 0x03FF_0001, CDW11 = 0x0001_0001, PRP1 = 0x0000_0001_2340_0000. Decode.

    Answer key

    Admin opcode 01h = Create I/O Submission Queue. CDW10: Queue Size 0x03FF (zero-based: 1,024 entries), Queue Identifier 1. CDW11: Completion Queue Identifier 1, priority 0, Physically Contiguous 1. PRP1: queue base 0x1_2340_0000 (64 KiB for 1,024 × 64 B). The controller will reject it if CQ 1 does not exist, if the size exceeds CAP.MQES + 1, or if the identifier exceeds what Number of Queues granted. Chapter 27, Chapter 30.

Part E — Queue analysis (20 points)

  1. (5 points) An I/O SQ has 8 slots. The controller's head is 6; the host's tail is 2. How many commands are waiting, which slots hold them, and how many more can the host add before the queue is full?

    Answer key

    Slots 6, 7, 0, 1: four commands. Full when (tail + 1) mod 8 = head; the host can add slots 2, 3, 4 (three more) and then must stop with tail = 5, because advancing to 6 would equal the head. Chapter 25.

  2. (5 points) A CQ has 4 slots. The host has consumed slots 0–3 on its first pass (all had Phase Tag 1) and its head is now 0 again. Slot 0 currently holds an entry with Phase Tag 1. Is it a new completion? What about when slot 0 holds Phase Tag 0?

    Answer key

    After the wrap the host expects phase 0. An entry with phase 1 in slot 0 is the stale entry from the first pass: not new. An entry with phase 0 is new. The host flips its expected phase each time its head wraps. Chapter 27.

  3. (5 points) An analyzer shows these SQ1 tail doorbell values in order: 5, 7, 6. What is wrong, who is at fault, and what may the controller do?

    Answer key

    The tail moved backwards (7 to 6), which is only valid if the queue wrapped all the way round; with a 1,024-entry queue and no wrap, it is an invalid doorbell value. The host is at fault (a race between submitting threads, or a lost update). The controller may treat it as an error: the NVMe specification describes an asynchronous error event for invalid doorbell writes, and controller behaviour after that is specified there; confirm the details in the Base specification. Evidence to collect: driver locking around the SQ tail. Chapter 28, Module 27.

  4. (5 points) Two SQs share one CQ of 16 entries. Each SQ has 12 commands outstanding. What can go wrong and what should have been done when the queues were created?

    Answer key

    Up to 24 completions may be pending but the CQ can hold 15 unconsumed entries (one slot is kept empty). If the host is slow to consume, the controller cannot post and stalls further completions; commands appear to hang. The CQ should have been sized for the sum of possible outstanding commands on all SQs that use it, or the host must limit outstanding commands accordingly. Chapter 27.

Part F — DMA analysis (20 points)

  1. (5 points) A 64 KiB Read into a 4 KiB-aligned buffer. How many device-to-host TLPs do you expect for the whole command, by type?

    Answer key

    Data: 65,536 ÷ 256 = 256 MWr TLPs. Plus 1 MWr for the CQE and 1 MWr for Message Signalled Interrupts Extended (MSI-X): 258 MWr. Plus device-to-host MRds: 1 for the SQE and 1 for the PRP list (16 pages need a list), so 2 MRd. 1 point is lost if the PRP list fetch is forgotten. Chapter 41.

  2. (5 points) A 64 KiB Write from the same kind of buffer. How many MRd TLPs and at least how many CplD TLPs carry the data? Why "at least"?

    Answer key

    65,536 ÷ 512 (MRRS) = 128 MRds. Each completion carries at most 256 bytes (MPS), so at least 256 CplDs. The completer may split further at RCB (64-byte) boundaries, up to 1,024 completions; the number is implementation-dependent. Chapter 52.

  3. (5 points) A 512-byte Read has PRP1 = 0x2_0000_0F80. List the data TLPs the device sends.

    Answer key

    The buffer runs from 0x2_0000_0F80 to 0x2_0000_117F, crossing a 4 KiB page boundary at 0x2_0000_1000, so PRP2 gives the second page. A request must not cross a 4 KiB address boundary, and payloads are limited to 256 B: MWr 128 B at 0x…0F80, then MWr 256 B at 0x…1000 (second page, from PRP2), then MWr 128 B at 0x…1100: three TLPs (an implementation may split differently within these rules, but never across the 4 KiB boundary or above MPS). Chapter 36, Chapter 11.

  4. (5 points) Bus Master Enable is cleared on the SSD by mistake while I/O is running. What happens, what does each layer show, and how do you find who cleared it?

    Answer key

    The function may no longer issue memory requests: it cannot fetch SQEs, read or write data, post CQEs or send MSI-X. Outstanding commands stop; the host sees I/O timeouts; Abort cannot complete (the controller cannot post the Abort's CQE either); the driver resets and, if Bus Master Enable stays clear, fails. PCIe shows no errors (the device simply stops issuing requests). lspci shows BusMaster−. To find who cleared it, trace configuration writes to the Command register (analyzer) or audit software that touches configuration space. Controller behaviour while it cannot master is implementation-dependent. Chapter 18, Chapter 20.

Part G — Trace reconstruction (30 points)

(20 points) The rows below belong to one 8 KiB Write on SQ2 of the reference system (CQ2 uses MSI-X vector 2 at address 0xFEE0_2000), but they were exported out of order and one row is missing. Put them in order, name the missing row, and explain each step in one line.

LabelDirTypeFields
R1Host→DevCplD ×4Tags 0x31, 0x32, 256 B each
R2Dev→HostMWr64Addr=CQ2 slot 7, 16 B, CID 0x0077, P=1, status 0
R3Host→DevMWr32Addr=0xFE60_1010 Data=0x0000_0008
R4Dev→HostMRd64Addr=SQ2 base + 0x1C0, 64 B, Tag 0x30
R5Dev→HostMRd64 ×16512 B each, Tags 0x31–0x40
R6Host→DevCplDTag 0x30, 64 B
R7Dev→HostMWr32Addr=0xFEE0_2000
Answer key
  1. R3: host writes SQ2 tail doorbell = 8 (slot 7 filled).
  2. R4: controller fetches the SQE in slot 7 (0x1C0 ÷ 64 = 7).
  3. R6: root complex returns the 64-byte SQE.
  4. R5 interleaved with R1 and the rest of the completions: the controller reads 8 KiB of write data. Inconsistency to flag: 8 KiB at MRRS 512 needs 16 MRds — consistent — but R1 shows only four 256-byte completions (1 KiB) for two tags. The full set should be at least 32 CplDs (two per tag). R1 is an excerpt; say so, or ask for the full export.
  5. R2: CQE posted in CQ2 slot 7.
  6. R7: MSI-X for vector 2.
  7. Missing: the host's CQ2 head doorbell write (MWr32 to 0xFE60_1014 with value 8) after it processes the completion.

Marking: order (10), missing row (4), explanation lines (3), spotting that R1 cannot be the complete set of completions (3). A candidate who silently accepts R1 as complete loses those 3 points: noticing incomplete evidence is a senior behaviour. Chapter 40, Chapter 41.

(10 points) In a second capture of the same command, R7 appears before R2. Is that possible in a correct system? What would you suspect, and what field in the TLP headers would you check?

Answer key

On one link, a posted write may not pass an earlier posted write unless Relaxed Ordering (or ID-based ordering) permits it. If the interrupt appears before the CQE on the link, the device sent it first (a device ordering bug), or the CQE write was sent with Relaxed Ordering and something reordered it, or the capture's export ordering is wrong (check timestamps and sequence numbers). Check the Attr bits (Relaxed Ordering, ID-Based Ordering) in both TLP headers and the Data Link Layer sequence numbers. Consequence in a real system: the interrupt handler may find no new CQE and the completion waits for the next interrupt or a timeout. Chapter 14, Chapter 51.

Part H — AER debugging (25 points)

For each case, say what the evidence means, which layer the first abnormal event is likely in, and the next piece of evidence you would collect.

  1. (5 points) Root port: Correctable Error Status shows Receiver Error and Bad TLP, counts rising under load. SSD: all AER status clear.

    Answer key

    The root port's receiver is seeing errors: traffic from the SSD to the root port (upstream direction) is degraded. Physical layer, with Data Link Layer replay recovering. Next: link speed and width, LTSSM Recovery count, lane-level errors on an analyzer, temperature correlation, and swap tests (slot, riser, drive). Chapter 15, Chapter 54.

  2. (5 points) SSD: Uncorrectable Error Status shows Completion Timeout. Root port: clear. NVMe Writes time out; Reads work.

    Answer key

    The SSD's own non-posted requests (reads of write data) are not being answered. Transaction layer, but the cause is on the completer side or the path: where are the write buffers? (Peer-to-peer targets, unusual memory regions, platform limitations.) Next: addresses of the SSD's MRds in an analyzer trace and the platform's routing of those addresses. See Scenario 6.

  3. (5 points) Root port: Uncorrectable Error Status shows Unsupported Request; Header Log DW0 = 0x2000_0002, Requester ID 0x0100, address 0x0000_0001_5000_0000. Kernel log shows an IOMMU fault for the same address.

    Answer key

    An MRd64 of 2 DW (8 bytes) from the SSD to 0x1_5000_0000, refused by the root complex because the IOMMU has no mapping for that I/O virtual address (IOVA). The SSD read a PRP list or other host structure the host never mapped (or already unmapped). Host-side DMA mapping bug, or a stale pointer in a command. Next: which command referenced that address (search SQEs and PRP lists), and the host's mapping lifetime for it. Chapter 20, Module 02.

  4. (5 points) What does the First Error Pointer in the AER capability tell you, and why does it matter when several uncorrectable status bits are set?

    Answer key

    It identifies which uncorrectable error status bit was set first (the error whose header is in the Header Log, when logged). When several bits are set, it tells you which error happened first, the most important fact for finding the first abnormal event, and which error the header log belongs to. Chapter 46, Chapter 55.

  5. (5 points) A platform programs Completion Timeout as fatal in the root port's severity register. What changes in behaviour, and what is the risk?

    Answer key

    A Completion Timeout now causes a fatal error message and typically link-level recovery (link reset or containment) rather than a contained non-fatal error for one transaction. Benefit: a stuck device is reset quickly. Risk: every transient timeout (for example a device slow during firmware activation) takes the whole link down, affecting all outstanding I/O and destroying evidence. The choice should be deliberate and documented. Chapter 46, Chapter 55.

Part I — Performance debugging (25 points)

  1. (5 points) A drive delivers 400,000 I/O operations per second (IOPS) of 4 KiB random reads at queue depth 64 (total). What is the average latency? If you raise queue depth to 256 and IOPS stays at 400,000, what happens to latency?

    Answer key

    Little's Law: W = L ÷ λ = 64 ÷ 400,000 = 160 µs. At L = 256 with the same λ: W = 640 µs. The drive is saturated; extra depth only adds queueing delay. Mathematics for Storage and Links.

  2. (5 points) Estimate the maximum sequential read throughput on the reference Gen4 x4 link with 256-byte payloads, showing your overhead assumptions.

    Answer key

    Raw: 16 GT/s × 4 × 128/130 ÷ 8 ≈ 7.88 GB/s per direction. Per 256-byte MWr with a 4DW header: roughly 256 B payload + 16 B header + about 4 B framing/sequence + 4 B Link CRC (LCRC) ≈ 280 B, so about 91% efficiency: ≈ 7.2 GB/s, minus a few percent for DLLPs (ACKs, UpdateFC): about 7 GB/s. Accept answers in the 6.8–7.3 GB/s range with stated assumptions; credit is for the method and labelling the result as approximate. Calculators.

  3. (5 points) A segment of a capture for one 4 KiB Read: doorbell → SQE MRd 0.8 µs; SQE CplD +0.5 µs; first data MWr +78 µs; last data MWr +1.6 µs; CQE +0.3 µs; MSI-X +0.2 µs; application sees completion 14 µs after MSI-X. Where is the time, and what would you look at to cut it?

    Answer key

    Total ≈ 95 µs. Device-internal time (SQE received → first data) ≈ 78 µs dominates: media read. Host side after interrupt: 14 µs is high (typical interrupt-to-application paths are a few µs): look at interrupt affinity, CPU power states (wake latency), context switches, and the I/O engine. Link and transfer time are small. Module 53, Performance Engineering.

  4. (5 points) Throughput for 128 KiB sequential writes is 30% lower than for reads on the same drive and link. Give two link-level and two device-level explanations, and the evidence for each.

    Answer key

    Link: (1) writes depend on device reads: limited outstanding tags × round-trip latency (check outstanding MRds in a capture versus tag limit); (2) MRRS small, so more requests and completion overhead (check Device Control). Device: (3) media write bandwidth lower than read bandwidth (normal for NAND; check the drive's specification); (4) write cache full / garbage collection (check over time, after preconditioning). Chapter 13, Module 31.

  5. (5 points) Enabling interrupt coalescing halves CPU use but raises P99 latency at queue depth 1 from 90 µs to 190 µs. Explain and recommend.

    Answer key

    Coalescing waits for either an aggregation threshold or an aggregation time before interrupting. At queue depth 1 the threshold is never reached, so every completion waits for the timer (about 100 µs here). Recommend: coalescing only for throughput-oriented queues or workloads with deep queues; keep latency-sensitive queues uncoalesced (the feature may allow per-vector disabling, check the controller's support), or tune the time to the latency budget. Chapter 42.

Part J — Architecture design (30 points)

Answer one of the two questions. Write a structured design of about one to two pages: requirements, architecture (a diagram is welcome), key decisions with alternatives, failure modes, validation plan, and open questions.

  1. J1. Design a validation suite, run on every SSD firmware release, that would catch the class of bug in the Final Capstone: interrupts or completions lost around power-state and link-state transitions.

    Grading guide and model outline

    Model outline. Requirements: cover every NVMe power state allowed by the Autonomous Power State Transitions (APST) table, every Active State Power Management (ASPM) state (L0s, L1, L1.1, L1.2) the platform can enable, and idle durations spanning every internal timer (log-spaced from 1 ms to 10 min, plus fine sweeps around known thresholds); probe I/O at queue depth 1 after each idle (the worst case for a lost interrupt); also probes on admin and every I/O queue. Instrumentation: host-side per-vector interrupt counts versus completions, "completion polled" detection, per-I/O latency; analyzer check "every CQE followed by its MSI-X within X µs" on a subset of runs. Platforms: at least two root port vendors, with and without L1 substates. Pass criteria: zero lost interrupts, zero timeouts, latency after idle within the advertised exit latency plus a margin. Cost control: overnight runs with randomised idle schedules; shorter smoke version per build. Failure modes of the suite itself: analyzer perturbing L1.2 timing (run some without interposer), host timers too coarse.

    Marking (30): requirements tied to the mechanism (6); coverage of power and link states and idle durations (6); instrumentation that distinguishes lost interrupt from lost completion (6); pass criteria and tolerances (4); platform diversity and the suite's own failure modes (4); cost and cadence tradeoff (4). Level 6 answers cover the mechanism; level 7–8 answers add instrumentation limits, platform diversity and how results feed fleet monitoring.

  2. J2. Design the storage architecture for a cloud service that gives each of up to 16 tenants a slice of one NVMe SSD with performance isolation and per-tenant observability. Use NVMe and PCIe features where they help.

    Grading guide and model outline

    Model outline. Single Root I/O Virtualization (SR-IOV) with one VF (secondary controller) per tenant, assigned to the tenant's virtual machine (VM); namespaces per tenant attached only to that controller; queue and interrupt resources allocated per secondary controller through virtualization management; performance isolation with controller-enforced limits (NVMe 2.4 rate limiting, after checking what it can express), plus endurance or NVM-set separation where available to limit garbage-collection interference; host-side admission control as a backstop. Observability: per-controller statistics, latency histograms from the host side, SMART per subsystem, AER per function; isolation tests with adversarial tenants. Failure containment: Function Level Reset (FLR) per Virtual Function (VF) must not affect others (validate it); PF-level errors affect all tenants, so define the blast radius and the recovery runbook. Security: namespace attachment as the boundary; lockdown controls for virtualized environments (2.4) to stop tenants changing settings. Alternatives: software partitioning on one controller (less isolation), NVMe over Fabrics (NVMe-oF) disaggregation (more flexibility, more latency).

    Marking (30): isolation of control, capacity and performance (8); correct use of SR-IOV and NVMe virtualization concepts (6); observability and validation (6); failure containment and blast radius (5); honest treatment of 2.4 features without invented details (5).

Part K — 2026 NVMe feature analysis (20 points)

Answer from the NVMe 2.4 baseline (Base Specification 2.4, released August 2026). Do not invent opcodes, log identifiers or field layouts; say where you would confirm details.

  1. (4 points) Where in the architecture does the post-quantum cryptography update sit, and does it change what you see in a PCIe trace of Read and Write commands?

    Answer key

    In the security and authentication framework (asymmetric key exchange used for authentication and secure channels; preparing for algorithms such as those in NIST FIPS 203/204/205). It does not change Read/Write commands or their PCIe transactions. It may change the size and timing of security protocol exchanges at authentication time. Details: Base 2.4 and the Revision Changes document. 2026 NVMe 2.4 Updates.

  2. (4 points) List the reported steps of PCIe Exported NVM Subsystem migration and name the step you consider riskiest, with a reason.

    Answer key

    Create the Exported NVM Subsystem on the destination; recreate exported controller and namespaces with matching identifiers; apply the same configuration template (128-bit universally unique identifier (UUID)); capture source runtime state; transfer and apply it; migrate the VM; clean up the source. Riskiest (any well-argued choice): state capture and transfer, because in-flight commands and asynchronous state must be neither lost nor duplicated; or identifier recreation, because a mismatch breaks the guest's view. 2 points for the steps, 2 for the argument. Module 60.

  3. (4 points) How would you verify that controller-enforced rate limits actually isolate two controllers?

    Answer key

    Configure caps; saturate controller A with an adversarial workload (small random writes, then large sequential); measure controller B's achieved bandwidth, IOPS and tail latency against its configured guarantee, with stated tolerances; vary I/O size and mix; change limits at runtime; repeat across resets and power transitions; measure independently (analyzer throughput per Requester ID), not only from host tools. Validation Engineering.

  4. (4 points) A fleet dashboard shows voltage-monitoring alerts on 3% of drives in one rack row. What do you check before concluding the drives are faulty?

    Answer key

    Whether alerts correlate with the rack row's power distribution rather than drive model or firmware; whether they coincide with load steps; platform-side voltage measurements; threshold settings and measurement accuracy (implementation-dependent); correlation with resets, link errors or media errors; and whether other devices on the same rails show anomalies. The voltage monitoring capability reports what the device sees; the cause may be upstream. 2026 NVMe 2.4 Updates.

  5. (4 points) Restore manufacturing defaults and persistent lockdown are both 2.4 topics. How could they interact, and what would you require before allowing manufacturing restore in production?

    Answer key

    Lockdown can prevent certain commands or settings changes and can now persist across reboots, with separate controls for virtualized environments; a manufacturing restore could undo settings that lockdown was protecting, or be blocked by lockdown. Requirements: clear authorisation (only from a privileged management path), interaction with lockdown defined and tested, explicit sanitization policy (do not assume restore sanitizes data), logging (for example in the Persistent Event log if supported) and fleet tooling guards. Confirm exact semantics in Base 2.4. 2026 NVMe 2.4 Updates.

  1. (6 points) L1. Identify the first abnormal event and explain the completions in rows 3–6.

    Answer key

    The SSD issued two outstanding reads with 10-bit tags 0x041 and 0x141, which differ only in the upper two bits. Four completions came back, all carrying tag 0x041: two for the first request (512 then 256 remaining) and two that should have carried 0x141 (the second request). The first abnormal event is row 5: a completion with tag 0x041 and Byte Count 512 after the 0x041 request already had a completion stream in progress, i.e. the completer returned the second request's completion with the upper tag bits dropped. Equally acceptable: row 2 itself is the configuration-level abnormality (a 10-bit tag sent to a completer that does not support 10-bit tags), with row 5 the first observable symptom on the wire. Credit for either with justification.

  2. (6 points) L2. Rank at least three hypotheses, with evidence for and against each.

    Answer key

    (1) 10-bit tag misconfiguration: the SSD's 10-bit Tag Requester Enable is set although the root port does not support 10-bit tag completion; the completer truncates tags → completions mismatched. For: DevCap2 difference, trace tags, only at high outstanding counts (tags above 0xFF only used when many reads are outstanding), only platform N. Against: none significant. (2) SSD firmware tag bug: against: the same SSD and firmware are clean on platform O; the SSD's requests carry distinct tags. (3) Signal integrity: against: no errors on this link, data corruption is a clean 512-byte block from another buffer, not bit errors. (4) Kernel/I/O scheduler change: against: it changes timing but cannot rewrite tags; could change the rate. Ranked 1 ≫ 2 > 4 > 3.

  3. (6 points) L3. Design two experiments that discriminate between your top two hypotheses.

    Answer key

    (a) On platform N, clear 10-bit Tag Requester Enable on the SSD (an expert-level configuration change on a lab system) and rerun: prediction under hypothesis 1, no mismatches and all tags ≤ 0xFF; under hypothesis 2, mismatches continue. (b) Limit outstanding reads so tags never exceed 0xFF (lower queue depth or MRRS changes): prediction under 1, mismatches disappear; this also explains the queue-depth dependence. Also useful: run platform O with 10-bit tags enabled as a control. Each experiment must state a prediction that could fail.

  4. (8 points) L4. State the root cause, your confidence, the owner of the fix, a workaround with its cost, and a regression test.

    Answer key

    Root cause: platform firmware N-0.9 (or the OS, whichever set it; check which agent wrote Device Control 2) enables 10-bit Tag Requester on the SSD although the root port, the completer, does not support 10-bit tags. The PCI Express Base Specification's 10-bit tag rules require that a requester use 10-bit tags only when its completers support them, and assign responsibility to system software when enabling it (confirm the exact wording in the 10-bit tag sections). The root complex drops the upper tag bits, so completions for different outstanding reads collide; the SSD matches data to the wrong request and writes another command's data to media, silently. Confidence: high after experiment (a); confirmed if the platform vendor confirms tag truncation. Owner: platform firmware (or OS PCIe configuration code). Workaround: clear 10-bit Tag Requester Enable on platform N (cost: fewer outstanding reads; negligible at Gen4 x4 for this drive, measure it). Regression: platform qualification check that 10-bit Tag Requester Enable is set only if every completer the function talks to reports 10-bit Tag Completer support; plus a high-outstanding-read write/verify test. Secondary: the SSD silently accepted ambiguous completions; the vendor could add detection (it logged one Unexpected Completion; a stricter check would have raised an error rather than corrupting data).

  5. (6 points) L5. Write the title and the first-abnormal-event field of the bug report, and say to whom you send it.

    Answer key

    Title: "Platform N (FW N-0.9): SSD 10-bit Tag Requester Enable set while root port lacks 10-bit Tag Completer support; root port returns completions with truncated tags, causing silent write data corruption at high queue depth." First abnormal event: "Packet n (row 5): CplD with Tag 0x041, Byte Count 512, arriving while the request with Tag 0x041 already has a completion in progress; the outstanding request with Tag 0x141 never receives a completion carrying its tag." Recipients: platform firmware team (owner), SSD vendor copied (for the detection improvement), OS PCIe maintainers if the OS set the bit. See Engineering Bug Report Training.

  6. (8 points) L6. As a principal engineer, what would you change so that this class of problem is caught before a platform reaches qualification, and how do you explain the incident to a non-technical program manager in three sentences?

    Answer key

    Process: a configuration-consistency checker run at boot in qualification (and optionally in production) that validates capability/enable pairs along every path (10-bit tags, MPS, ASPM and L1 substates, Latency Tolerance Reporting (LTR), Relaxed Ordering, Extended Tags) against both link partners; data-integrity tests with high outstanding reads in every platform qualification; a requirement that pre-release platform firmware changes to PCIe enables come with a capability check; fleet telemetry for Unexpected Completion counts. Explanation to a manager: "The new server's firmware switched on a feature in the SSD that the server itself cannot handle, so under heavy load the server sometimes hands the SSD the wrong piece of data, and the SSD writes it. No data is affected on the old servers, and switching the feature off removes the problem with no measurable cost. We are adding an automatic check so that new platforms cannot ship with mismatched settings like this." Marking emphasises systemic changes (level 8) and clarity without jargon.

Grading guide

Marking principles

  • Method over final answer. In Parts C, D, F and I, award most credit for the decoding or calculation steps; a correct final number with no working earns half.
  • Honest uncertainty earns credit. "I would confirm the exact status code in the Base specification's status tables" alongside a correct name earns full marks; an invented code earns zero for that item even if the name is right.
  • Penalise invented specifics. Inventing a section number, opcode, log identifier or bit position deducts 1 point each (maximum 5 per part), because it would mislead a reader of the candidate's report.
  • Evidence references. In Parts G, H and L, statements that cite a row, register or log line score higher than the same statement without a reference.
  • Distractors. In Part L, explicitly dismissing the Receiver Errors on the other slot and the kernel scheduler change, with reasons, is part of full marks for L2.

Certification decision

  1. Two raters mark independently. Part totals that differ by more than 15% are re-marked together.
  2. Pass requires: total ≥ 225/300; Part L at level 7 or higher (both raters); every part ≥ 50%.
  3. Borderline (215–224 with Part L at level 7): a 30-minute oral scenario from Scenario Interviews decides.
  4. Feedback lists, per part, the behaviour that was missing for the next level.
  5. Retake after at least four weeks, with a different Part G and Part L scenario.

Calibrating examiners

Before marking, both raters mark the same three anonymised sample answers (one weak, one borderline, one strong) and compare levels. If they differ by more than one level on any sample, they re-read the rubric's observable behaviours together. Calibrated raters disagree mostly at the 6/7 boundary; the tie-breaker is whether the candidate chose an experiment that could prove them wrong.
