NVMe and PCIe are two separate technologies; the NVMe over PCIe Transport Specification is the agreement that joins them. This module draws the borders precisely: what the NVMe Base Specification owns, what the PCI Express Base Specification owns, what the transport binding adds, and how to tell which document governs any observation in a register dump, a kernel log or a trace.

Orientation

The Final Mental Model introduced the three-way split (NVMe, PCIe, NVMe over PCIe) with a restaurant analogy and a classification rule. The Most Important Chapter walked one READ across all three. This module goes deeper on the binding itself: how a controller is discovered as an NVMe function, how its properties become memory-mapped registers, how doorbells and interrupts are defined only for PCIe, how the memory-based transport model differs from the message-based one used by fabrics, and which failures belong to the binding rather than to either side. You will also learn to read the specification set as a set: where to look first, and what changed when the PCIe material moved out of the Base Specification.

You should already know: the classification rule in The Final Mental Model; controller registers (NVMe Registers); doorbells (Doorbells); BARs (BARs and MMIO); MSI-X (MSI-X); and the fabrics picture in NVMe-oF Mental Model, which is the best contrast for seeing what is PCIe-specific.

Where this goes next: Trace Correlation uses the binding's address map (doorbells, queue bases, MSI-X window) as its correlation context; NVMe/TCP and NVMe/RDMA are the sibling bindings.

Kid story

Two friends play chess. The rules of chess say how each piece moves and how you win. Those rules are the same whether you play across a table, by sending postcards, or over a walkie-talkie.

When the friends play across a table, they agree on table rules: the board sits in the middle; when you finish your move you tap the clock; and here is the odd part: your friend is allowed to reach over and pick up the notes you leave on your side of the table, and to put her answers there too, without asking. When they play by postcard, the table rules are different: every move is written on a card and posted, and nobody reaches into anybody's house.

Chess rules are NVMe. The table, the clock and the reaching across are PCIe. The little sheet of table rules is NVMe over PCIe.

What it hides. The table-rules sheet is not small: discovery through a PCI class code, the whole register map in a BAR, interrupts, controller memory, PCIe resets and power states are binding matters. The story also hides that PCIe is a full network with its own errors, retries and flow control, not a passive table.

Where it misleads. Chess rules are perfectly separate from the table; NVMe is not perfectly separate from its transport. The NVMe Base Specification describes a "memory-based" transport model that PCIe fits, and some concepts (PRPs, controller properties) are defined in Base but used differently per transport. And the story suggests you could swap tables mid-game; a real controller is either a PCIe controller or a fabrics controller, and a host cannot move a live controller between transports.

Mechanism

The binding's work is mostly done once, at bring-up, and then repeated in a tiny form for every command. We follow bring-up of the Lab SSD from enumeration to the first admin completion.

  1. Identify the function as NVMe. Who: platform firmware and the OS PCI core. What changes: nothing in the device; the OS learns class code 010802h and binds the nvme driver. Where: configuration space of 01:00.0. Evidence: CfgRd0 TLPs to offset 08h (Revision ID / Class Code); lspci class line.
  2. Locate the register window. Who: enumeration software. What: BAR0/BAR1 sized and programmed to 0xFE60_0000; Memory Space Enable set. Where: configuration offsets 10h, 14h, 04h. Evidence: CfgWr0 to those offsets; lspci "Region 0".
  3. Enable bus mastering and interrupts. Who: OS. What: Bus Master Enable set; MSI-X capability enabled; table entries written with address and data. Where: Command register; MSI-X Message Control; table at BAR0 + 0x2000. Evidence: CfgWr0; MWr32 into 0xFE60_2000 range.
  4. Read the controller's capabilities. Who: NVMe driver. What: CAP and VS read. Where: BAR0 + 00h, + 08h. Evidence: MRd32 from the root complex and CplD from 0x0100 with CAP = 0x0040_0830_1401_0FFF, VS = 0x0002_0000.
  5. Configure the admin queues. Who: driver. What: AQA = 0x001F_001F; ASQ = 0x1_0000_0000; ACQ = 0x1_0000_1000. Where: BAR0 + 24h, 28h, 30h. Evidence: MWr32 TLPs (64-bit registers written as two 32-bit halves on the lab system).
  6. Enable the controller. Who: driver. What: CC = 0x0046_0061 (EN = 1, CSS, IOSQES 6, IOCQES 4, MPS 0). Where: BAR0 + 14h. Evidence: MWr32.
  7. Wait for ready. Who: controller (initialises), driver (polls). What: CSTS.RDY goes 0 → 1 within CAP.TO (10 s here). Where: BAR0 + 1Ch. Evidence: repeated MRd32 of CSTS; the first completion with data 0x0000_0001.
  8. First admin command. Who: driver, then controller. What: Identify Controller (06h) placed in Admin SQ slot 0; Admin SQ tail doorbell = 1. Where: host memory 0x1_0000_0000; BAR0 + 1000h. Evidence: MWr32 to 0xFE60_1000; MRd64 of the SQE; 4 KiB of MWr64 data; CQE MWr64 to 0x1_0000_1000; MSI-X write for vector 0.

The central concept on the ladder

The central concept is the transport binding: the agreement that turns NVMe's abstract objects (properties, queues, commands, completions, data) into concrete PCIe objects (registers, memory regions, TLPs, interrupts).

Chess rules stay the same; the table rules say where the board is, how to tap the clock, and that your friend may reach across.

NVMe defines what a controller does; PCIe defines how devices talk over a link; NVMe over PCIe defines where NVMe's queues, settings and signals live on PCIe.

The controller decodes BAR0 accesses into property reads/writes and doorbell updates, runs bus-master engines that fetch queues and move data in host memory, and raises MSI-X writes from a table the OS programmed.

NVMe Base 2.4 defines properties, queues, commands, status and the memory-based transport model; NVMe over PCIe Transport 1.4 defines the PCI register interface, the doorbell registers, interrupt use, and PCIe-specific behaviour; the PCI Express Base Specification defines the packets, configuration space and capabilities.

Host-to-device memory writes to BAR0 carry property writes and doorbells; device-to-host memory reads and writes carry queues and data; device-to-host memory writes to the MSI-X address carry interrupts; configuration requests carry discovery and setup.

In an analyzer, only the binding turns "MWr32 0xFE60_1008" into "SQ1 tail doorbell" and "MRd64 0x1_2340_0140" into "SQE fetch". Without the BAR address, CAP.DSTRD, queue bases and the MSI-X table, an NVMe decoder cannot decode.

Ask three separate questions: does the PCIe function work (config reads, link, BARs)? Does the NVMe controller work (CSTS, admin commands)? Are they joined correctly (doorbell layout, interrupt vectors, queue placement and contiguity)? Binding faults often pass the first two checks.

Cells marked Uncertain are about which document holds the text, not about the behaviour. The NVMe 2.0 restructuring moved PCIe material out of the Base Specification, and later revisions have continued to adjust the boundary. Confirm placement in the tables of contents of NVMe Base 2.4 and NVMe over PCIe Transport 1.4 before citing.

Well established: NVMe controllers on PCIe identify with class code 010802h; controller registers are memory-mapped through BAR0 (with BAR1 as the upper half of a 64-bit BAR); CAP is at 00h, VS 08h, INTMS 0Ch, INTMC 10h, CC 14h, CSTS 1Ch, NSSR 20h, AQA 24h, ASQ 28h, ACQ 30h; doorbells start at 1000h with spacing 4 << CAP.DSTRD; INTMS/INTMC are not used when MSI-X is enabled; Create I/O SQ/CQ carry a Physically Contiguous (PC) bit, and CAP.CQR = 1 requires it set.

Reasoned: the classification rule ("would it still be true over TCP?") is a heuristic derived from the document split, not a rule stated in any specification. The "same action on two transports" table is a comparison we built from the Base, PCIe Transport and fabrics transport documents.

Implementation-dependent: BAR size; whether the MSI-X table lives in BAR0 or another BAR (found through the capability's Table BIR and offset); how many vectors are offered; time to ready within CAP.TO; whether 64-bit register accesses are accepted as single TLPs; CMB/PMR presence and size; whether FLR is supported.

Version-dependent: before NVMe 2.0 there was no separate PCIe transport document; NVMe 2.0 (2021) introduced NVMe over PCIe Transport 1.0, now at 1.4 in the 2026 set. Some features have PCIe-specific aspects introduced in the 2.4 set, such as PCIe Exported NVM Subsystem Migration; their field-level details must be read in the 2.4 documents (see 2026 NVMe 2.4 Updates). PCIe revisions add capabilities (for example FLIT mode) that change link behaviour without changing the NVMe binding.

The NVMe documents do not define PCIe link behaviour, TLP formats or AER; the PCIe specification does not know what a submission queue is. Neither says how an OS must sequence enumeration and driver binding. Check in the specification: the exact list of PCI capabilities an NVMe controller must implement; the required behaviour for register accesses of unsupported widths; the rule for PRP versus SGL on admin commands; how each PCIe reset type affects controller state and CSTS.

Never cite the PCIe specification for an NVMe rule, or the NVMe Base Specification for a PCIe rule. Never assume a fabrics behaviour (for example a Connect command or a keep-alive timeout) exists on PCIe, or vice versa (doorbells, MSI-X).

The binding makes the submission path cheap by letting the host write doorbells without reading anything. Reads of doorbell registers are not meaningful (their read value is not defined for use by the host Uncertain: check the doorbell register definitions), and a read would cost a full non-posted round trip. A driver that reads a doorbell is wasting microseconds per command and learning nothing.

Ordering the binding depends on

  • Host side: the SQE must be visible in memory before the doorbell write reaches the controller. The CPU's store to cacheable memory and its MMIO store are ordered by a write barrier (Linux's writel includes the necessary ordering on common architectures). PCIe then delivers the doorbell; the controller's fetch is a later read, which sees the SQE because it was written first.
  • Device side: data writes, then the CQE write, then the MSI-X write, all posted and without Relaxed Ordering on the CQE and MSI-X (see DMA with NVMe).
  • Register sequencing: CC.EN = 1 only after AQA, ASQ and ACQ are written; the posted writes arrive in order, so no read-back is needed, though some drivers read back for robustness.

Analysis. Legal but alarming. The controller is answering every read with real data, so PCIe and the binding are fine; the NVMe controller is doing internal recovery (for example rebuilding mapping tables after an unsafe shutdown, which the SMART log's Unsafe Shutdowns count can corroborate). The host must wait up to CAP.TO (here 0x14 × 500 ms = 10 s). Risks: a host with a shorter private timeout gives up and resets, which may restart the recovery and make things worse; boot firmware with a shorter timeout may skip the drive. The evidence that distinguishes "slow controller" from "dead link" is the completion data: 0x0000_0000 is a valid CSTS; 0xFFFF_FFFF would not be. Compare with Reset for timeouts after resets.

Intermediate lab — A binding-aware register and doorbell decoder

Safe lab Python 3, standard library only.

Objective
Decode CAP, CC and AQA, compute the doorbell map for any stride, and classify an MMIO address as property, doorbell (with queue and type) or MSI-X table.
Prerequisites
NVMe Registers, Doorbells.
Setup
Save as binding_map.py and run it.
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Decode NVMe-over-PCIe register values and classify BAR0 offsets (teaching tool)."""

PROPS = {0x00: "CAP", 0x08: "VS", 0x0C: "INTMS", 0x10: "INTMC", 0x14: "CC",
         0x1C: "CSTS", 0x20: "NSSR", 0x24: "AQA", 0x28: "ASQ", 0x30: "ACQ"}

def bits(v, hi, lo):
    return (v >> lo) & ((1 << (hi - lo + 1)) - 1)

def decode_cap(cap):
    return {"MQES (entries)": bits(cap, 15, 0) + 1, "CQR": bits(cap, 16, 16),
            "TO (ms)": bits(cap, 31, 24) * 500, "DSTRD": bits(cap, 35, 32),
            "NSSRS": bits(cap, 36, 36), "CSS": hex(bits(cap, 44, 37)),
            "MPSMIN (bytes)": 4096 << bits(cap, 51, 48),
            "MPSMAX (bytes)": 4096 << bits(cap, 55, 52)}

def decode_cc(cc):
    return {"EN": bits(cc, 0, 0), "CSS": bin(bits(cc, 6, 4)),
            "MPS (bytes)": 4096 << bits(cc, 10, 7), "SHN": bits(cc, 15, 14),
            "IOSQES (bytes)": 1 << bits(cc, 19, 16), "IOCQES (bytes)": 1 << bits(cc, 23, 20)}

def decode_aqa(aqa):
    return {"ASQS (entries)": bits(aqa, 11, 0) + 1, "ACQS (entries)": bits(aqa, 27, 16) + 1}

def classify(offset, dstrd, msix_table=0x2000, msix_entries=33, pba=0x3000):
    stride = 4 << dstrd
    if offset in PROPS:
        return PROPS[offset]
    if offset < 0x1000:
        return "property space (other or reserved)"
    if msix_table <= offset < msix_table + 16 * msix_entries:
        e, f = divmod(offset - msix_table, 16)
        return f"MSI-X table entry {e} ({['addr lo', 'addr hi', 'data', 'vector ctrl'][f // 4]})"
    if pba <= offset < pba + 8:
        return "MSI-X PBA"
    idx, rem = divmod(offset - 0x1000, stride)
    if rem:
        return f"between doorbells (stride {stride}): invalid"
    q, is_cq = divmod(idx, 2)
    return f"{'CQ' if is_cq else 'SQ'}{q} {'head' if is_cq else 'tail'} doorbell"

if __name__ == "__main__":
    cap, cc, aqa = 0x0040_0830_1401_0FFF, 0x0046_0061, 0x001F_001F
    for name, d in (("CAP", decode_cap(cap)), ("CC", decode_cc(cc)), ("AQA", decode_aqa(aqa))):
        print(name, d)
    for dstrd in (0, 1):
        print(f"\nDSTRD={dstrd}")
        for off in (0x14, 0x1000, 0x1004, 0x1008, 0x100C, 0x1010, 0x1018, 0x2010, 0x200C, 0x3000):
            print(f"  BAR0+0x{off:04X}: {classify(off, dstrd)}")
Procedure
  1. Run it. Compare the CAP decode with NVMe Registers.
  2. Read the two doorbell maps. Find the address whose meaning changes most between DSTRD 0 and 1.
  3. Add the CAP value 0x0040_0831_1401_0FFF and confirm the stride.
Observations
0x1008 is SQ1 tail at DSTRD 0, CQ0 head at DSTRD 1; 0x100C is CQ1 head at DSTRD 0 and "between doorbells" at DSTRD 1.
Questions
  1. Why is BAR0 + 0x1000 safe regardless of stride?
  2. Why can the MSI-X table share BAR0 with doorbells without overlapping?
  3. What does an MSI-X vector-control write look like in a trace?
Expected reasoning
The first doorbell has index 0; the table location comes from the MSI-X capability; vector control is the fourth dword of each 16-byte entry.
Solution

CAP decodes to MQES 4096, CQR 1, TO 10,000 ms, DSTRD 0, NSSRS 1, CSS 0x41 (bits 0 and 6 of the field, i.e. CAP bits 37 and 43), MPSMIN 4 KiB, MPSMAX 64 KiB. CC: EN 1, CSS 0b110, MPS 4 KiB, SHN 0, IOSQES 64, IOCQES 16. AQA: 32 and 32. (1) Index 0 × stride = 0, so SQ0 tail is always at 0x1000; this is why admin traffic hides stride bugs. (2) The controller chooses the table offset (here 0x2000) and advertises it in the MSI-X capability; a driver must read it, never assume it. With 1024 queue pairs at stride 4 the doorbell region would end at 0x1000 + 8 × 1024 = 0x3000, so a real controller sizes its BAR and places its table to avoid overlap. (3) A host→device MWr32 to BAR0 + 0x200C + 16 × n with data bit 0 = 1 (masked) or 0 (unmasked): an OS action, not a doorbell. 0x200C is entry 0's vector control and 0x2010 is entry 1's address-low dword.

Interview

Beginner

  1. Name the three specifications involved when an NVMe SSD sits in a PCIe slot.

    Model answer

    NVMe Base (with the command set specifications), the NVMe over PCIe Transport Specification, and the PCI Express Base Specification.

  2. How does the operating system know a PCIe function is an NVMe controller?

    Model answer

    From its class code: base class 01h (mass storage), subclass 08h (non-volatile memory), programming interface 02h (NVM Express).

  3. Where are NVMe controller registers on PCIe?

    Model answer

    Memory-mapped in the space described by BAR0 (and BAR1 for the upper 32 bits of a 64-bit BAR), starting at offset 0.

  4. Does NVMe over TCP have doorbells?

    Model answer

    No. Commands are sent in capsules over the connection; doorbells exist only in the PCIe binding.

  5. Which interrupt mechanism do NVMe SSDs normally use on PCIe?

    Model answer

    MSI-X, with one vector per completion queue in typical drivers; MSI and pin-based INTx are fallbacks.

Intermediate

  1. What is a controller property and how is it accessed on PCIe and on fabrics?

    Model answer

    A named piece of controller state defined in the Base Specification (CAP, CC, CSTS…). On PCIe it is a memory-mapped register in BAR0; on fabrics it is read and written with Property Get and Property Set commands.

  2. Why do the INTMS and INTMC registers not affect MSI-X?

    Model answer

    They exist for pin-based and MSI interrupts, which lack per-vector masking; MSI-X has a per-vector Mask bit in its table and a Function Mask, so the binding does not use INTMS/INTMC with MSI-X.

  3. What does CAP.CQR require of the host?

    Model answer

    That I/O queues are physically contiguous (in the address space the device uses) and that the host sets PC = 1 when creating them; the host cannot use a PRP list for queue memory on such a controller.

  4. How is the admin queue created on PCIe compared with fabrics?

    Model answer

    On PCIe the host writes AQA, ASQ and ACQ registers before enabling the controller; on fabrics the admin queue is established by the Connect command.

  5. What changed for PCIe material in NVMe 2.0?

    Model answer

    It moved from the Base Specification into a separate NVMe over PCIe Transport Specification (1.0 then, 1.4 in the 2026 set), alongside the TCP and RDMA transport specifications.

Advanced

  1. Explain the memory-based transport model and its key asymmetry.

    Model answer

    Queues and data live in memory both sides can reach; the host writes commands into memory and signals with a doorbell, and the controller pulls commands and moves data itself. The asymmetry: almost all traffic after the doorbell is initiated by the device, which is why device DMA, Bus Master Enable and the IOMMU matter so much.

  2. How do you place submission queues in the Controller Memory Buffer, and how does the trace change?

    Model answer

    If the CMB supports submission queues, the host points ASQ or Create I/O SQ at CMB addresses and writes SQEs with MMIO into the device BAR. The trace shows host→device MWr of 64-byte SQEs and no device MRd fetch; correlation must key on writes into the CMB range.

  3. How do PCIe resets relate to NVMe resets?

    Model answer

    NVMe defines Controller Level Reset (CC.EN 1 → 0) and NVM Subsystem Reset. PCIe resets (conventional, hot, FLR) reset the function; the binding defines that they also reset the controller, with the additional loss of PCIe state (BARs, MSI-X) for conventional resets. Software must restore PCIe state before NVMe initialisation.

  4. Why can an analyzer's NVMe decode be wrong even when every TLP is captured correctly?

    Model answer

    Because decoding needs binding context: BAR0 address, DSTRD, queue bases and sizes, MSI-X table. If capture starts after initialisation or the context changes (reset, rebar), the decoder applies stale assumptions.

  5. Give an example of an NVMe error caused by a binding misuse.

    Model answer

    A doorbell written for a nonexistent queue produces an asynchronous event of type Error status, "Write to Invalid Doorbell Register": an NVMe-reported event whose cause is in the binding.

Senior

  1. In a design review someone cites the PCIe specification for a CQ phase-tag rule. What do you say?

    Model answer

    The phase tag is an NVMe Base concept; PCIe knows nothing of queues. Cite NVMe Base for the rule, the PCIe Transport for where the CQ lives on PCIe, and PCIe for ordering that makes the CQE visible after data. Wrong citations lead to wrong owners for bugs.

  2. How would you design a conformance test plan specifically for the binding?

    Model answer

    Configuration space requirements (class code, capabilities); register access widths and alignments; reserved bits; doorbell stride and invalid doorbell handling with expected asynchronous events; CQR and PC behaviour; interrupt modes (MSI-X, MSI, INTx) and masking; IV mapping per CQ; CMB/PMR if present; behaviour across each PCIe reset type; behaviour with MSE or BME cleared.

  3. A drive works in one server and fails to initialise in another. How do you separate PCIe, binding and NVMe causes?

    Model answer

    PCIe: link trains, configuration reads valid, BAR assigned. Binding: register reads return real values, MSI-X vectors allocated, CC accepted, doorbell addresses correct. NVMe: CSTS.RDY within CAP.TO, Identify succeeds, no CFS. Compare platforms at each stage; differences in vector allocation, BAR placement above 4 GiB, or boot-firmware timeouts are common binding-level culprits.

  4. What risks does the 2.4 PCIe Exported NVM Subsystem Migration add to binding-level debugging?

    Model answer

    Controllers and namespaces are recreated on a destination with matching identifiers and configuration templates, so the same identifiers may appear on different PCIe functions or BARs; correlation that keys on BDF or BAR alone becomes ambiguous during and after migration. Field-level details must be read in the 2.4 documents.

  5. Why is it valuable that NVMe's core is transport-independent, from a debugging standpoint?

    Model answer

    It lets you reproduce NVMe-level behaviour (status, command semantics) on another transport or an emulator, removing PCIe from the experiment. If a bug follows the controller across transports, it is NVMe; if it disappears, suspect the binding or PCIe.

Quiz

  1. Which document defines the CQE Status Field?

    • NVMe Base Specification
    • NVMe over PCIe Transport Specification
    • PCI Express Base Specification
    • NVM Command Set Specification only
    Answer and reasoning

    Status is part of the core queue model in the Base Specification (command sets add their own codes).

  2. On a controller with CAP.DSTRD = 1, where is the CQ1 head doorbell?

    • BAR0 + 0x100C
    • BAR0 + 0x1008
    • BAR0 + 0x1010
    • BAR0 + 0x1018
    Answer and reasoning

    Stride 8: 0x1000 + (2 × 1 + 1) × 8 = 0x1018.

  3. Which item has no equivalent in NVMe over TCP?

    • Identify Controller
    • MSI-X vector per CQ
    • CC.EN
    • Command Identifier
    Answer and reasoning

    Interrupt vectors exist only on PCIe. CC exists on fabrics as a property; CID and Identify are core NVMe.

  4. What does a CSTS read of 0x0000_0000 that repeats for 8 seconds after CC.EN = 1 indicate?

    • The link is down
    • The controller is fatal
    • The controller is alive but not yet ready, which may be legal up to CAP.TO
    • The BAR is unassigned
    Answer and reasoning

    Real completions with valid data mean PCIe works; RDY = 0 means not ready; CAP.TO bounds the wait.

  5. The class code of an NVMe I/O controller is:

    • 010601h
    • 010802h
    • 020000h
    • 010800h
    Answer and reasoning

    Mass storage 01h, non-volatile memory 08h, NVM Express I/O controller programming interface 02h. 010601h is AHCI (SATA).

  6. Which TLP type carries a doorbell on the lab system?

    • MWr32
    • MWr64
    • CfgWr0
    • Msg
    Answer and reasoning

    BAR0 is at 0xFE60_0000, below 4 GiB, so the 3-DW header MWr32 is used.

  7. State the three-question test that separates PCIe, binding and NVMe faults when a drive fails to initialise.

    Model answer

    Does the PCIe function work (link, configuration space, BAR)? Are the two joined correctly (register reads real, CC accepted, doorbells and vectors right)? Does the NVMe controller work (RDY within CAP.TO, no CFS, admin commands succeed)?

  8. Why can an analyzer not decode NVMe commands from a capture that starts after initialisation, and what can you do?

    Model answer

    It lacks binding context: queue bases, sizes, doorbell stride, MSI-X table. Supply the context manually (from nvme show-regs, driver logs or debugfs), or infer it from traffic patterns (see Trace Correlation).

  9. Give two behaviours the binding must define that the Base Specification cannot.

    Model answer

    The register layout of doorbells (with stride) and interrupt use (MSI-X vectors, INTMS/INTMC). Others: class code, required PCI capabilities, PCIe reset effects, CMB placement.

Teach-back

  1. To a child. Why are there three rule books for one SSD?

    Model explanation

    One book is the rules of the game: how to ask for a story and how the answer comes back. One book is about the road: how trucks drive and what they carry. The third little book says how to play this game on this road: where to leave your note, which bell to ring, and that the helper may come into your room to fetch the note.

  2. To a junior engineer. What does the NVMe over PCIe Transport Specification add?

    Model explanation

    It says how an NVMe controller appears as a PCIe function (class code, BARs, capabilities), that its properties are registers in BAR0, where the doorbells are and how far apart, that queues and data are in host memory (or controller memory) and are accessed by the controller with DMA, how interrupts work (MSI-X per CQ, or MSI/INTx with INTMS/INTMC), and how PCIe resets and power states affect the controller. Everything else, such as commands, status and queues as concepts, is Base; packets and links are PCIe.

  3. To a senior engineer. Why does the separation matter in a failure analysis?

    Model explanation

    Because each document defines a different contract with a different owner, and binding faults pass both other layers' health checks. A drive can train its link, answer configuration reads and report a healthy CSTS while a stride or vector mismatch prevents I/O. Classify each observation by layer, cite the right document, test each contract independently, and use transport independence (reproduce on another transport or an emulator) to remove PCIe from the experiment.

Mastery gate

Do not consider this module mastered until you can:

  • explain in two minutes what NVMe Base, the NVMe over PCIe Transport and the PCI Express Base Specification each define
  • draw the three-band diagram and place doorbells, properties, MSI-X, PRPs, TLPs and resets correctly
  • read a bring-up trace and label each TLP with the document that gives it meaning
  • debug a stride mismatch, a vector mismatch and a slow ready from evidence
  • distinguish the binding from its neighbours: PCIe registers and BARs (BAR and MMIO), NVMe registers (NVMe Registers), doorbells (Doorbells), the fabrics bindings (NVMe/TCP)
  • cite the right source for each rule, and say when its placement between Base and Transport must be checked
