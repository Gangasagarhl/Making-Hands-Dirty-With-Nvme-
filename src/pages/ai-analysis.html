How to use a large language model (LLM) responsibly when analyzing NVM Express (NVMe) and PCI Express (PCIe) traces: where it helps, where it fails, and the architecture, guardrails, confidence model, validation and report format that keep it honest. The model never sees raw packets as its source of truth and never invents them; it reasons over deterministic, normalized evidence and every claim it makes is checked.

Why this part exists

LLMs are fluent. Given a trace excerpt and a question, they produce a confident, well-structured explanation in seconds. Sometimes that explanation is excellent: it connects a Completion Queue Entry (CQE) error to an earlier Unsupported Request (UR) completion, suggests checking Input–Output Memory Management Unit (IOMMU) logs, and drafts a clear report. Sometimes it invents a packet that is not in the capture, quotes a status code value from a different revision, or adds two hexadecimal numbers incorrectly — in the same confident tone.

In protocol debugging, a wrong answer delivered confidently is worse than no answer, because it sends a team down the wrong path and erodes trust in the tooling. This part teaches the discipline that makes LLM assistance net-positive: deterministic evidence first, model reasoning second, mechanical validation always.

Learning objectives

  • List the specific ways LLMs fail on protocol data and the countermeasure for each.
  • Design an architecture in which raw traces pass through deterministic parsers before any model reasoning.
  • Use retrieval and specification grounding so that every specification statement a model makes is a quoted, cited passage.
  • Apply the guardrails: no invented packets, timestamps, fields, register values, specification requirements or root causes; EVIDENCE → CLAIM, never CLAIM → imagined evidence.
  • Use the five-level confidence model (CONFIRMED, HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW, UNKNOWN) and explain why it is not a probability.
  • Build a claim validator and an evaluation corpus.
  • Produce an automated HTML report in the standard section order.

The story

Imagine a brilliant storyteller joins a detective team. Give them clues and they can tell you a wonderful story about what happened. But if a clue is missing, they will happily make one up so the story flows. So the detectives make a rule: the storyteller may only talk about clues that are in the evidence box, must hold up each clue while talking about it, and a second detective checks every clue against the box before anyone believes the story.

An LLM in trace analysis is like a very well-read junior analyst who has never touched your hardware. They have read thousands of documents and can explain anything, but they cannot see your lab, they remember specifications imperfectly, and they are eager to please. A good manager gives such an analyst structured data rather than raw dumps, asks them to cite exactly what they relied on, and reviews every conclusion before it leaves the team.

A junior analyst learns from feedback and remembers yesterday's mistake; a model in a fresh session does not. A junior who is unsure usually hesitates; a model's tone does not reliably signal its uncertainty. A junior who invents data has made a moral choice; a model generating a plausible packet is doing exactly what it was built to do — produce likely text. So the controls must be mechanical, not based on trust or tone.

Retrieval

Retrieval decides what the model sees. A model cannot reason about evidence it was not given, and it will fill gaps with plausible inventions. Good retrieval is therefore half of hallucination prevention.

What to retrieve

  • Event windows around each finding: for example 50 events before and 20 after the first abnormal event, plus all events linked to the same command.
  • Command records for every command touched by a finding, with their lifecycle events.
  • System context: link speed and width, BAR0, queue table, MPS/MRRS, IOMMU state, specification revisions claimed by the device (VS register) and by the decoder tables.
  • Specification passages: from your field dictionary, command matrix and notes, chunked by section name, each carrying document, revision and section name.
  • Prior solved cases with similar findings, clearly labelled as analogies, not evidence.
  • A statement of what was not included: "events outside 0–60 µs omitted; 2 capture gaps".

Retrieval keys

Retrieve by structured keys first (event kind, rule ID, status name, opcode, register name), then by text similarity. A finding "R2: completion status UR" should pull the PCIe completion status passage and the error-handling notes for UR deterministically, not by hoping a similarity search finds them.

Window sizes, chunk sizes and the retrieval method are engineering choices that depend on your model's context size and your corpus. Measure them on your evaluation set.

Specification grounding

Specification grounding means every statement about what a specification requires is backed by a retrieved passage the model was shown, cited by ID, and traceable to a document, revision and section name.

  • Passages carry IDs (S1, S2 …) and a citation string, for example "NVMe Base 2.4 — Generic Command Status".
  • The model may paraphrase but must cite; it may not introduce a requirement not present in a cited passage.
  • Normative strength is preserved: a passage that says should must not become "the device must".
  • When no passage covers a question, the correct output is "not covered by retrieved specification text", and the retrieval gap is logged.

A model's training data may include older revisions, drafts, forum posts and vendor documents. "The specification says" from an ungrounded model may mean any of these. Treat ungrounded specification claims as LOW confidence at best.

EVIDENCE → CLAIM

The direction of reasoning is fixed. Start from evidence that exists and derive the claim it supports. Never start from a claim and describe the evidence that "would" support it as if it had been seen.

valid claim = statement + { evidence IDs ⊆ event store } + { spec IDs ⊆ retrieved passages } + confidence + falsification test
statement
the sentence the model asserts
evidence IDs
event identifiers from the normalized event store that support the statement; must be non-empty
spec IDs
identifiers of retrieved specification passages the statement relies on (may be empty for pure observations)
confidence
one of CONFIRMED, HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW, UNKNOWN, chosen by the criteria below
falsification test
an observation that would show the statement is false (required for hypotheses)

Structured output

Require the model to answer in a structured form — a list of claim objects — rather than free prose. Prose is generated afterwards from validated claims. The kind of a claim is one of observation, causal, hypothesis, root_cause or question. A claim object:

{
  "id": "C2",
  "kind": "causal",
  "claim": "The Write cid 0x0041 failed because its data read received a UR completion",
  "evidence": ["E0008", "E0009", "E0015"],
  "asserts": {"E0009": {"status": "UR"}, "E0015": {"sct": 0, "sc": 4}},
  "spec": ["S1", "S2"],
  "confidence": "HIGH",
  "falsify": "A CQE with the same status on a command with no unsuccessful completion",
  "next": "Check the host IOMMU fault log for IOVA 0x2_0000_1000"
}

A task template

An illustrative instruction block for the reasoning step. It is model-agnostic and contains no data; the pipeline fills the placeholders.

ROLE: You assist a PCIe/NVMe protocol engineer. You do not decode packets; they are decoded for you.
INPUTS:
  CONTEXT     = {system context with sources}
  EVENTS      = {normalized events with IDs, window W, omitted ranges listed}
  FINDINGS    = {rule findings, sorted by detection time; first abnormal event marked}
  SPEC        = {retrieved passages S1..Sn with document, revision, section name}
RULES:
  1. Use only EVENTS and SPEC. If something is not there, say "not in evidence".
  2. Every claim cites event IDs. Specification statements cite S-IDs and keep shall/should/may.
  3. Do not state numbers (times, addresses, tags, status values) except by citing the event that holds them.
  4. Start from the first abnormal event. For it, answer the senior questions you can from evidence;
     list the ones you cannot.
  5. Propose hypotheses with confidence (CONFIRMED/HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW/UNKNOWN per the criteria given)
     and a falsification test each. Do not mark causal claims CONFIRMED.
  6. Do not adopt a hypothesis suggested by the user unless the evidence supports it; say so.
OUTPUT: JSON list of claim objects, then a list of open questions phrased as experiments.

The confidence model

Every claim carries one of five confidence levels. Each level is defined by the kind of evidence behind it, so two engineers (or a validator) can check that the level is justified.

Confidence levels
LevelEvidence criterionExample (reference trace)
CONFIRMEDDirectly observed in decoded evidence, or demonstrated by a reproduced experiment that isolates the cause"The Write cid 0x0041 completed with Data Transfer Error" (E0015)
HIGHMultiple independent pieces of evidence share identifiers and are in causal order; known mechanism; no contradicting evidence"The CQE error is a consequence of the UR on its data read" (E0008, E0009, E0015 linked by address and command)
MEDIUMEvidence consistent with the claim, but alternatives remain plausible and are not excluded"cid 0x0042 was fetched and never completed in the window; a controller stall is likely"
LOWPlausible by mechanism or analogy; weak or indirect evidence; no shared identifier"The stall of cid 0x0042 may be a side effect of the earlier UR"
UNKNOWNEvidence absent or insufficient (outside the window, not captured, gap)"Whether slot 3 was fetched" (capture ended)

Why confidence is not probability

"HIGH" does not mean "85 % likely". A probability is a number with a defined meaning: over many similar cases, the claim is true that fraction of the time. That requires a model of the cases and calibration data — for example, a labelled corpus of past investigations where you measured how often HIGH claims turned out true. Without that, attaching a percentage creates false precision: it invites people to multiply and compare numbers that were never measured.

The five levels are an ordinal scale defined by evidence criteria. They tell the reader what kind of support a claim has and therefore what it would take to change it. If your organization does collect outcome data, you can later calibrate: measure the fraction of HIGH claims later confirmed and report it alongside. Only then is a number honest.

Confidence of a chain is bounded by its weakest link. A HIGH causal link built on a MEDIUM observation is at most MEDIUM. Validators can enforce this mechanically when claims cite other claims.

Uncertainty and hallucination prevention

Expressing uncertainty

  • List unknowns explicitly: what the capture cannot show (internal controller state, the other link, host software timing, events outside the window).
  • Keep alternatives: every MEDIUM or LOW hypothesis is shown with its competitors.
  • Turn unknowns into experiments: "capture 10 ms longer", "read the IOMMU fault log", "repeat with IOMMU in passthrough mode on a lab system".
  • Abstain when evidence is insufficient: "no root cause can be stated from this capture" is a correct, valuable output.

A claim validator

The claim validator is ordinary deterministic code. It does not judge whether reasoning is clever; it checks that reasoning is supported. Below is a runnable miniature. The events are the normalized output of the reference pipeline; the "LLM output" is a hand-written example of what a model might return, including four typical faults.

# claim_validator.py - check an LLM's structured analysis against deterministic evidence.
# The events are the normalized output of the reference pipeline (synthetic trace).
EVENTS = {
    "E0008": {"kind": "dma_read", "t_ns": 3300, "addr": 0x2_0000_1000, "tag": 0x13, "cmd": "1:0x0041"},
    "E0009": {"kind": "completion", "t_ns": 3950, "status": "UR", "tag": 0x13, "request": "E0008"},
    "E0015": {"kind": "cqe_write", "t_ns": 53000, "cid": 0x0041, "sqid": 1, "sct": 0, "sc": 0x04},
    "E0006": {"kind": "sqe_fetch", "t_ns": 2150, "slot": 2, "cmd": "1:0x0042"},
}
SPEC = {   # retrieved specification notes, each with its citation
    "S1": "PCIe Base Spec, Transaction Layer - completion status 001b = Unsupported Request",
    "S2": "NVMe Base 2.4, Generic Command Status - 04h = Data Transfer Error",
}
def command_of(eid):
    """Which command an event belongs to, following correlation links (deterministic)."""
    e = EVENTS.get(eid, {})
    if "cmd" in e:
        return e["cmd"]
    if "request" in e:
        return command_of(e["request"])
    if e.get("kind") == "cqe_write":
        return f"{e['sqid']}:0x{e['cid']:04X}"
    return None

LEVELS = ["UNKNOWN", "LOW", "MEDIUM", "HIGH", "CONFIRMED"]

LLM_OUTPUT = [   # what a model returned (structured output was required)
    {"id": "C1", "claim": "The Write command cid 0x0041 failed with Data Transfer Error",
     "evidence": ["E0015"], "spec": ["S2"], "asserts": {"E0015": {"sc": 0x04, "sct": 0}},
     "confidence": "CONFIRMED", "kind": "observation"},
    {"id": "C2", "claim": "The failure was caused by the UR completion to the controller's data read",
     "evidence": ["E0008", "E0009", "E0015"], "spec": ["S1", "S2"],
     "asserts": {"E0009": {"status": "UR"}}, "confidence": "HIGH", "kind": "causal"},
    {"id": "C3", "claim": "The host IOMMU logged a fault for IOVA 0x200001000",
     "evidence": ["E0021"], "spec": [], "asserts": {}, "confidence": "HIGH", "kind": "observation"},
    {"id": "C4", "claim": "The data read used tag 0x14",
     "evidence": ["E0008"], "spec": [], "asserts": {"E0008": {"tag": 0x14}},
     "confidence": "CONFIRMED", "kind": "observation"},
    {"id": "C5", "claim": "The Read cid 0x0042 hung because of firmware deadlock",
     "evidence": [], "spec": [], "asserts": {}, "confidence": "HIGH", "kind": "root_cause"},
    {"id": "C6", "claim": "Root cause: the UR caused the controller to stall cid 0x0042",
     "evidence": ["E0009", "E0006"], "spec": ["S1"], "asserts": {}, "confidence": "CONFIRMED",
     "kind": "causal"},
]

def validate(c):
    problems = []
    if not c["evidence"]:
        problems.append("no evidence: EVIDENCE -> CLAIM violated")
    for e in c["evidence"]:
        if e not in EVENTS:
            problems.append(f"cites non-existent event {e}")
    for s in c["spec"]:
        if s not in SPEC:
            problems.append(f"cites non-retrieved spec passage {s}")
    for e, fields in c["asserts"].items():
        for f, v in fields.items():
            actual = EVENTS.get(e, {}).get(f)
            if actual != v:
                fmt = lambda x: hex(x) if isinstance(x, int) else repr(x)
                problems.append(f"{e}.{f}: claim says {fmt(v)}, decoded value is {fmt(actual)}")
    if c["confidence"] not in LEVELS:
        problems.append("unknown confidence level")
    # Causal claims need at least two events in causal (time) order and cannot be CONFIRMED
    # from timing alone; CONFIRMED needs a direct observation or a reproduced experiment.
    if c["kind"] in ("causal", "root_cause"):
        ts = [EVENTS[e]["t_ns"] for e in c["evidence"] if e in EVENTS]
        if len(ts) < 2:
            problems.append("causal claim with fewer than two evidence events")
        cmds = {command_of(e) for e in c["evidence"] if e in EVENTS} - {None}
        if len(cmds) > 1:
            problems.append(f"evidence spans unrelated commands {sorted(cmds)}: "
                            "no shared identifier links cause and effect")
        if c["confidence"] == "CONFIRMED":
            problems.append("causal claim marked CONFIRMED without a reproduced experiment")
    return problems

for c in LLM_OUTPUT:
    p = validate(c)
    verdict = "ACCEPT" if not p else "REJECT"
    print(f"{c['id']} {verdict:6s} {c['confidence']:9s} {c['claim']}")
    for x in p:
        print(f"      - {x}")

      - cites non-existent event E0021

C6 REJECT CONFIRMED Root cause: the UR caused the controller to stall cid 0x0042

      - causal claim marked CONFIRMED without a reproduced experiment

Reading the verdicts

  • C1 is a direct observation: CONFIRMED is correct.
  • C2 is a causal claim with linked evidence (the UR's request belongs to cid 0x0041) in causal order: HIGH is accepted.
  • C3 invents an event. The IOMMU fault is a good next step, but there is no such evidence in the capture. Correct form: an open question, "check the IOMMU fault log".
  • C4 asserts a field value that contradicts the decode — the classic invented field.
  • C5 is a root cause with no evidence at all.
  • C6 links events from two unrelated commands and calls the link CONFIRMED. Rephrased as a LOW hypothesis ("possibly related; no shared identifier; falsify by reproducing the UR without the stall") it would be acceptable.

The validator is valuable even without an LLM: run it over human-written reports too. Engineers also cite the wrong event or misremember a tag.

Validating an AI-assisted analysis system

Before trusting the system, measure it. Build an evaluation corpus of synthetic and real (approved) captures with known answers.

Evaluation metrics
MetricDefinitionWhy
Citation validityFraction of claims whose cited events and passages existDetects invention
Assertion accuracyFraction of asserted field values matching decodeDetects mis-reading
Claim support rateFraction of claims judged supported by reviewersDetects unsupported reasoning that passes mechanical checks
First-abnormal-event accuracyFraction of cases where the system's first event matches the known answerCore analytical skill
Abstention correctnessOn cases with insufficient evidence, fraction where the system abstainsDetects forced conclusions
Confidence calibrationFor each level, fraction later confirmedMakes confidence meaningful over time
StabilityAgreement across repeated runs on the same inputDetects non-determinism

Adversarial cases to include

  • A clean capture (correct answer: no abnormal event).
  • A capture with one direction missing (correct answer: absences unknown).
  • A loud kernel-log timeout with an earlier, quiet PCIe UR.
  • A user prompt asserting a wrong cause ("it's obviously the firmware").
  • Status codes that are command-specific (SCT=1h) for different opcodes.
  • Two unrelated errors close in time.
  • A capture longer than the model's context.

Executive Summary

A Write command (SQ1, CID 0x0041) completed with NVMe status Data Transfer Error (SCT 0h, SC 04h) at 53.0 µs [E0015]. The first abnormal event is an Unsupported Request completion at 3.95 µs [E0009] to the controller's Memory Read of that command's data buffer at IOVA 0x2_0000_1000 [E0008]. Best-supported hypothesis (HIGH): the host could not service the DMA read of the Write buffer, and the controller reported this as a Data Transfer Error. A Read (CID 0x0042) was fetched but not completed within the capture (MEDIUM). Recommended action: check the host IOMMU fault log and the buffer mapping for that IOVA.

Course lab system: Gen4 x4 (from link status in configuration dump), BAR0 0xFE60_0000, CAP.DSTRD 0 (register dump), I/O SQ1 at 0x1_2340_0000 and CQ1 at 0x1_2341_0000 with 1024 entries (driver queue setup), namespace 1 with 512-byte LBAs (Identify Namespace), MPS 256 B, MRRS 512 B, IOMMU enabled.

E0009, 3950 ns, PCIe Transaction Layer: completion status UR to a device-issued Memory Read. Expected: a successful completion with data (CplD). Observed: Cpl with status UR.

Errors

PCIe: E0009 (UR). NVMe: E0015 (SCT 0h, SC 04h, Data Transfer Error). Derived: no CQE for CID 0x42 by the 2 ms analysis deadline.

Evidence

See Timeline; decoded fields for E0008 (address 0x2_0000_1000, 128 DW, tag 0x13, requester 0100), E0009 (requester 0100, tag 0x13, status UR), E0015 (SQID 1, CID 0x0041, SCT 0, SC 04h).

Hypotheses

  1. HIGH — The Data Transfer Error on CID 0x41 results from the UR on its data read. Evidence E0008, E0009, E0015. Falsify: the same CQE status without an unsuccessful completion on that command's data. Next: IOMMU fault log for the IOVA.
  2. MEDIUM — CID 0x42 is stalled inside the controller. Evidence E0006, E0007, absence to 2.6 ms. Falsify: a late CQE in a longer capture, or a CQE on another CQ. Next: capture longer; read controller status and logs.
  3. LOW — The stall of CID 0x42 is related to the earlier UR. No shared identifier. Falsify: reproduce the UR without a stall, or the stall without a UR.

Root Cause

Not determined. The HIGH hypothesis localizes the failure to host-side handling of the DMA read; the reason (mapping, IOMMU permission, driver unmapping early) requires host evidence.

Recommendations

Collect IOMMU fault logs and driver DMA mapping traces for the failing I/O; rerun with a longer capture to resolve CID 0x42; review driver unmap timing for Write buffers.

Regression Tests

Write with buffers at page-crossing IOVAs under IOMMU; inject an unmapped page and confirm the expected UR + Data Transfer Error + host recovery; check that no later command stalls after a UR.

What a senior engineer notices

That the paragraph reads well and is almost entirely wrong. Fluency is not evidence. The one correct fact (a UR occurred) was wrapped in invented causes.

Failure variants

  • The model gives the right status name but cites the wrong event.
  • The model is correct but uses a percentage.
  • The model abstains on a case that the evidence actually resolves.

Failure patterns in AI-assisted analysis

Confident report, wrong layer

Symptom: the report blames the SSD; the fix later lands in the host driver. Possible causes: anchoring on the NVMe-visible symptom; the model did not see host evidence. Evidence: first abnormal event's requester and completer. First check: who generated the first abnormal event? Second check: what host logs were included? Experiment: rerun with host logs in retrieval. Likely conclusion: retrieval gap. Confidence: HIGH once the first abnormal event's origin is identified.

Plausible but wrong status names

Symptom: status names differ between reports for the same code. Cause: model naming from memory. Fix: names from lookup tables only; validator check. Confidence: CONFIRMED by comparing with the table.

Invisible truncation

Symptom: the analysis ignores an early event. Cause: the window started after it. Fix: always include the first abnormal event and state omitted ranges.

Agreement with the requester

Symptom: the assistant confirms whatever cause the user suggests. Cause: sycophancy. Fix: instruct to evaluate suggestions as hypotheses with falsification tests; test with adversarial prompts.

Unstable conclusions

Symptom: reruns produce different hypotheses. Cause: sampling; ambiguous evidence. Fix: deterministic layers carry the findings; report variation as uncertainty; record model version and inputs with each report.

Quiz

  1. Where should TLP decoding happen in an AI-assisted pipeline?

    • In deterministic code before the model sees anything
    • In the model, with a careful prompt
    • In both, and take the majority
    • In the report generator
    Answer and reasoning

    RAW TRACE → deterministic parser → normalized events → LLM reasoning. Decoding must be exact and testable.

  2. Which confidence level fits "the CQE for CID 0x41 reports SC 04h", read from a decoded event?

    • CONFIRMED
    • HIGH
    • MEDIUM
    • LOW
    Answer and reasoning

    Directly observed in decoded evidence.

  3. Why may a causal claim not be CONFIRMED from a trace alone?

    • Traces are always incomplete
    • The specification forbids it
    • Correlated, ordered events support causation but do not prove it; confirmation needs an experiment that isolates the cause
    • Because LLMs cannot reason causally
    Answer and reasoning

    Shared identifiers and order make causation HIGH; reproducing the effect by injecting the cause (and removing it) confirms it.

  4. A model writes "per the specification the device must retry". No passage was retrieved. What is wrong?

    Model answer

    Ungrounded specification claim: no cited passage, and possibly altered normative strength. Correct output: "not covered by retrieved specification text", and retrieve the relevant section.

  5. Explain EVIDENCE → CLAIM versus CLAIM → imagined evidence.

    Model answer

    Evidence-first reasoning derives a claim from events that exist. Claim-first reasoning starts with a conclusion and describes supporting evidence that was never observed. The second is how invented packets enter reports.

  6. Why is "HIGH (85 %)" misleading without calibration?

    Model answer

    The number implies a measured frequency that does not exist. Readers will treat it as a probability, compare and combine it. Use the ordinal level defined by evidence until calibration data exist.

  7. List three things retrieval should always include.

    Model answer

    The first abnormal event and its window; all events of the commands involved; system context with sources; cited specification passages; a statement of omitted ranges (any three).

  8. Name the report sections in order.

    Model answer

    Executive Summary, System Configuration, Capture Scope, Timeline, Key Transactions, First Abnormal Event, Errors, Evidence, Hypotheses, Root Cause, Recommendations, Regression Tests, Appendix.

  9. What does a claim validator check, and what can it not check?

    Model answer

    It checks that cited IDs exist, asserted fields match decoded values, confidence obeys rules, causal claims link events by shared identifiers in order, and names match lookup tables. It cannot judge whether the reasoning is the best explanation or whether evidence was cherry-picked; that needs human review.

Interview questions

  1. Intermediate: How would you stop an LLM from inventing packets in a trace summary?
    Model answer

    Give it only normalized events with IDs; require structured claims citing IDs; validate that IDs exist and fields match; generate numbers in the report from events, not model text.

  2. Advanced: How do you ground specification statements?
    Model answer

    Retrieve passages from a curated, revision-tagged corpus keyed by the findings; require S-ID citations; preserve normative keywords; reject claims without a cited passage.

  3. Advanced: Define your confidence levels and defend not using percentages.
    Model answer

    CONFIRMED direct observation or isolating experiment; HIGH linked independent evidence in causal order; MEDIUM consistent with alternatives open; LOW plausible, weak evidence; UNKNOWN insufficient. Percentages require calibration data; without it they are false precision.

  4. Senior: How would you evaluate whether an AI assistant improves your team's debugging?
    Model answer

    A labelled corpus including clean and adversarial cases; metrics for citation validity, assertion accuracy, first-abnormal-event accuracy, abstention correctness, calibration and stability; a controlled comparison of time-to-first-abnormal-event and error rates with and without the assistant; ongoing review of misses.

  5. Senior: Who owns the root cause in your architecture and why?
    Model answer

    The engineer. Root cause carries consequences (fixes, customer communication). The system provides evidence and hypotheses; a human with access to the full context decides and signs.
