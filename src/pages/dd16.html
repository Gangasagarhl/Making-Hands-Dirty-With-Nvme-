Chapter 15 taught what a Data Link Layer Packet (DLLP) is and how Ack/Nak replay repairs a damaged packet. This module goes underneath: the state each port keeps, the timers that decide when an Ack is late, the arithmetic that decides whether an error-free link is also a fast one, and the forensic skill of telling a sick channel from a sick protocol engine from a sick analyzer.

Orientation

This module deepens one mechanism of PCI Express (PCIe): the Data Link Layer's reliability loop — 12-bit sequence numbers, the 32-bit Link Cyclic Redundancy Check (LCRC), the replay buffer, Ack and Nak DLLPs, the replay timer, the AckNak latency timer and the 2-bit REPLAY_NUM counter. Here you treat the loop as a control system with latencies and capacities, read it in unfamiliar traces, and debug it from partial or contradictory evidence.

Be fluent with Chapter 15 — DLLP (DLLP format and types, receiver decisions, REPLAY_NUM rollover, the Data Link Layer states). Recovery comes from Chapter 8 — PCIe LTSSM; the Advanced Error Reporting (AER) bit map from Chapter 46 — AER. The wider layer picture is in Module 15 — PCIe Data Link Layer.

Next: flow-control DLLPs in Module 17, the error bits in Module 18 — AER, electrical causes in Chapter 54.

Kid story

Mia and Leo build a domino line across two rooms joined by a narrow doorway. Mia slides numbered dominoes through one at a time, and before each one she sticks a note with its number on her wall. Leo stands them up in order. Every so often he knocks and shouts "up to 41!", and Mia peels off notes 1 to 41. If a domino arrives chipped, Leo bins it and everything after it and shouts "only up to 40 — again!", and Mia slides copies of everything from 41 onwards, even the good ones.

Mia's wall has room for twenty notes. If Leo is slow to shout, the wall fills and Mia must wait although nothing is broken. If she hears nothing for a long time she resends anyway, in case his shout got lost. And if she resends the same batch four times without Leo saying anything new, she asks everyone to check the doorway.

Analogy boundary

What it represents. Numbered dominoes are TLPs with sequence numbers; wall notes are replay-buffer entries; "up to 41" is a cumulative Ack; "only up to 40 — again" is a Nak carrying 40; the full wall is a stalled transmitter; resending after silence is the replay timer; four fruitless resends are REPLAY_NUM rolling over; "check the doorway" is Recovery.

What it hides. Leo's shouts can be garbled and are never repeated on request — a bad DLLP is simply dropped. Leo also slides dominoes the other way through the same doorway, an independent loop. Leo is not the final destination; the next doorway numbers dominoes afresh. And Leo separately reports floor space — flow control — which can stop Mia even with an empty wall.

Where it misleads. It suggests the chipped domino is Mia's fault. In PCIe a Nak almost never means the transmitter built a bad packet; it means the channel corrupted it. It also suggests replay is slow; in hardware it starts within a few hundred nanoseconds.

Architecture

Each end of the link holds two half-machines: a transmit side owning sequence numbers and the replay buffer, and a receive side owning the expected number and the Ack/Nak decision. The two directions are independent loops sharing wires and DLLP slots.

Mechanism

Follow one 256-byte posted Memory Write (MWr) of NVMe read data from the Lab SSD to the Root Port through a corruption and a replay.

  1. Number, seal, store. Who: SSD transmit Data Link Layer. What changes: the TLP gets Seq 0x2B7, NEXT_TRANSMIT_SEQ becomes 0x2B8, a copy enters the replay buffer, the replay timer starts if idle. Where: SSD replay RAM. Evidence: analyzer shows Seq=0x2B7; software sees nothing.
  2. Corruption. Who: the channel. What changes: some symbols. Where: between SSD transmitter and Root Port receiver. Evidence: possibly a Receiver Error at the Root Port; an LCRC mismatch.
  3. Reject. Who: Root Port receiver. What changes: TLP discarded, Bad TLP set, NAK_SCHEDULED set, Nak 0x2B6 queued. Where: Root Port counters and AER. Evidence: Correctable Status bit 6 at the Root Port; Nak on the wire.
  4. Discard followers. Who: Root Port receiver. What changes: good TLPs 0x2B8 … are later than expected and discarded; no extra Nak. Evidence: clean TLPs that never earn an Ack.
  5. Purge and replay. Who: SSD transmitter. What changes: entries through 0x2B6 purged (progress, REPLAY_NUM to 0), then replay starts and REPLAY_NUM becomes 1; new TLPs are held back. Evidence: repeated sequence numbers on the wire.
  6. Accept. Who: Root Port receiver. What changes: 0x2B7 matches, is delivered once to the Transaction Layer, NAK_SCHEDULED clears; an Ack follows within the latency limit. Evidence: Ack ≥ 0x2B7.
  7. Retire. Who: SSD transmitter. What changes: buffer purged, ACKD_SEQ advanced, REPLAY_NUM 0, timer stopped if empty. Evidence: only the Ack, and at most a correctable error report from the Root Port.

The timers, quantitatively

The receiver promises to Ack within its AckNak latency limit; the transmitter waits a multiple of that before replaying. Early revisions of the PCI Express Base Specification wrote both limits as one formula — maximum payload plus TLP overhead, times an "Ack factor", divided by link width, plus internal delay — with the replay limit about three times the Ack limit plus L0s-exit allowances. Later revisions tabulate values per rate and width with a tolerance. Uncertain Constants and multipliers differ by revision; read the replay-timer and AckNak-latency material for yours.

Derived On the lab link a 256-byte MWr occupies 16 + 256 + 6 + 2 = 280 bytes ≈ 35.5 ns at ≈ 7.88 GB/s. A 4 KiB buffer therefore covers about 520 ns of Ack round trip; beyond that, throughput falls in proportion, with no error. With a 400 ns round trip each Nak re-sends about 12 TLPs, so one Nak per 1,000 TLPs costs about 1.2 % of bandwidth and one per 100 costs about 12 % — "only correctable errors".

The ladder

Number every domino, keep a note until your friend shouts, resend from the first chipped one, check the doorway after four fruitless resends.

Each port numbers TLPs, protects them with an LCRC, keeps copies until a cumulative Ack, replays on Nak or timeout, and retrains after four replays without progress.

Per direction per port: replay SRAM sized for bandwidth × Ack round trip, CRC-32 and CRC-16 engines, 12-bit counters, 2-bit REPLAY_NUM, two timers scaled by negotiated speed and width, wires into AER.

PCI Express Base Specification, Data Link Layer chapter: sequence and LCRC rules, receiver/transmitter rules, Ack/Nak formats, timer requirements, rollover → retrain, Data Link Protocol Error conditions; buffer size and coalescing left to implementations.

TLPs with Seq fields one way, Ack/Nak DLLPs the other, replayed TLPs with repeated numbers, TS1/TS2 ordered sets on rollover.

Analyzer: Ack latency per TLP, Nak positions, replay bursts, stalls with a full buffer, Recovery entries, speed changes.

Read AER on both ends, name the failing direction, correlate with speed, slot, temperature and power-state exits; reserve "protocol-engine bug" for what noise cannot produce: impossible numbers, altered replays, late Acks on a clean channel.

1What is it?A transmitter timer forcing a replay when no forward-progress Ack/Nak arrives in time.
2Why created?DLLPs are never retransmitted; a lost Nak or a TLP the receiver never framed would otherwise stall the link forever.

8Where does it live?Every transmitting port: Root Ports, switch ports, endpoints.

10What happens before it?A TLP sent while idle (start) or forward progress (restart).

Specification discipline

Applicable specification and revision. PCI Express Base Specification, Revision 6.x/7.0 — Data Link Layer Specification, with error classification in PCI Express Base Specification — Error Signaling and Logging and registers in PCI Express Base Specification — Advanced Error Reporting Extended Capability. It is a PCI-SIG members' document. No NVM Express document defines anything here; the NVMe over PCIe Transport Specification 1.4 assumes a reliable link. Everything before the Flit Mode note describes non-flit operation.

Normative 12-bit sequence numbers; 32-bit LCRC; Ack (00h) and Nak (10h) with a 12-bit AckNak_Seq_Num; cumulative acknowledgement; replay of all unacknowledged TLPs in order on Nak or timer expiry; 2-bit REPLAY_NUM whose rollover directs retraining; Bad TLP, Bad DLLP, Replay Timer Timeout and REPLAY_NUM Rollover as correctable errors; Data Link Protocol Error and Surprise Down as uncorrectable; bad DLLPs discarded, never retransmitted.

Informative The specification's figures and implementation notes are explanatory. This page's throughput bounds, replay-cost estimate and Ack-latency margin rule are derived from the normative rules, not quoted.

Optional / Implementation-dependent Replay-buffer size; Ack coalescing within the limit; use of nullification; scheduling among equal-priority choices; vendor replay counters; AER mask settings beyond defaults.

Version-dependent Presentation and values of the timer limits; DLLP types added over time (for example Data Link Feature and NOP); Flit Mode from 6.0, mandatory at 64.0 GT/s, where flits carry a flit CRC and forward error correction, acknowledgement and retry operate per flit, and the per-TLP sequence number and LCRC are not used. Details such as a selective-Nak option and the byte budget of a flit must be confirmed in the Flit Mode sections. Uncertain

The specification does not say how large a replay buffer must be, how many Naks per second are acceptable, what correctable-error rate is "healthy", or how analyzers label replays. It does not require ports to count replays. Check in your revision: the DLLP/TLP transmit priority table, the timer hold and reset rules during Recovery and L0s, and the full list of Data Link Protocol Error conditions.

Never assume that zero correctable errors means zero replays (masking, the other port, firmware-first); that the Ack came from the final destination; that a replay may differ from the original; that a Nak implicates the transmitter's silicon; or that classic-mode reasoning applies to Flit Mode.

Hardware view

Participation and observability
CategoryHardware
Must participateBoth ports' Data Link Layers (sequence counters, LCRC engines, replay RAM, timers, DLLP logic) and Physical Layers — independently in each direction.
May participateRetimers (Physical Layer only: never Ack, but add round-trip latency and take part in equalization, so a poor retimer result looks like a bad slot); switches (terminate the loop on one link and start a new one on the next); redrivers; interposers and probes (passive, but they load the channel).

Two hardware facts dominate debugging. First, an SSD's replay RAM is sized for an assumed Ack latency; behind a switch or retimer a tightly sized buffer loses throughput silently. Second, a TLP's chance of corruption grows with its length: on a channel with bit error rate p, a 280-byte data TLP fails about 280 × 8 × p of the time, fourteen times more often than a 20-byte doorbell. Derived "Doorbells work, data is slow" does not exonerate the link.

Link acknowledgement is not completion. The Root Port's Ack for the SSD's 64-byte Memory Read (MRd) of a Submission Queue Entry means only that the MRd crossed the link. The Completion with Data (CplD) carrying the entry comes later with its own sequence number in the other direction, and the SSD Acks that. Four Data Link Layer events frame one request/completion exchange.

Credits are not replayed. A TLP consumes flow-control credits once, when first handed down; replays re-send stored copies and consume none, and the receiver kept no buffer for discarded copies. Derived Replay and flow control can be analysed separately, but both make a transmitter go idle — see the ambiguous trace in Module 17.

Priority. Broadly, a port finishes the packet in progress, then favours a pending Nak, then due Acks and required flow-control updates, then replays, then new TLPs, then other DLLPs. Uncertain The exact table varies by revision. An Ack that appears only after a long TLP burst is an observation to note, not automatically a violation.

Analysis. Unlike Chapter 15's lost-Ack trace: the receiver-side bits (Receiver Error, Bad TLP) are at the SSD and the transmitter-side bits (Timeout, Rollover) at the Root Port, so the host-to-device direction is failing. """dllp_decode.py - decode non-flit DLLP bytes (1 type byte, 3 content bytes, 2 CRC bytes).
Teaching tool: it does NOT verify the 16-bit CRC; the sample CRC bytes are placeholders."""

FC_KINDS = {0x4: "InitFC1-P", 0x5: "InitFC1-NP", 0x6: "InitFC1-Cpl",
            0xC: "InitFC2-P", 0xD: "InitFC2-NP", 0xE: "InitFC2-Cpl",
            0x8: "UpdateFC-P", 0x9: "UpdateFC-NP", 0xA: "UpdateFC-Cpl"}
PM = {0x20: "PM_Enter_L1", 0x21: "PM_Enter_L23",
      0x23: "PM_Active_State_Request_L1", 0x24: "PM_Request_Ack"}


def decode(b):
    if len(b) != 6:
        raise ValueError("a DLLP is 6 bytes before Physical Layer framing")
    t = b[0]
    if t in (0x00, 0x10):
        seq = ((b[2] & 0x0F) << 8) | b[3]
        return f"{'Ack' if t == 0 else 'Nak'}: AckNak_Seq_Num=0x{seq:03X}"
    if (t >> 4) in FC_KINDS and not (t & 0x08):
        hdr_scale = b[1] >> 6
        hdr_fc = ((b[1] & 0x3F) << 2) | (b[2] >> 6)
        data_scale = (b[2] >> 4) & 0x3
        data_fc = ((b[2] & 0x0F) << 8) | b[3]
        return (f"{FC_KINDS[t >> 4]} VC{t & 7}: HdrScale={hdr_scale} HdrFC={hdr_fc} "
                f"DataScale={data_scale} DataFC={data_fc}")
    if t in PM:
        return PM[t]
    return f"type 0x{t:02X}: not in this table - check your revision's DLLP type table"


SAMPLES = [  # synthetic DLLPs from this module's traces
    "00 00 02 B8 00 00",
    "10 00 07 9F 00 00",
    "80 09 01 40 00 00",
    "50 04 00 10 00 00",
    "40 48 11 00 00 00",
    "02 00 00 00 00 00",
]

if __name__ == "__main__":
    for s in SAMPLES:
        print(f"{s}  ->  {decode(bytes.fromhex(s))}")

Procedure. Decode the first three samples by hand, run the script, then for each DLLP name the trace in this module it belongs to and the port that sent it. Finally encode an UpdateFC-P with HdrFC = 40 and DataFC = 0x200 by hand.

Observations. Ack and Nak differ only in the type byte; UpdateFC values are running totals.

Questions. (a) Why can the bytes alone not tell you the sender? (b) Why not decode unknown types as "reserved"?

Expected reasoning. DLLPs have no source field; direction comes from the capture side. Unknown types may be newer-revision types.

Solution

Ack 0x2B8 (Mechanism, sent by the Root Port); Nak 0x79F (Trace 3, sent by the SSD); UpdateFC-P VC0 HdrFC 36 DataFC 320 (sent by the receiver of posted TLPs). Encoding: byte1 = 0x28 >> 2 = 0x0A; byte2 = ((0x28 & 3) << 6) | (0x200 >> 8) = 0x02; byte3 = 0x00 → 80 0A 02 00. (b) Mislabelling hides real behaviour; flag it and check the revision.

Extension. Add a CRC-16 check from your revision's definition; validate it against real analyzer exports.

Lab 2 (intermediate) — Size the replay buffer

Safe lab Paper and calculator.

Objective. Quantify the replay bounds. Prerequisites. The Mechanism formulas. Setup. A calculator.

Procedure. For 256-byte MWrs (280 bytes of link time) compute: (1) the Ack round trip a 4 KiB buffer tolerates at Gen3 x4 (≈ 3.94 bytes/ns), Gen4 x4 (≈ 7.88) and Gen5 x4 (≈ 15.75); (2) payload throughput at Gen4 x4 with a 4 KiB buffer and 1,200 ns round trip; (3) the same with a Nak every 100 TLPs; (4) the buffer Gen5 x16 (≈ 63 bytes/ns) needs for a 400 ns round trip.

Observations. The tolerated round trip halves with each generation; Nak cost grows with the round trip.

Questions. Why does a retimer matter more at Gen5?

Expected reasoning. Bytes in flight = rate × time; each Nak re-sends one round trip of TLPs.

Solution

(1) 4096 ÷ 3.94 ≈ 1,040 ns; ÷ 7.88 ≈ 520 ns; ÷ 15.75 ≈ 260 ns. (2) 4096 B ÷ 1,200 ns ≈ 3.41 bytes/ns of link time × 256/280 ≈ 3.1 GB/s. (3) Each Nak re-sends ≈ 1,200 ÷ 35.5 + 1 ≈ 35 TLPs; 1 % Naks → η ≈ 0.65 → ≈ 2.0 GB/s. (4) ≈ 25 KB plus one maximum TLP. A retimer's added latency eats a larger fraction of a smaller tolerated round trip; behind a switch or retimer the SSD may lose throughput with no error.

Extension. Repeat for 128-byte MPS and explain both opposing effects.

Lab 3 (advanced) — Audit a capture's replay behaviour

Safe lab Synthetic event list.

Objective. Reconstruct the transmitter's hidden state from one direction of a capture and flag violations. Prerequisites. Labs 1–2; modular comparison. Setup. Python 3.

"""replay_audit.py - audit one direction of a synthetic non-flit capture from the
transmitter's point of view. Simulation/teaching tool; times in ns."""
MOD = 4096

def later(a, b):                       # 12-bit modular "a is later than b"
    return 0 < (a - b) % MOD < MOD // 2

EVENTS = [  # (time, kind, seq, payload)  TLPs from the transmitter, ACK/NAK from the peer
    (0, "TLP", 0x100, "A"), (36, "TLP", 0x101, "B"), (72, "TLP", 0x102, "C"),
    (180, "ACK", 0x101, None), (220, "NAK", 0x101, None),
    (260, "TLP", 0x102, "C"), (300, "TLP", 0x103, "D"), (420, "ACK", 0x103, None),
    (500, "TLP", 0x104, "E"), (536, "TLP", 0x105, "F"),
    (2600, "TLP", 0x104, "E"), (2636, "TLP", 0x105, "F*"),
    (2800, "ACK", 0x105, None), (3000, "ACK", 0x10A, None),
]

def audit(events):
    nts = ackd = None
    buf, replay_num, last = {}, 0, None
    for t, kind, seq, data in events:
        if kind == "TLP":
            if nts is None:
                nts, ackd = seq, (seq - 1) % MOD
            if seq == nts:                                   # new TLP
                buf[seq] = (data, t); nts = (nts + 1) % MOD
            elif seq in buf:                                 # replayed TLP
                oldest = min(buf, key=lambda s: (s - ackd) % MOD)
                if seq == oldest:                            # every replay starts here
                    replay_num += 1
                    cause = "Nak" if last == "NAK" else "timer (no Nak)"
                    print(f"{t:5} replay from 0x{seq:03X}, cause {cause}, REPLAY_NUM={replay_num % 4}")
                    if replay_num == 4:
                        print(f"{t:5} !! REPLAY_NUM rollover: expect Recovery"); replay_num = 0
                if buf[seq][0] != data:
                    print(f"{t:5} !! replay of 0x{seq:03X} differs from original")
        else:
            if later(seq, (nts - 1) % MOD):
                print(f"{t:5} !! {kind} 0x{seq:03X} covers an unsent TLP: Data Link Protocol Error")
            else:
                done = [s for s in buf if not later(s, seq)]
                for s in done:
                    print(f"{t:5} retire 0x{s:03X} after {t - buf.pop(s)[1]} ns")
                if done:
                    ackd, replay_num = seq, 0
        last = kind

if __name__ == "__main__":
    audit(EVENTS)

Procedure. Predict every output line; run; encode Trace 3 as events and confirm one Nak-driven replay, two timer-driven replays and a rollover; add a warning for a Nak arriving while a replay of the same range is still being sent.

Observations. Three planted problems: a timer-driven replay, an altered replay, an Ack for an unsent TLP.

Questions. Which problems are legal and which are violations? Give two explanations for the altered replay and how to separate them.

Expected reasoning. Timer replay is legal (correctable). Altered replay is a transmitter bug or capture error. The impossible Ack is a Data Link Protocol Error at the port receiving it.

Solution

Trace 3 events: TLP 0x79F, 0x7A0, 0x7A1; NAK 0x79F; TLP 0x7A0, 0x7A1; TLP 0x7A0 (×2, timer); TLP 0x7A0, 0x7A1 after Recovery; ACK 0x7A1 — the script reports REPLAY_NUM 1 (Nak), 2 and 3 (timer) and a rollover on the fourth replay. For the warning, remember the sequence number that ends the current replay and flag a Nak seen before it. Separate bug from capture error with a second capture point or by comparing the receiver's delivered data.

Extension. Feed it an analyzer CSV export and compute Ack-latency percentiles.

Lab 4 (senior forensic) — "The fleet's slow drives"

Safe lab Synthetic evidence. Real error injection or speed forcing is an Expert lab on test machines only.

Objective. A root-cause statement with confidence from partial evidence. Prerequisites. This module; Chapter 47 — Error Correlation.

Setup (synthetic evidence pack). 3 % of servers of one model show NVMe p99.9 read latency 4× the rest; same SSD and firmware everywhere. No NVMe errors. A few correctable AER reports per day, always the Root Port, decoded "Timeout". Root Port Correctable Status 0x0000_1000; SSD 0x0000_0000. All links 16 GT/s x4. ASPM L1 enabled only on affected servers (a BIOS difference). A 20 ms capture: no Naks, no DLLP CRC errors; SSD Ack latency 100–200 ns normally, 1.5–3 µs for the first Acks after each L1 exit.

Procedure. List hypotheses and what each predicts; choose one with confidence; propose a disambiguating experiment, a fix, and a three-sentence bug summary.

Questions. Why is the SSD's AER clean? Why so few kernel messages?

Solution

Lost Acks (device-to-host noise) would set Bad DLLP at the Root Port and show DLLP CRC errors — absent. Naks (host-to-device noise) would set Bad TLP at the SSD — absent. A slow SSD Ack after L1 exit predicts Timeout at the Root Port only, clean SSD AER, latency spikes after exits and correlation with ASPM — all observed. Sticky status and rate limiting hide the frequency. Conclusion: SSD Ack latency after L1 exit; confidence medium-high. Experiment: disable L1 for that slot on a lab server; latency tail and Timeout should vanish; repeat with another SSD model. Fix: SSD PCIe-core/firmware fix; interim L1 disable at a power cost. Summary: "With ASPM L1 enabled on platform X, SSD Y's first Ack after L1 exit takes 1.5–3 µs (normally 0.1–0.2 µs), causing Root Port Replay Timer Timeouts and a 4× p99.9 regression. The channel is clean (no Naks or DLLP CRC errors; SSD AER clean). Disabling L1 removes the symptom; vendor analysis requested against the AckNak latency requirement."

Extension. Write the regression test: cycle L1 every 100 µs under reads; pass if post-exit Ack latency stays well below the replay limit and no Timeout is logged in an hour.

Interview

Beginner

  1. What does Ack 0x123 tell the transmitter?

    Model answer

    The neighbour has every TLP through 0x123 intact and in order; those copies can be discarded.

  2. Who can read a replay buffer?

    Model answer

    Nobody outside the port. Its effects show only on the wire and through AER bits.

  3. Why does a Nak also acknowledge?

    Model answer

    Its number means "everything through N is good", so only what follows is replayed.

  4. Does an NVMe driver ever see a replay?

    Model answer

    No; at most slightly higher latency and a correctable AER report from one port.

  5. Does a retimer send Acks?

    Model answer

    No. It is Physical Layer only; it adds latency to the Ack round trip.

Intermediate

  1. A TLP is corrupted twice in a row. How many Naks?

    Model answer

    One. NAK_SCHEDULED stays set until the expected TLP arrives intact; the replay timer drives the next replay.

  2. Which errors does each end log?

    Model answer

    Receiver: Receiver Error, Bad TLP, Bad DLLP. Transmitter: Replay Timer Timeout, REPLAY_NUM Rollover. The port receiving an impossible DLLP logs Data Link Protocol Error.

  3. What resets REPLAY_NUM?

    Model answer

    Forward progress (an Ack/Nak retiring at least one TLP) or link down; not a repeated Ack.

  4. How do you measure Ack latency, and against what?

    Model answer

    From a TLP's end to the first Ack/Nak covering it; compare its distribution, especially after power-state exits, with the peer's replay limit for the negotiated configuration.

  5. What is a nullified TLP?

    Model answer

    A TLP cancelled with an inverted LCRC and end-bad marker, typically by a cut-through switch; silently discarded with no Nak or error.

Advanced

  1. Replay buffer for Gen5 x4 with a 500 ns round trip?

    Model answer

    ≈ 15.75 bytes/ns × 500 ns ≈ 7.9 KB plus one maximum TLP; less stalls the transmitter silently.

  2. One Nak per 200 TLPs: healthy?

    Model answer

    Correct but not healthy: each Nak re-sends a round trip of TLPs (several percent of bandwidth), adds tail latency and signals margin loss that will worsen.

  3. Why can replay and flow control be analysed separately, and where do they interact?

    Model answer

    Credits are consumed once; replays use stored copies. They interact through shared link time, lost UpdateFCs on a noisy link, and look-alike stalls.

  4. What changes in Flit Mode for replay debugging?

    Model answer

    Retry is per flit with flit CRC and FEC; no per-TLP LCRC or sequence number. FEC corrects many errors without retry, so "no retries" does not mean a clean channel; FEC and flit-retry statistics become the margin indicators.

  5. A replayed TLP differs from the original. Next steps?

    Model answer

    Rule out capture error (second probe, sync statistics, receiver's delivered data); if confirmed, escalate to the transmitter's IP vendor — replays must be identical.

Senior

  1. Outline a validation plan for an SSD's Data Link Layer.

    Model answer

    Exerciser: corrupt LCRCs (expect Nak, replay, Bad TLP), drop Acks briefly (timer replay within the specified limit at every speed/width/MPS) and persistently (rollover, Recovery, continued replay), inject duplicates, gaps, nullified TLPs and impossible Acks. Measure DUT Ack latency including after L0s/L1 exits; run full-rate traffic with worst-case Ack latency and with retimers in the path.

  2. "PCIe corrupted our data." Your response?

    Model answer

    Which hop, and how did it pass the LCRC? LCRC-detected corruption is never delivered; delivered corruption happened outside LCRC coverage (switch internals, device or host memory, IOMMU). Ask for ECRC and NVMe protection-information results and AER on every port.

  3. How would you alert on correctable Data Link Layer errors across a fleet?

    Model answer

    Baseline per platform, slot and model; alert on rate changes, on transmitter-side errors and on speed downgrades rather than absolute counts; account for sticky bits, rate limits and firmware-first platforms.

  4. Why is a slow-Acking device also a platform problem?

    Model answer

    Its impact depends on the peer's replay limit and buffer, ASPM policy and topology; the specification's latency requirement decides who must fix it.

  5. When do you accept "it's the analyzer"?

    Model answer

    Only with no protocol reaction, data verified end to end, and an anomaly that moves with the probe — at medium confidence, capture kept.

Quiz

  1. The SSD receives the same TLP corrupted twice. Which is true?

    • Two Naks
    • One Nak; the Root Port's timer drives the next replay
    • No Nak
    • Immediate retrain
    Answer and reasoning

    NAK_SCHEDULED suppresses further Naks.

  2. Root Port Correctable Status 0x0000_1100, SSD 0x0000_0041. Which direction fails?

    • Host-to-device
    • Device-to-host
    • Both equally
    • Undeterminable
    Answer and reasoning

    The SSD received bad TLPs; the Root Port's TLPs went unacknowledged.

  3. Which does not reset REPLAY_NUM?

    • Ack retiring two TLPs
    • Nak retiring one TLP
    • Ack repeating ACKD_SEQ
    • Link down
    Answer and reasoning

    Only progress or link down.

  4. A transmitter resumes exactly when an Ack arrives. The limit is:

    • Posted credits
    • Tags
    • MRRS
    • Replay buffer vs Ack latency
    Answer and reasoning

    Resumption on Ack, not on UpdateFC.

  5. A replayed TLP must be:

    • Identical, same sequence number
    • Renumbered
    • Re-credited
    • Given a new LCRC algorithm
    Answer and reasoning

    Replays re-send stored copies.

  6. A nullified TLP is reported as:

    • Bad TLP
    • Receiver Error
    • Nothing
    • Malformed TLP
    Answer and reasoning

    It is a deliberate cancellation.

  7. Open: Why are Acks safe to lose but TLPs are not?

    Model answer

    Acks are cumulative and backed by the replay timer; a TLP carries unique work and must be held until acknowledged.

  8. Open: In Trace 2, what rules out signal integrity?

    Model answer

    No Naks, no DLLP CRC errors, no Bad TLP/Bad DLLP; the replay is timer-driven and the late Ack arrives intact; stalls end on Acks.

Teach-back

To a child: the domino doorway, the wall that fills up, and why Mia resends dominoes nobody complained about.

Model explanation

"Mia keeps a note for every domino until Leo shouts how far he's got. A chipped one means she sends everything after the last good one again. If Leo is quiet too long she resends anyway in case his shout got lost. Her wall is small, so a slow Leo makes her wait even when nothing is broken."

To a junior engineer: how AER on both ends tells you which direction is corrupting packets.

Model explanation

"Receivers log what arrives bad: Receiver Error, Bad TLP, Bad DLLP. Transmitters log missing acknowledgements: Timeout and Rollover. Bad TLP at the SSD plus Timeout and Rollover at the Root Port means TLPs going down to the SSD are corrupted. Read both ends, record before clearing, and check whether firmware owns AER."

To a senior engineer: is a platform with 0.5 % Naks at Gen5 x4 and occasional timeouts shippable?

Model explanation

"Correctness holds, but each Nak re-sends a round trip of TLPs, so several percent of bandwidth and a visible tail are lost, and timeouts mean lost DLLPs or a slow-Acking peer. I need direction, lane localisation, unit spread, post-L1 Ack latency and the trend. A channel-margin problem needs a fix or a speed cap before shipping; a peer latency bug needs firmware plus an interim ASPM mitigation and fleet telemetry on transmitter-side errors."
