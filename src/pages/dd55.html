Kid story

Two friends stand at two bridges on the same river, each writing down every boat that passes, with the time from their own watch. Later they compare notebooks. The red boat with a white flag passed Ana at 10:00 and Ben at 10:07. The blue boat came back past Ben at 10:20 and Ana at 10:21. Their watches disagree, but by using one boat going down and one coming up, they work out both how wrong the watches are and how long the river takes between bridges.

One day Ana's notebook has a boat Ben never saw. Did it sink between the bridges? Or did Ben look away while eating his sandwich? Before they tell anyone a boat sank, they check: was Ben writing the whole time?

Analogy boundary

What it represents. The bridges are two probe points (above and below a PCIe switch); the notebooks are captures with independent timestamps; the boats are TLPs identified by unique fields (address, tag, requester); a boat going each way gives the clock offset and the transit time; Ben eating a sandwich is a capture gap, filter or lock loss.

What it hides. Boats on a river do not get copied. On a PCIe link a TLP can appear twice when the Data Link Layer replays it, and a switch builds new link-level framing on each side, so the sequence numbers differ between the two notebooks even for the same TLP. The river also has no rules about overtaking; PCIe ordering rules decide which TLPs may pass which.

Where it misleads. The story suggests that matching boats is easy. TLPs with identical headers (two writes of the same doorbell value, two reads of the same address with a reused tag) can be matched wrongly; you must choose anchors that are unique in the window. And "the watch is wrong by a fixed amount" ignores drift: over long captures, the offset changes.

The central concept on the ladder: the capture as evidence

Two friends' notebooks at two bridges. Before saying a boat sank, check that the second friend was watching the whole time.

A capture is evidence only within its scope: the probe point, the time window, the stored event types, and the decoder's assumptions. Conclusions must cite raw fields and state that scope.

Each probe recovers symbols on one link; each link has its own sequence numbers, Acks and credits; a switch re-frames every TLP it forwards. Absence on one side and presence on the other localises a fault to the element between them, if both captures were complete.

The PCI Express Base Specification defines the TLP, DLLP and ordering rules that a decode applies; NVMe specifications define queue structures. No specification defines analyzer behaviour; decodes, views and exports are implementation choices.

Requests and completions are paired by Requester ID and Tag; replays share a sequence number on one link; a TLP's header (type, address, requester, tag, length) is preserved across a switch while its link framing is not.

In a capture, line up Transaction Layer rows with the Data Link Layer rows around them and with the NVMe decode above them. Across captures, match on header fields and align clocks.

Before any device-fault conclusion, rule out analyzer artefacts: filters, gaps, lock loss, stale decoder configuration, replay duplicates and clock misalignment.

Aligning two clocks

θ = ((tB,d − tA,d) − (tA,u − tB,u)) / 2    δ = ((tB,d − tA,d) + (tA,u − tB,u)) / 2
θ
clock offset of analyzer B relative to analyzer A
δ
average one-way transit time between the probe points (assumes equal transit in both directions)
tA,d, tB,d
timestamps of one downstream anchor TLP at A and at B
tA,u, tB,u
timestamps of one upstream anchor TLP at A and at B

This is the same arithmetic network time protocols use. It is Derived, assumes symmetric transit and negligible drift between the anchors, and should be recomputed with fresh anchors across long captures. If your analyzers can share a time base or exchange trigger signals, prefer that and use anchors only as a check.

Specification discipline

Sources: PCI Express Base Specification, Revision 6.x/7.0 — Transaction Layer (TLP formats, completion rules, ordering), Data Link Layer (sequence numbers, Ack/Nak, replay), switch forwarding rules, Flit mode; NVM Express Base Specification, Revision 2.4 and NVMe over PCIe Transport Specification, Revision 1.4 for the structures a decoder reconstructs. Analyzer documentation is the source for the instrument's behaviour; it is not a specification of the protocol.

Retimers move the probe point. A retimer between the probe and the device re-transmits the signal and participates in link training. Errors seen by the device's receiver may never be visible at a probe on the other side of the retimer, and timing measured there includes the retimer's latency. Record retimers in the topology of every capture.

Probe transparency is a measurement, not a promise. Each interposer adds loss and length. At 16.0 GT/s and above, compare speed, width and correctable error counters with and without the probe before trusting a capture (Lab A on the training page).

Ordering inside a switch has a practical side effect: if a switch's shared ingress buffering holds a completion behind posted writes that are waiting for credits at a different egress port, the completion's transit grows even though its own path is idle. Whether that can happen depends on the switch's design, virtual channels and ordering attributes Implementation-dependent; the two-analyzer slow trace shows how to see it.

Why it is ambiguous. Two explanations fit. H1, Data Link Layer replay: the receiver Nak'd or did not Ack the first copy, the SSD replayed it with the same sequence number, and the receiver discarded the duplicate (or accepted only the replay); nothing is wrong at the NVMe level. H2, device bug: firmware wrote the same CQE twice; harmless here because it is the same slot and phase, but a sign of a completion-path bug. The evidence that separates them was not kept: DLLPs (a Nak naming the previous sequence number) and the TLP sequence numbers. The same address does not settle it: a replay and a firmware rewrite both target the same slot. Disambiguation. Re-open the raw packet view and read the framing sequence numbers if the analyzer stored them (equal means replay); otherwise recapture with DLLPs. Check correctable-error counters (Bad TLP, Bad DLLP, Replay Timer Timeout) at the root port and SSD for the same moment. Intermediate lab below shows a replay identified by sequence number and removed before NVMe reconstruction. Likely conclusion when DLLPs are present and show a Nak: H1, high confidence. Lesson: filters chosen to extend a window can delete the very evidence an anomaly needs.

Labs

Beginner lab: write a capture plan

Objective. Turn a symptom into a capture plan that another engineer could execute. Safe lab (paper).

Prerequisites. Capture, Triggers and Filters on the training page.

Setup. Symptom: "Once every few hours, an NVMe flush on the SSD behind the switch takes 300 ms. No errors logged." Gen4 x4 link at about 60% utilisation.

Procedure. Fill in: question; events and layers; probe points; trigger (expressed generically); pre-trigger share; filters (with what each would make impossible to see); metadata to record; how you will anchor host and analyzer clocks.

Observations. Note which parts of your plan depend on features your analyzer may not have.

Questions. (a) Why can a capture filter not be used to keep "only Flush commands"? (b) Where should the trigger position be?

Expected reasoning. The symptom is a long interval; trigger on its end, keep the beginning.

Solution

Question: "Where do the 300 ms go between Flush submission and its CQE?" Events: SQ doorbells, SQE fetches (to find Flush, opcode 00h), all device traffic during the interval (writes from other commands, power-state hints), CQE and MSI-X, DLLPs and link state (Recovery, L1). Trigger: "CQE for the Flush's queue arriving more than 100 ms after an SQE fetch whose opcode is 00h" if expressible; otherwise trigger on any CQE gap longer than 100 ms on that queue, with 95% pre-trigger. (a) Opcode lives in the SQE payload inside a completion; a hardware filter on "Flush only" would have to keep fetches and completions anyway, and removing other traffic deletes the context (what else the device was doing). (b) Late: the cause is before the symptom. Anchor with a marker I/O. Record probe type, software version, settings export.

Extension. Estimate the capture window with the training page's formula for a 4 GB buffer and decide whether a 300 ms interval fits.

BAR0, SQ1, CQ1 = 0xFE60_0000, 0x1_2340_0000, 0x1_2341_0000

def load(text):
    rows = []
    for r in csv.DictReader(io.StringIO(text)):
        r["time_ns"] = int(r["time_ns"])
        r["address"] = int(r["address"], 16) if r["address"] else None
        r["payload"] = bytes.fromhex(r["payload"]) if r["payload"] else b""
        rows.append(r)
    return rows

def drop_replays(rows):
    seen, kept = {}, []
    for r in rows:
        if r["layer"] == "TL":
            key = (r["dir"], r["seq"])
            if key in seen and seen[key] == (r["type"], r["address"], r["payload"]):
                print(f"  row {r['idx']}: replay of seq {r['seq']} ({r['type']}) - not a new transaction")
                continue
            seen[key] = (r["type"], r["address"], r["payload"])
        kept.append(r)
    return kept

def rebuild(rows):
    cmds, slot_of_tag = {}, {}
    for r in rows:
        t, a, p = r["type"], r["address"], r["payload"]
        if t == "MWr32" and a == BAR0 + 0x1008:
            print(f"  {r['time_ns']:>6} ns  SQ1 tail doorbell = 0x{int.from_bytes(p, 'little'):X}")
        elif t == "MRd64" and SQ1 <= a < SQ1 + 0x10000:
            slot_of_tag[r["tag"]] = (a - SQ1) // 64
        elif t == "CplD" and r["tag"] in slot_of_tag:
            opc, cid = p[0], int.from_bytes(p[2:4], "little")
            cmds[cid] = {"opcode": opc, "fetched": r["time_ns"]}
            print(f"  {r['time_ns']:>6} ns  SQE slot 0x{slot_of_tag[r['tag']]:X}: opcode {opc:02X}h CID 0x{cid:04X}")
        elif t == "MWr64" and CQ1 <= a < CQ1 + 0x4000 and len(p) == 16:
            cid, sf = int.from_bytes(p[12:14], "little"), int.from_bytes(p[14:16], "little")
            state = "DUPLICATE CQE" if cmds.get(cid, {}).get("cqe") else "CQE"
            cmds.setdefault(cid, {})["cqe"] = r["time_ns"]
            print(f"  {r['time_ns']:>6} ns  {state} CID 0x{cid:04X} slot 0x{(a - CQ1) // 16:X} "
                  f"phase {sf & 1} status 0x{sf >> 1:X}")
        elif t == "MWr32" and a == 0xFEE0_1000:
            print(f"  {r['time_ns']:>6} ns  MSI-X vector data 0x{p[0]:02X}")
        elif t == "MWr32" and a == BAR0 + 0x100C:
            print(f"  {r['time_ns']:>6} ns  CQ1 head doorbell = 0x{int.from_bytes(p, 'little'):X}")
    return cmds

Solution

(b) Merging exports from two links (sequence numbers are per link), or sequence-number wrap in a long capture (12-bit space in non-Flit mode); the filter must be per link and per window. Advanced lab: two analyzers, one switch

Objective. Align two captures and localise delay and loss. Safe lab (paper with the formula; optional hardware variant is Caution lab because it requires installing interposers).

Prerequisites. The clock-alignment formula; the normal, slow and failed traces above.

Setup. Anchors (synthetic): downstream doorbell at A 12 000, B 16 210; upstream MRd at A 12 950, B 16 860. Later events: CplD Tag 0x18 at A 20 400 and B 26 900; MRd Tag 0x22 at B 30 000, not found on A.

Procedure. Compute θ and δ; convert B times to A's clock; compute transit for the CplD; decide what to check before calling the MRd lost.

Observations. One transit is far above δ.

Questions. (a) θ and δ? (b) CplD transit? (c) Steps before concluding the MRd was lost?

Expected reasoning. Use both directions; audit before asserting absence.

Solution

(a) Downstream difference 4 210, upstream (A − B) = −3 910; θ = (4 210 + 3 910) / 2 = 4 060 ns; δ = (4 210 − 3 910) / 2 = 150 ns. (b) B 26 900 → A clock 22 840; transit 2 440 ns, about 16 × δ: queueing inside the switch. (c) Check A's lock and error indicators, segment boundaries, capture filters and storage overruns around A 25 940 (B 30 000 − θ); check A-link sequence continuity; check switch AER; only then state "lost in the switch". Recompute θ with a later anchor to rule out drift.

Extension. Write a script that finds candidate anchors automatically (unique address-and-tag pairs present in both exports) and estimates drift from several of them.

Senior forensic lab: the vendor dispute

Objective. Reconcile two captures that support opposite conclusions, and write the joint finding. Safe lab (paper exercise on synthetic evidence).

Prerequisites. This module; Analyzer limitations.

Setup: evidence (synthetic).

  • Your capture (probe at the root port side, Analyzer A position): the host writes SQ1 tail doorbells to 0xFE60_1008 normally before the I/O timeout.
  • SSD vendor's capture (probe at the SSD, behind a retimer, their own analyzer and decode project): their NVMe view shows no doorbells for 40 seconds before the timeout; they conclude "host stopped submitting".
  • Vendor's settings: capture filter "NVMe traffic only (register and queue accesses)"; decoder BAR0 configured as 0xFE50_0000, saved from an earlier session on a different platform.
  • lspci on the test system: Region 0 at 0xFE60_0000. The platform re-enumerated after a BIOS update between sessions.

Procedure. Explain the contradiction; state which conclusions each capture supports; propose the joint next step; write the limitations paragraph.

Questions. (a) Why did the vendor's capture show no doorbells? (b) Is your capture alone enough to blame the SSD? (c) What does the joint report say?

Expected reasoning. A filter defined in decoded terms inherits the decoder's mistakes.

Solution

(a) The filter kept only what the decoder recognised as NVMe register accesses; with BAR0 configured at 0xFE50_0000, writes to 0xFE60_1008 were not recognised and were never stored. The absence is an artefact. (b) No: it shows doorbells left the root port side, not that the SSD received them (a retimer and link lie between). It does show the host did not stop submitting. (c) "Doorbells were sent (capture A, raw MWr32 to 0xFE60_1008). Capture B cannot support 'no doorbells' because its capture filter depended on a stale BAR configuration. Next: recapture at the SSD with no capture filter and the decoder configured from the current enumeration; check SSD-side correctable errors and retimer status; then evaluate whether the SSD fetched the SQEs." Limitations: probe points, retimer, software versions, filters, clock alignment method.

Extension. Draft a shared capture procedure for both teams: settings checklist, decoder-configuration verification against lspci, mandatory raw packet export alongside any decoded view.

Interview

Beginner

  1. What must a capture plan contain?

    Model answer

    The question, required events and layers, probe point, trigger and pre-trigger share, filters with their consequences, metadata and anchoring method.

  2. Why do sequence numbers differ for the same TLP on both sides of a switch?

    Model answer

    Each link has its own Data Link Layer; the switch re-frames the TLP with its own sequence number.

  3. What is a replay duplicate?

    Model answer

    A TLP retransmitted with the same sequence number after a Nak or replay timeout; not a new transaction.

  4. Why keep the original trace file unmodified?

    Model answer

    It is the evidence; exports and annotations are derived and must be reproducible from it.

  5. What makes a good anchor event?

    Model answer

    Uniqueness in the window, visibility in every capture or log being aligned, and a known relationship in time (same TLP, or a marker I/O).

Intermediate

  1. How do you compute clock offset and transit from two anchors?

    Model answer

    Take one downstream and one upstream anchor; offset is half the difference of the two one-way differences, transit is half their sum, assuming symmetric delay.

  2. Why is a filter defined in decoded terms risky?

    Model answer

    It inherits the decoder's configuration errors; anything misdecoded is never stored.

  3. What evidence separates a replay from a duplicate CQE written by firmware?

    Model answer

    Equal Data Link Layer sequence numbers and a Nak or replay-timer event identify a replay; different sequence numbers mean two transactions.

  4. How do retimers affect what an analyzer can see?

    Model answer

    They re-drive the signal and participate in training; errors on one side may be invisible on the other, and timing includes their latency.

  5. What belongs in a normalised export schema?

    Model answer

    Time, direction, link, layer, type, requester, tag, address, length, sequence, status, payload prefix, plus capture metadata.

Advanced

  1. A TLP is on B but not A. List what you check before saying it was lost.

    Model answer

    A's lock and overrun indicators, segment boundaries, capture filters, sequence continuity on A's link, clock alignment, and the switch's AER registers.

  2. How would you detect drift between analyzers?

    Model answer

    Compute offsets from anchors at the start and end of the window; a changing offset indicates drift; interpolate or use a shared time base.

  3. Why can completion transit through a switch grow while the destination link is idle?

    Model answer

    Internal queueing or head-of-line blocking behind traffic for other ports, depending on switch buffering, virtual channels and ordering attributes.

  4. How do you keep an NVMe decode valid across a controller reset in one capture?

    Model answer

    Re-learn queue bases from the capture (ASQ/ACQ writes, Create I/O queue commands) after the reset, or split the analysis at the reset with separate configurations.

  5. What does IDE change for capture analysis?

    Model answer

    Payloads (SQEs, CQEs, data) are encrypted; without keys only framing and some header information is visible, so NVMe decoding is limited.

Senior

  1. Two teams' captures disagree. How do you run the meeting?

    Model answer

    Compare scope first (probe points, filters, decode settings, versions), then raw fields at anchors; agree on what each capture can support; design a joint recapture.

  2. What is your standard for "the device did X" in a report?

    Model answer

    Raw field citations, decode configuration stated, capture audited for the window, competing hypotheses addressed, corroboration from a second source where possible.

  3. How would you institutionalise capture quality?

    Model answer

    A capture procedure with checklists, mandatory settings export, raw packet export, probe transparency checks, and a review of every report's limitations section.

  4. When is a second analyzer worth the cost?

    Model answer

    When an element between endpoints (switch, retimer, bridge) is a suspect, or when ownership disputes need bracketing evidence.

  5. How do you avoid vendor-specific blind spots?

    Model answer

    Translate vendor annotations into specification terms, verify surprising decodes at packet level, and keep analysis tooling on normalised exports rather than one product's views.

Quiz

  1. Same TLP seen on both sides of a switch. Which field differs?

    • Address
    • Requester ID
    • Data Link Layer sequence number
    • Tag
    Answer and reasoning

    Link framing is per link; the header is forwarded unchanged.

  2. A Nak carries sequence number 0x1B7. The replay starts at:

    • 0x1B6
    • 0x1B8
    • 0x1B7
    • 0x000
    Answer and reasoning

    The Nak names the last good TLP; replay resends from the next.

  3. Anchors: B − A = 4 180 ns downstream; A − B = −3 880 ns upstream. Transit:

    • 150 ns
    • 4 030 ns
    • 300 ns
    • 8 060 ns
    Answer and reasoning

    (4 180 − 3 880) / 2 = 150 ns.

  4. A filter "NVMe traffic only" hides doorbells. Most likely:

    • Host stopped
    • Link down
    • IDE enabled
    • Decoder BAR configuration stale
    Answer and reasoning

    The filter depends on what the decoder recognises.

  5. Duplicate CQE, DLLPs not captured. Correct statement:

    • Device bug proven
    • Undetermined; replay not excluded
    • Replay proven
    • Host bug
    Answer and reasoning

    The separating evidence was filtered out.

  6. Why audit capture A before declaring a TLP lost in a switch?

    • To compute θ
    • To find the tag
    • Absence may be a capture artefact
    • To decode NVMe
    Answer and reasoning

    Gaps, filters and lock loss create false absences.

  7. Which element can hide receiver errors from a probe on the other side?

    • A retimer
    • An IOMMU
    • MSI-X
    • The NVMe driver
    Answer and reasoning

    It terminates and re-drives the electrical link.

  8. Open: why must replay filtering be per link?

    Model answer

    Sequence numbers are per link and direction; merging links would match unrelated TLPs.

  9. Open: what goes in a capture report's limitations section?

    Model answer

    Probe points and retimers, filters and payload truncation, gaps, analyzer and software versions, decode configuration, clock alignment method and residual uncertainty.

Teach-back

  1. To a child: explain the two notebooks and the boat that "sank".

    Model explanation

    "We each write down every boat. If one friend has a boat the other doesn't, maybe it sank, or maybe the other friend wasn't looking. We check that first."

  2. To a junior engineer: explain how to tell a replayed CQE from a duplicate CQE.

    Model explanation

    "Look at the Data Link Layer sequence numbers on that link. Same number, with a Nak or replay timeout nearby, means the link resent it and the receiver discarded the copy. Different numbers mean the device really sent two. Keep DLLPs in the capture or you cannot tell."

  3. To a senior engineer: argue why decoded-term capture filters should be banned in dispute investigations.

    Model explanation

    "They make storage depend on decode configuration, which is often stale. When they are wrong, evidence is never stored and cannot be recovered. For disputes, capture raw, decode afterwards, and state the configuration."
