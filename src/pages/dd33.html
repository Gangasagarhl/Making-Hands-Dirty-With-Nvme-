Dataset Management with Deallocate is the command that tells a solid-state drive what it may forget. Chapter 35 taught its bytes and its effect on garbage collection. This module follows deallocation into the places where real systems get it wrong: range lists on the wire, discard policies that cause latency storms, error-on-read semantics, mapping granularity that silently ignores small ranges, and storage layers that never pass the information down at all.

Orientation

Chapter 35 (Dataset Management) covered the Non-Volatile Memory Express (NVMe) Dataset Management (DSM) command, opcode 09h: Number of Ranges (NR, 0's based), the attributes Deallocate (AD), Integral Dataset for Write (IDW) and Integral Dataset for Read (IDR), the 16-byte range entries, Deallocate Logical Block Features (DLFEAT), the preferred granularity and alignment (NPDG/NPDA), the write-amplification model, and three traces. This module goes deeper on four fronts: the range list as a data transfer (alignment, page crossings, limits), discard policy (continuous, batched, huge) and its latency cost, read semantics after deallocation including the Deallocated or Unwritten Logical Block Error (DULBE) behaviour and granularity effects, and end-to-end pass-through of discards through filesystems, device-mapper and virtualisation.

Read first: Dataset Management (command format, DLFEAT, write amplification), WRITE (the range list moves like write data), Physical Region Pages (the list's data pointer), FLUSH (durability of deallocation).

Where it goes next: Deep dive: PRP and Deep dive: SGL (how the list is described), Deep dive: ZNS (zoned namespaces reset zones instead of deallocating LBAs), Deep dive: Performance Engineering.

Kid story

A big toy library lends toys in boxes. Each shelf holds four box-spots that are cleaned together, a whole shelf at a time. When you no longer want a toy, you post a card: "I don't need spots 12 to 19 any more." The librarian crosses them off, and when she cleans a shelf she doesn't have to move crossed-off toys.

Three surprises. If your card says "spot 13 only", she cannot clean a quarter of a shelf, so she may just leave the toy there; ask for spot 13 later and the old toy might still be in it. If you post ten thousand tiny cards one after another, she spends the whole afternoon reading cards instead of lending toys. And if your big brother collects your cards "to post later" and forgets, the librarian never hears about them at all.

Analogy boundary

What it represents. Cards are DSM commands with range lists; box-spots are logical blocks; a shelf is the controller's mapping unit (often reported as NPDG); crossing off is unmapping in the FTL; cleaning a shelf is garbage collection; the big brother is any layer between filesystem and drive (device-mapper, encryption, hypervisor) that may not forward discards.

What it hides. The card must itself be fetched over PCIe like write data; an unaligned card costs extra read requests. The librarian can also be told to answer "that spot is empty" with an error instead of a blank toy (DULBE). And the crossing-off list lives in volatile memory for a while: a power cut can undo it unless it is flushed.

Where it becomes misleading. Crossed-off toys are not destroyed; they remain physically on the shelf until it is cleaned — deallocation is not erasure. And "the old toy might still be there" is not a broken promise if the device reports that it does not guarantee zeros (DLFEAT 000b) or if the range was smaller than its unit; it is a broken promise only when the device claims deterministic zeros for blocks it actually deallocated.

Mechanism

fstrim on a filesystem over namespace 1 finds 256 free extents and the block layer issues one DSM.

  1. Build the list. Who: NVMe driver (or user-space tool). What: 256 × 16 bytes = 4 KiB of (context, Length, Starting LBA) entries. Where: a host buffer, here at IOVA 0x2_0002_0800 (not page-aligned). Evidence: nvme_setup_cmd shows nvme_cmd_dsm with nr and attributes, not the ranges.
  2. Describe the list. Who: driver. What: the 4 KiB list at offset 0x800 touches two pages, so PRP1 = 0x2_0002_0800 and PRP2 = second page 0x2_0007_4000. Where: SQE Dwords 6–9. Evidence: SQE bytes in a capture.
  3. Submit. Who: driver. What: SQE (opcode 09h, NSID 1, CDW10 = 0x000000FF, CDW11 = 0x00000004) in SQ1 slot 0x60; doorbell 0x61. Where: SQ1 ring and BAR0 + 0x1008. Evidence: Memory Write (MWr) doorbell.
  4. Fetch and validate. Who: controller. What: Memory Read (MRd) of the SQE; checks ONCS support, NR against DMRL. Where: controller. Evidence: SQE CplD; an immediate error CQE if NR exceeds DMRL.
  5. Pull the list. Who: controller DMA. What: 8 MRds of 512 bytes (4 in each page, Max Read Request Size (MRRS) 512), answered by CplDs. Where: link. Evidence: MRd addresses at PRP1 and PRP2; CplD payloads are the ranges.
  6. Validate ranges. Who: controller. What: each range against Namespace Size (NSZE), DMRSL, total against DMSL. Where: controller. Evidence: error CQE (LBA Out of Range, Invalid Field) with no partial-progress indication.
  7. Unmap. Who: FTL. What: whole mapping units inside ranges are unmapped; partial units may be ignored, tracked, or zero-filled internally; large ranges may be journaled for lazy processing. Where: device DRAM map and NAND journal. Evidence: none directly; NUSE may drop; later reads.
  8. Complete. Who: completion engine. What: CQE status 0 in CQ1 slot 0x60, MSI-X. Where: host. Evidence: CQE MWr.
  9. Aftermath. Who: garbage collector and readers. What: cheaper reclaim; reads return DLFEAT data, old data (not deallocated), or a DULBE error if enabled. Evidence: write latency at steady state; read results.

The central concept on the ladder: effective deallocation

The central idea of this module is not "the command" but whether deallocation is effective: whether the information reaches the FTL, at a granularity it can act on, without harming latency, and with read-back semantics the host understands.

Cards only help if they arrive, are big enough to clear a whole shelf-spot set, and are not so many that the librarian stops lending.

Discards must pass every layer, be aligned to the drive's preferred granularity, be batched sensibly, and be followed by reads whose result the host expects (DLFEAT or DULBE).

The FTL frees whole mapping units; partial units stay mapped or are tracked at extra cost; the map update is journaled and may be lazy; the list is fetched by DMA like write data.

NVM Express NVM Command Set Specification, Revision 1.3 — Dataset Management command; Identify Namespace (DLFEAT, NPDG, NPDA, NUSE); I/O Command Set specific Identify Controller (DMRL, DMRSL, DMSL); NVM Express Base Specification, Revision 2.4 — ONCS; Error Recovery feature (DULBE).

A DSM moves its list host-to-device with MRd/CplD; one CQE answers all ranges; no per-range status.

Opcode byte 09h, CDW11 bit 2 set, list MRds totalling (NR+1)×16 bytes, ranges in CplD payloads; absence of DSM in the device trace when the filesystem claims it trimmed.

"Trim didn't help": prove DSMs reach the device and match NPDG. "Latency storm": count DSMs per second and range sizes. "Reads fail after trim": check DULBE. "Old data after trim": check granularity and DLFEAT before blaming the device.

The specification does not say that deallocation erases data; that a DSM completes quickly; how much a sub-granular range is honoured; how DSM is ordered with concurrent reads or writes of the same LBAs; or whether a failed DSM processed some ranges. Check in the specification: the exact Error Recovery feature bit for DULBE and the Identify Namespace bit that reports DULBE support; the status code value for "Deallocated or Unwritten Logical Block" in the Media and Data Integrity Errors table; the Write Zeroes Deallocate bit position; whether a range Length of zero is permitted.

Never assume a discard reached the drive because a tool reported bytes trimmed; never rely on DSM to produce zeros (use Write Zeroes); never deallocate as a security measure (use Sanitize or a cryptographic erase); never issue DSM concurrently with I/O to the same LBAs.

High-density drives often map in units larger than 4 KiB (for example 16 KiB) to shrink their mapping tables. On such a drive a filesystem freeing scattered 4 KiB blocks may produce DSMs that free nothing, until three neighbouring blocks in the same unit are also freed. NPDG is the drive's way to say so; a host that ignores it sends work that has no effect Implementation-dependent.

Ordering and errors

  • No ordering with I/O. A Read racing a DSM of the same LBA may return old data or the deallocated value; a Write racing it may be lost if the unmap is applied after it. Filesystems never discard blocks they might reuse until the discard completes; custom software must do the same.
  • Error granularity. One status for the whole list; the host cannot know which ranges were processed. Because deallocation is idempotent, the safe retry is to resubmit only valid ranges.
  • Typical statuses. Success; Invalid Field in Command (for example NR beyond DMRL, attribute problems — confirm which status your revision mandates for each); LBA Out of Range; Invalid Opcode if unsupported; Data Transfer Error if the list pull fails.
  • Reads after deallocation. With DULBE enabled, a Read of a deallocated block completes with an error in the Media and Data Integrity Errors family and transfers no data for the command.

Labs

Beginner lab — encode a deallocation by hand

Safe lab Paper or Python prompt.

Objective
Turn byte extents into a correct DSM command and list.
Prerequisites
Chapter 35 command format.
Setup
Namespace 1 (512-byte LBAs), NPDG = NPDA = 8. Free extents (byte offsets on the namespace): [0x0100_0000, +64 KiB), [0x0300_0400, +3 KiB), [0x7_0000_0000, +1 MiB).
Procedure
Compute SLBA and Length for each; write CDW10 and CDW11; write the 48-byte list as little-endian Dwords; mark ranges that violate NPDG/NPDA.
Observations
Note which range a careful host would trim or round.
Questions
(1) Why is NR 2 and not 3? (2) What happens to the misaligned range on a drive that ignores sub-unit requests? (3) How many bytes will the controller fetch for the list?
Expected reasoning
Byte offset ÷ 512 = LBA; Length is a count; NR is 0's based.
Solution

Range 0: SLBA 0x0000_8000, Length 128. Range 1: 0x0300_0400 / 512 = SLBA 0x0001_8002, Length 6 — not 8-aligned and not a multiple of 8: a careful host drops it or waits until the surrounding unit is free. Range 2: 0x7_0000_0000 / 512 = SLBA 0x0380_0000, Length 2048. CDW10 = 0x0000_0002 (three ranges), CDW11 = 0x0000_0004 (AD). List Dwords: (0, 0x80, 0x8000, 0), (0, 6, 0x18002, 0), (0, 0x800, 0x3800000, 0). (1) NR is 0's based. (2) Those 3 KiB stay mapped; reads return old data; garbage collection keeps copying them. (3) 48 bytes, one MRd.

Extension. Recompute for namespace 2 (4 KiB LBAs, NPDG 4): which extents can be expressed at all?

Intermediate lab — a range-list auditor

Safe lab Python 3, standard library.

Objective
Decode range lists as seen in CplD payloads and flag host mistakes before they reach a device.
Prerequisites
Beginner lab.
Setup
Save as dsm_ranges.py.
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""dsm_ranges.py - decode and audit a Dataset Management range list.

Synthetic teaching model (standard library only). Feed it the CDW10 value (NR, 0's based)
and the bytes the controller fetched for the range list (as hex, little-endian, as the
analyzer shows them). It decodes every 16-byte range and reports problems a host should
never send: wrong list size, out-of-range LBAs, overlaps, and ranges the controller may
not be able to deallocate because they ignore the preferred granularity/alignment.
"""
import struct

NSZE = 0x7470_6DB0          # lab namespace 1, 512-byte LBAs
NPDG, NPDA = 8, 8           # preferred deallocate granularity/alignment in LBAs (synthetic)

def decode(cdw10, payload_hex):
    nr = (cdw10 & 0xFF) + 1
    raw = bytes.fromhex(payload_hex.replace(" ", ""))
    out = []
    if len(raw) != nr * 16:
        out.append(f"list is {len(raw)} B but NR={nr - 1} (0's based) needs {nr * 16} B")
    ranges = []
    for i in range(min(nr, len(raw) // 16)):
        ctx, length, slba = struct.unpack_from("<IIQ", raw, i * 16)
        ranges.append((slba, length))
        last = slba + length - 1
        note = []
        if length == 0:
            note.append("Length 0 (check how your revision treats it)")
        elif last >= NSZE:
            note.append(f"ends at 0x{last:X} beyond NSZE-1 = 0x{NSZE - 1:X} -> LBA Out of Range")
        if length and (slba % NPDA or length % NPDG):
            note.append("not NPDG/NPDA aligned: may be only partly deallocated")
        if ctx:
            note.append(f"context attributes 0x{ctx:08X} (hints)")
        out.append(f"range {i}: SLBA 0x{slba:X} Length {length} LBAs ({length * 512} B)"
                   + ("; " + "; ".join(note) if note else ""))
    spans = sorted((s, s + n) for s, n in ranges if n)
    for (a0, a1), (b0, b1) in zip(spans, spans[1:]):
        if b0 < a1:
            out.append(f"overlap: [0x{a0:X},0x{a1:X}) and [0x{b0:X},0x{b1:X})")
    return out

if __name__ == "__main__":
    payload = ("00000000 08000000 00100000 00000000"     # ctx 0, Length 8, SLBA 0x1000
               "00000000 06000000 03200000 00000000"     # Length 6 at SLBA 0x2003
               "00000000 00080000 00090000 00000000"     # Length 0x800 at SLBA 0x900 (!)
               "00000000 10000000 00040000 00000000")    # Length 16 at SLBA 0x400
    for line in decode(0x00000003, payload):
        print(line)
Procedure
(1) Run it. (2) Explain why range 2 is at SLBA 0x900 although its author meant 0x9000. (3) Feed it the payload of Trace 35-C's range 3 and confirm the out-of-range finding. (4) Add a DMRSL check (maximum Length per range) and a DMSL check (sum of Lengths).
Observations
Which finding would destroy live data on a real device?
Questions
(a) Why is the payload byte order the most common source of decoding errors? (b) Why is overlap not an error the device must reject?
Expected reasoning
Analyzers show bytes; ranges are little-endian fields.
Solution

Output: ranges 0 and 3 clean; range 1 misaligned; range 2 at SLBA 0x900 Length 2048; an overlap between range 2 [0x900, 0x1100) and range 0 [0x1000, 0x1008). (2) The bytes 00 09 00 00 read little-endian are 0x0000_0900; 0x9000 would be 00 90 00 00. That slip deallocates 0x900–0x10FF, which may include live data: the destructive finding. (3) Trace 35-C's range (Length 0x20 at 0x7470_6DA0) ends at 0x7470_6DBF ≥ NSZE. (b) Deallocating a block twice is idempotent; the problem is that one of the two ranges is probably wrong. For (4), add if length > DMRSL and accumulate a total compared with DMSL, both skipped when the limit is zero (no limit reported); confirm the units in the NVM Command Set specification.

Extension. Read a hex dump file of many DSM list payloads and output a heat map of deallocated LBAs per GiB.

Advanced lab — discard policy and latency

Safe lab Python 3, standard library.

Objective
Quantify the trade-off between command count and per-command blocking.
Prerequisites
Trace M33-S and Trace 35-B.
Setup
Save as discard_policy.py.
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""discard_policy.py - continuous, batched, or one huge discard?

Synthetic teaching model (standard library only). A filesystem frees EXTENTS extents.
The device charges a fixed cost per DSM command plus a cost per range and per GiB
unmapped, and (in this model) serialises DSM work with other commands on the same
internal FTL core, so a concurrent read waits for the DSM in progress.
All costs are illustrative, not measured.
"""
EXTENTS, GIB_TOTAL = 20_000, 40.0
T_CMD_US, T_RANGE_US, T_GIB_US = 60.0, 2.0, 900.0

def policy(ranges_per_cmd, extents=EXTENTS):
    cmds = -(-extents // ranges_per_cmd)              # ceiling division
    gib_per_cmd = GIB_TOTAL / cmds
    per_cmd = T_CMD_US + ranges_per_cmd * T_RANGE_US + gib_per_cmd * T_GIB_US
    return cmds, cmds * per_cmd / 1000, per_cmd / 1000   # count, busy ms, worst read stall ms

if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(f"{'ranges/cmd':>10} {'commands':>9} {'device busy':>12} {'worst read stall':>17}")
    for r in (1, 16, 256):
        cmds, busy, stall = policy(r)
        print(f"{r:>10} {cmds:>9} {busy:>9.1f} ms {stall:>14.3f} ms")
    cmds, busy, stall = policy(1, extents=1)          # the same 40 GiB as ONE contiguous extent
    print(f"{'1 (40 GiB)':>10} {cmds:>9} {busy:>9.1f} ms {stall:>14.3f} ms")
Procedure
(1) Run it. (2) Set T_GIB_US to 50 (a controller that journals big extents lazily) and rerun. (3) Add a policy that splits any range above 1 GiB (a discard_max_bytes limit) and report the worst stall.
Observations
Which policy minimises total device time, and which minimises the worst stall?
Questions
(a) Why are those two different policies? (b) Which one does Trace 35-B correspond to, and which Trace M33-S?
Expected reasoning
Fixed per-command cost favours batching; per-GiB synchronous cost inside one command favours splitting.
Solution

Default output: 1 range per command → 20,000 commands, ≈1,276 ms busy, 0.064 ms worst stall; 256 per command → 79 commands, ≈81 ms busy, ≈1.0 ms worst stall; one 40 GiB extent → 36 ms busy, but a 36 ms stall. (a) Batching amortises fixed cost; splitting bounds the time any one command holds the FTL. The best practice combines them: many ranges per command, each range capped in size. (b) Trace 35-B is the huge-extent stall; Trace M33-S is the many-small-commands storm. With T_GIB_US = 50, the huge extent becomes harmless — which is why the same fstrim is fine on one drive and painful on another.

Extension. Add a concurrent read stream with Poisson arrivals and compute its 99th-percentile latency under each policy.

Senior forensic lab — the thin pool that never shrinks

Safe lab Evidence analysis.

Objective
Find where discards die in a layered stack and weigh the security trade-off of fixing it.
Prerequisites
This module.
Setup (evidence pack, synthetic)
  • Database hosts on encrypted volumes: filesystem → dm-crypt → LVM → NVMe namespace. Over months, steady-state write latency doubled and the drives' over-provisioning appears exhausted.
  • A weekly fstrim reports "trimmed 1.8 TiB" every week.
  • NVMe tracepoints during fstrim: zero nvme_cmd_dsm commands.
  • lsblk -D shows non-zero discard granularity for the NVMe namespace but zero for the dm-crypt device.
  • dmsetup table for the crypt mapping has no allow_discards option.
  • Identify Namespace: NPDG = 8; DLFEAT = 001b; NUSE equals NCAP and never changes.
Procedure
Identify the first layer that drops discards; decide owner and fix; state the trade-off and the verification.
Observations
Contrast what fstrim reports with what reaches the driver.
Questions
(1) Why does fstrim report success? (2) What does enabling discards through encryption reveal to an attacker with the raw drive? (3) How do you verify the fix?
Expected reasoning
A layer that does not advertise discard support makes the block layer return without sending anything downstream.
Solution

The dm-crypt mapping does not allow discards, so the discard stops there; the filesystem's fstrim reports the free space it asked to trim (and in some versions the kernel reports nothing sent). The SSD therefore believes almost all LBAs are live, garbage collection copies dead data, and write amplification rises — the latency doubling. Owner: platform storage configuration. Fix: enable discards on the crypt mapping (and any other stacked layer), then run fstrim once and verify with DSM tracepoints and steady-state write latency. NUSE gives no evidence here because this drive reports it statically. Trade-off: discards through encryption reveal which blocks are free (and thus file-size and allocation patterns) to someone with the raw device; many organisations accept that, some do not — document the decision. Confidence: high.

Extension. Write the monitoring check that would have caught this: periodic comparison of filesystem-reported trims with device DSM counts.

Debugging

Six patterns beyond Chapter 35's five.

Trim reports success; drive performance keeps degrading

Symptom
Steady-state write latency rises over months despite weekly fstrim.
Possible causes
Discards dropped by a stacked layer; filesystem discard disabled; ranges below NPDG ignored; drive simply full.
Evidence
DSM tracepoints during fstrim; lsblk -D per layer; NPDG; filesystem fill level.
First check
Do any DSMs reach the NVMe driver during fstrim?
Second check
If yes, are range sizes and alignments compatible with NPDG?
Disambiguating experiment
On a test system, blkdiscard a large aligned scratch area and measure steady-state write latency before and after.
Likely conclusion
A non-forwarding layer (senior lab).
Confidence
High once tracepoints show zero DSMs.

Latency storm during deletes

Symptom
Trace M33-S.
Possible causes
Continuous discard producing many small DSMs; synchronous controller unmap; queue sharing.
Evidence
DSM count per second, NR per DSM, DSM latency, interleaved write latency.
First check
Is the filesystem mounted with continuous discard?
Second check
Average ranges per DSM (tracepoint NR).
Disambiguating experiment
Same deletion with batched discard later.
Likely conclusion
Policy, not a defect.
Confidence
High.

Reads fail on recently freed space

Symptom
Trace M33-F; errors look like media errors.
Possible causes
DULBE enabled; genuine media errors; reading unwritten LBAs after a namespace was formatted.
Evidence
Exact status code; feature 05h; SMART media-error counter; trim history.
First check
Decode the status precisely (not just the type).
Second check
Feature 05h DULBE for the namespace.
Disambiguating experiment
Write the LBA and read it again: success means it was a deallocated block, not damaged media.
Likely conclusion
Configuration leftover.
Confidence
High.

Old data returned after trim

Symptom
Trace M33-A on a DLFEAT 001b drive.
Possible causes
Sub-granular range; discard not delivered; deallocation undone by power loss before Flush; read racing the DSM; device bug.
Evidence
Range alignment vs NPDG/NPDA; DSM tracepoint; power events; I/O timing.
First check
Was the range a whole aligned unit?
Second check
Did the DSM complete before the read was submitted?
Disambiguating experiment
Deallocate the aligned unit and re-read.
Likely conclusion
Usually granularity.
Confidence
Medium until the experiment.

DSM rejected after a firmware update

Symptom
Every large fstrim DSM fails with Invalid Field in Command (or similar) after updating the drive.
Possible causes
New firmware reports DMRL/DMRSL/DMSL limits the kernel does not yet honour; older kernels ignore those fields.
Evidence
nvme nvm-id-ctrl before/after; NR and range sizes in failing commands.
First check
Compare failing NR with DMRL and range lengths with DMRSL.
Second check
Kernel version support for those limits.
Disambiguating experiment
Lower discard_max_bytes (writable on many kernels) and retry.
Likely conclusion
Host not honouring newly reported limits Version-dependent.
Confidence
Medium to high.

Space not reclaimed in a thin-provisioned namespace

Symptom
A thin-provisioned namespace's NUSE stays high after deleting most data.
Possible causes
Discards not forwarded; sub-granular ranges; controller reclaims lazily and updates NUSE later; NUSE not dynamically tracked by this controller.
Evidence
NSFEAT thin-provisioning support; NUSE over time; DSM tracepoints.
First check
Does the namespace report thin provisioning at all?
Second check
Does NUSE change after a large aligned blkdiscard on a scratch range?
Disambiguating experiment
Measure NUSE immediately and an hour after a known DSM.
Likely conclusion
Either forwarding or lazy accounting; the experiment separates them.
Confidence
Medium.

Interview

Beginner

  1. What does Deallocate tell the drive?

    Model answer

    That the host no longer needs the data in the listed LBA ranges, so the FTL may unmap them and garbage collection may stop preserving them.

  2. Is NR 0's based? Is Length?

    Model answer

    NR is 0's based; each range's Length is a plain count of LBAs.

  3. Does a trimmed block read as zeros?

    Model answer

    Only if DLFEAT reports 001b and the block was actually deallocated; with 000b nothing is promised.

  4. Is deallocation a secure erase?

    Model answer

    No; old pages persist until garbage collection erases them. Use Sanitize or cryptographic erase.

  5. Which way does the range list travel on PCIe?

    Model answer

    Host to device, pulled by the controller with Memory Reads, like write data.

Intermediate

  1. A 4 KiB range list starts at offset 0x800 in a page. How is it described and fetched?

    Model answer

    PRP1 at the list, PRP2 the second page; 8 MRds of 512 B, 4 per page.

  2. Why does NPDG matter to Linux?

    Model answer

    It sets discard_granularity so the block layer aligns and sizes discards to units the drive can free.

  3. What is DULBE and when is it useful?

    Model answer

    An Error Recovery setting making reads of deallocated blocks fail; useful for software that must distinguish never-written data, harmful to whole-device readers.

  4. When should you use Write Zeroes instead of DSM?

    Model answer

    When the host needs guaranteed zeros on read-back; Write Zeroes may still deallocate internally when allowed.

  5. Why is a failed DSM hard to retry correctly?

    Model answer

    One status covers all ranges; the host cannot know which were processed. Resubmit valid ranges; deallocation is idempotent.

Advanced

  1. Explain continuous versus batched discard trade-offs.

    Model answer

    Continuous informs the drive early but issues many small commands with fixed costs; batched amortises cost but delays information and can produce huge extents that stall synchronous controllers. Combine batching with range-size caps.

  2. Why should deallocation be followed by Flush when it must survive power loss?

    Model answer

    The map change may be in volatile memory like cached write data; without Flush a power loss can revert it, making old data reappear.

  3. How do large mapping units affect deallocation?

    Model answer

    Only whole units can be freed; scattered small frees accomplish nothing until neighbours are freed; NPDG reports the preferred unit.

  4. What evidence proves a discard reached the SSD?

    Model answer

    A DSM at the NVMe driver tracepoint or on the link with the expected ranges; filesystem reports are not proof.

  5. Why might a DRAM-less drive show device-initiated host-memory traffic during big deallocations?

    Model answer

    Its map lives partly in the Host Memory Buffer; updating map entries means DMA to host memory unrelated to the range list.

Senior

  1. Design a validation plan for DSM.

    Model answer

    NR 0, 255 and above DMRL; ranges at LBA 0, NSZE−1, beyond; zero-length and overlapping ranges; aligned/unaligned to NPDG/NPDA; read-back per DLFEAT; DULBE on/off; list at page offsets and via SGL; concurrent I/O latency; power loss after DSM with and without Flush; steady-state write performance before/after.

  2. Should encrypted volumes pass discards?

    Model answer

    It is a trade-off between SSD health/performance and leaking free-space patterns; decide explicitly per threat model and document it.

  3. A customer says trim corrupted their data. How do you investigate?

    Model answer

    Capture DSM ranges and compare with the files' extents; look for byte-order or 0's-based errors, stale extent maps after resize, racing writes; only then examine device unmap logic.

  4. How would you set fleet-wide discard policy?

    Model answer

    Batched trims at quiet times with range-size caps, verified by DSM counters; continuous discard only where filesystems implement it asynchronously and drives handle it well; monitoring that compares filesystem trims to device DSMs.

  5. How does deallocation interact with ZNS?

    Model answer

    Zoned namespaces reclaim space per zone with Zone Management reset; LBA-granular deallocation is not the mechanism there (see the ZNS module).

Quiz

  1. A 256-range list is at IOVA 0x2_0002_0F00. How many pages does it touch?

    • 1
    • 2
    • 3
    • 4
    Answer and reasoning

    256 B in the first page, 3,840 B in the second: two pages.

  2. DLFEAT bits 2:0 = 000b. After deallocating LBA X, a read returns:

    • Zeros
    • FFh bytes
    • An error
    • Unspecified data
    Answer and reasoning

    000b means not reported; nothing may be assumed (DULBE would make it an error if enabled).

  3. Which command guarantees zeros on read-back?

    • Write Zeroes
    • DSM with AD
    • Flush
    • DSM with IDW
    Answer and reasoning

    Write Zeroes is defined to return zeros.

  4. A DSM fails with LBA Out of Range. Which ranges were processed?

    • None
    • All except the bad one
    • Unknown from the status
    • Those before the bad one
    Answer and reasoning

    No per-range status exists.

  5. The bytes 00 90 00 00 00 00 00 00 in a range's SLBA field mean SLBA =

    • 0x0090_0000
    • 0x0000_9000
    • 0x9000_0000_0000_0000
    • 0x0000_0090
    Answer and reasoning

    Little-endian: least significant byte first.

  6. Twenty thousand single-range DSMs versus 79 DSMs of 256 ranges for the same extents: the main cost difference is

    • Fixed per-command overhead
    • PCIe bandwidth for the lists
    • NAND erase time
    • Interrupt count only
    Answer and reasoning

    Lists are tiny; per-command processing dominates.

  7. Why is "fstrim trimmed 1.8 TiB" not evidence that the SSD learned anything?

    Model answer

    It reports what the filesystem requested; a lower layer may have dropped the discards. Only DSMs at the driver or on the link prove delivery.

  8. Name two legal reasons for old data after trim on a DLFEAT 001b drive.

    Model answer

    The range did not cover a whole mapping unit so it was not deallocated; a power loss reverted an unflushed deallocation. (A racing write is a third.)

  9. What does a Read error with the Deallocated or Unwritten Logical Block status tell you?

    Model answer

    DULBE is enabled and the LBA is deallocated or never written; not a media failure.

Teach-back

  1. To a child: why telling the toy librarian about unwanted toys makes her faster.

    Model explanation

    When she cleans a shelf she must carry every wanted toy to a new shelf first. If you tell her which toys you don't want, she carries fewer, so she has more time to lend toys.

  2. To a junior engineer: how to tell whether trim is working on a server.

    Model explanation

    Check the drive supports DSM (ONCS bit 2) and its NPDG; check lsblk -D on every layer from the filesystem down; enable the NVMe setup tracepoint and run fstrim; you should see nvme_cmd_dsm commands. If you see none, a layer is dropping them. Then watch steady-state write latency over time.

  3. To a senior engineer: why deallocation is a cross-layer contract, not a command.

    Model explanation

    Its value depends on information surviving every layer, matching the FTL's mapping unit, arriving at a rate and size the controller can process without stalling I/O, being made durable like any other metadata change, and being interpreted on read-back exactly as DLFEAT and DULBE say. Each property is owned by a different layer, so the review must cover filesystem policy, stacked-device configuration, block-layer limits, controller behaviour and the security posture of exposing free space.
