Traces are the evidence of this profession. This page teaches how to handle them honestly and productively: how synthetic traces must be labelled, how real traces must be sourced and protected, an eleven-level progression from reading a packet table to designing a regression test, how to normalise raw captures into events, how to build and maintain golden traces, and how to diff a good trace against a bad one to find the first divergence, with a runnable Python example.

Why this part exists

A protocol analyzer capture of a PCI Express (PCIe) link carrying NVM Express (NVMe) traffic can contain millions of packets. Two mistakes ruin most trace work. The first is provenance confusion: a teaching example is mistaken for real behaviour, or a real capture is shared without its context (or with confidential details). The second is unstructured looking: scrolling through rows hoping something stands out, instead of comparing against a known-good reference in a normalised form.

This part fixes both. It defines what every trace must say about itself, gives a staged path for building trace-reading skill, and introduces the three techniques that make large traces tractable: normalisation, golden traces and diffing.

Learning objectives

  • Label a synthetic trace so that it cannot be mistaken for a capture, and record the provenance of a real one.
  • Identify what must be removed or protected before a real trace is shared.
  • Work a trace through the eleven analyzer levels, from reading the table to designing a regression test.
  • Convert raw capture rows into a normalised event schema, group them into transactions and build a timeline.
  • Explain why deterministic normalisation must happen before any Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning.
  • Define a golden trace, list its five properties, and state what may and may not differ in a matching run.
  • Align a good trace with a bad trace and report the first divergence, using a standard-library Python program.

The story

A school has two kinds of pictures of the class volcano experiment. One is a drawing the teacher made before the experiment, showing what should happen: the foam rises, spills over the left side, and stops. The other is a photo taken by a parent during the real experiment. The teacher writes "DRAWING — what we expect" on the first and "PHOTO — 3 May, Ms Lee's camera" on the second.

Next year the volcano goes wrong. The class puts last year's good photo next to this year's photo and looks for the first thing that is different: this year, the foam started before the vinegar was poured. Someone had put vinegar in the bottle already. The first difference was the clue.

A cardiologist learns from textbook ECG drawings (synthetic, idealised, labelled as such) and then reads real patient ECGs (which carry the patient's identity, the machine, the date, and must be anonymised before teaching). To diagnose, she compares a patient's ECG with their own previous normal ECG (a golden reference for that person), after aligning the beats, and looks for the first segment that differs.

What maps well: drawings vs photos (synthetic vs real), captions with provenance, comparison against a known-good reference, first difference.

What does not map: a photo shows the whole scene; a PCIe capture shows only one link, one probe point, perhaps filtered. An ECG beat repeats; NVMe traffic interleaves many independent commands whose order legitimately changes between runs.

What it hides: before you can compare two traces you must normalise them: addresses, tags and timestamps differ between perfectly good runs. Neither analogy prepares you for that.

The synthetic trace rule

Every invented trace is labelled: "SYNTHETIC TEACHING TRACE — NOT A REAL DEVICE CAPTURE." A synthetic trace must never be made to look like a verified real-world capture.

On this website the label is generated from data-kind="synthetic", and the course adds the sentence under each trace. In your own documents, put the label in the title and in the file itself (not only in the email that carries it), because files travel without their emails.

Honest synthetic traces: a checklist

  • Label in the title and the file.
  • No real identities: no real product names, serial numbers, firmware versions, customer host names or real capture-file headers.
  • Consistent with a stated system: the course uses one imaginary lab machine (Lab SSD at 0000:01:00.0, Gen4 x4, BAR0 0xFE60_0000, MPS 256, MRRS 512, RCB 64). State the system so readers can check arithmetic.
  • Plausible values, stated as illustrative: timings like "SQE fetch ≈ 0.9 µs after the doorbell" are teaching values, not measurements.
  • Assumptions written down: filtered or not, both directions or not, which rows are summarised.
  • No fake precision: do not invent analyzer-specific columns, error codes or decode names that imply a particular tool.

Counterexample

A slide shows a table titled "Capture_0412_customerX.trace — Gen5 x4" with a vendor-looking header, real-looking serial number and a "UR" row, and a footnote "illustrative". Six months later the slide is in a customer escalation as "evidence that the drive issues bad reads". The footnote did not travel; the realistic header did. A correct synthetic version is titled "SYNTHETIC TEACHING TRACE — NOT A REAL DEVICE CAPTURE: UR on a data read (lab system)" and contains nothing that names a customer, a product or a file.

What synthetic traces can and cannot teach

Uses and limits
Can teachCannot teach

A drawing says "drawing"; a photo says who took it and when.

Every trace declares provenance: synthetic, real, excerpted, reconstructed or conceptual.

A real trace comes from a probe on one link (interposer, slot or mid-bus), with a capture configuration; a synthetic trace comes from a person or a generator.

Neither kind is a specification. A real trace shows what one device did; the specification says what it should do.

The protocol content of both is decoded the same way; only the claims you may draw differ.

In a report, every trace excerpt carries its provenance, capture configuration and coverage statement.

In debugging, provenance decides weight: a synthetic trace can generate hypotheses, only real evidence can confirm them.

Protecting confidential information

A PCIe/NVMe capture can contain more than you think:

  • Data payloads: user data from the drive (files, database pages). Strip or zero payloads that are not needed.
  • Identify data: serial numbers, model numbers, firmware revisions, vendor-specific fields, NVMe Qualified Names.
  • Platform details: host names in accompanying logs, IOMMU layouts, BAR maps that identify unreleased hardware.
  • Vendor-specific commands and log pages: often under non-disclosure agreements.
  • Security-related traffic: security send/receive payloads, keys, authentication exchanges.

Redact by replacing values consistently (so correlation still works: every occurrence of one serial number becomes "SN-A"), and record that you did. An excerpt that has been modified is labelled excerpted, and if fields were synthesised to fill gaps, reconstructed.

Never paste an unredacted customer capture into an external tool (including an online LLM service) without explicit permission. The rule exists for legal, contractual and privacy reasons, not as courtesy.

Synthetic teaching trace — not a real device capture.

Level 0 — Read a packet table

Task: for each row, say who sent it, in which direction, and whether it carries data.

Answer

Rows 1 and 10: root complex to device, MWr with 1 DWORD of data. Row 2: device to host, MRd (no data, 4DW header because the address is above 4 GiB). Row 3: root complex to device, CplD with 32 DWORDs. Rows 4, 5, 7, 8: device to host, MWr with data. Rows 6, 9: device to host, 3DW MWr with 1 DWORD (address below 4 GiB).

Level 1 — Identify requester and completer

Task: for the non-posted request, name requester and completer.

Answer

Row 2's requester is the SSD (Requester ID 0x0100, bus 01 device 00 function 0). The completer for host memory is the root complex (row 3, Completer ID shown as RC). All other rows are posted writes: they have a requester but no completer and no completion.

Level 2 — Decode fields

Task: decode row 2 completely.

Answer

Memory Read, 4DW header (64-bit address), Length 32 DW = 128 bytes, Tag 0x40, First and Last DW byte enables all set, address 0x1_2340_0480. 0x480 / 64 = 18, so it starts at SQ1 slot 18 and covers slots 18 and 19 (two SQEs).

Level 3 — Correlate request and completion

Task: match row 3 to its request and check completeness.

Answer

Requester 0x0100 + Tag 0x40 match row 2. Byte Count 128 equals the request size and Length 32 DW equals 128 bytes, so this single completion finishes the request. Status SC.

Level 4 — Identify NVMe transactions

Task: fill the "meaning" column in NVMe terms.

Answer

Row 1: SQ1 tail doorbell = 20 (0x14). Rows 2–3: fetch of SQEs in slots 18–19. Rows 4 (+15): data for SQE B (buffer 0x2_0000_B000). Row 5: CQE in CQ1 slot 18 (0x120 / 16). Row 6: MSI-X interrupt (vector identity needs the data value; address in the x86 interrupt window). Rows 7 (+15): data for SQE A. Row 8: CQE in CQ1 slot 19. Row 9: MSI-X. Row 10: CQ1 head doorbell = 20.

Level 5 — Reconstruct queue behaviour

Task: build the queue ledger for SQ1 and CQ1.

Answer

SQ1: tail 18 → 20 (two commands submitted with one doorbell). Controller head after the single fetch of both entries: 20, reported as SQHD 20 in both CQEs. CQ slots are independent of SQ slots: the CQE for the command in SQ slot 19 landed in CQ slot 18, because it completed first. CQ1: tail 18 → 20, phase 1, head 18 → 20 at row 10. Two outstanding commands on SQ1 both carry CID 0x0007.

Level 6 — Identify the anomaly

Task: name the first abnormal event.

Answer

Row 3: the payload shows two simultaneously outstanding commands on SQ1 with the same CID (0x0007). The specification requires the CID to be unique among outstanding commands on a submission queue (the host's obligation; confirm the clause in the Base Specification's command format section). Everything the device did afterwards is protocol-correct: it completed B before A, which is permitted.

Level 7 — Form root-cause hypotheses

Task: list hypotheses for the application's stale data.

Answer

H1: the host reused CID 7 while it was still outstanding; when the first CQE (for B) arrived, the host matched CID 7 to A and reported A complete before A's data had been written, so the application read stale data. H2: the device wrote A's data to the wrong buffer (contradicted by row 7: A's data went to A's PRP1). H3: an IOMMU mapping error sent data elsewhere (no evidence; addresses match PRPs). H4: cache coherency issue on the host (possible but needs H1 excluded first).

Level 8 — Prove or disprove

Task: design the experiment that separates H1 from H4.

Answer

Instrument the driver's CID allocator (or tracepoints at submission and completion) and record which request each CQE was matched to. H1 predicts: CQE at row 5 is matched to request A. H4 predicts correct matching but stale cache lines. A second experiment: force unique CIDs (allocator fix or one outstanding command per CID) and rerun the workload; if stale data disappears, H1 is confirmed.

Level 9 — Write the bug report

Answer

Title: Host reuses CID 0x0007 on SQ1 while outstanding; completion mis-attributed, application reads stale data.

Environment: lab system, Gen4 x4, firmware and kernel versions, driver build.

Observation: SQ1 slots 18 and 19 both contain Read commands with CID 0x0007 (trace P row 3, payload). The device completed slot 19 first (row 5), then slot 18 (row 8); both completions success.

Expected: unique CIDs among outstanding commands per SQ.

Impact: application observed stale data for LBA 0x1000.

Root cause (confidence high after experiment): CID allocator race in the host driver (location).

Evidence: trace P rows 3, 5, 8; driver tracepoints showing CQE at row 5 matched to request A.

Not the cause: device data placement (row 7 matches A's PRP1).

Fix and regression test: below.

Level 10 — Design the regression test

Answer

A stress test at high queue depth with mixed sizes, plus a checker on the capture or on driver tracepoints that asserts "no CID appears twice among outstanding commands on one SQ". Add a data-integrity check (each read buffer pre-filled with a sentinel pattern and verified against expected content) so that any mis-attribution is caught even without a trace. Run it in every driver release's regression suite; keep trace P as a known-bad reference for the checker.

Trace normalization

Raw captures are hard to compare, search or hand to automation. Normalisation converts them through five stages:

Raw capturevendor file: symbols, packets, timestamps, analyzer metadata
Normalised event schemaone row per event, fixed fields (the event model), decoded and checked
Transaction groupsrequest + completions; doorbell + fetch + data + CQE + interrupt per command
Timelineordered groups with expected-next annotations
Anomalyfirst mismatch between expected and observed, with evidence

What normalisation does

Normalisation operations
OperationExampleWhy
Decode and verifyFmt/Type checked against the labelCatches decoder misconfiguration early
UnitsLength in DWORDs → bytes; time to ns on one clockPrevents factor-of-four and clock-domain errors
Region-relative addresses0x1_2340_0480 → SQ1+0x480 (slot 18)Survives different IOVAs between runs
Symbolic tags0x40 → T0 (first tag used)Tag values legitimately differ between runs
Correlation IDsSQ1:CID0x0007#18 (CID plus slot, because CIDs can repeat)Groups cross-layer events
GroupingOne MRd + its CplDs = one groupDetects missing or extra completions

Why deterministic normalisation comes before LLM reasoning

  • Reproducibility: the same capture always produces the same events; an LLM's reading of raw rows can vary run to run.
  • Auditability: every event points to a capture row; every LLM claim can then be required to cite event IDs that a program can check.
  • Correctness of arithmetic: slot numbers, byte counts and address offsets are computed exactly by code; language models are unreliable at bit and hex arithmetic.
  • Volume: millions of packets do not fit in a prompt; normalised, grouped and filtered events do.
  • Hallucination control: the model reasons over facts that were established deterministically and cannot "see" a packet that does not exist in the event list.

The full pipeline is built in Automated Trace Analysis; how to use an LLM safely on top of it is in AI + NVMe Protocol Analysis.

What may differ and what must match

Between two good runs, timestamps, tag values, IOVA bases and queue slots legitimately change. Independent commands may interleave differently. What must match is the shape of each command: the sequence of event types per correlation group, directions, regions, lengths and statuses, and timings within tolerance. Writing these rules into the golden manifest is what makes automated comparison possible.

A golden trace is only golden for its configuration. Change MPS from 256 to 128 and the "sixteen 256-byte writes" become thirty-two 128-byte writes; that is not a regression. Diff against the golden trace for the same configuration, or normalise data movement into "bytes per region" before comparing.

Lifecycle

  • Create: capture several runs, normalise, confirm they agree within tolerance, review against the specification, then freeze.
  • Use: every regression run is normalised and diffed against the golden trace for its configuration.
  • Update: only when behaviour intentionally changes (new firmware with a different fetch policy), with a new version and a review note.
  • Retire: when the configuration is no longer supported, keeping the file for history.

Trace diffing

The program

The standard-library Python program below holds a golden and a suspect trace as CSV text (both synthetic: a 1 KiB Read whose buffer starts 0xE00 into a page, so it spans two pages and uses PRP1 and PRP2). It normalises addresses to regions, replaces tags with symbols, aligns the two event sequences with difflib.SequenceMatcher, prints the aligned view and reports the first divergence.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""trace_diff.py - align a golden trace with a suspect trace and report the first divergence.

SYNTHETIC TEACHING TRACES - NOT REAL DEVICE CAPTURES.
Standard library only. Run: python3 trace_diff.py
"""
import csv
import difflib
import io

GOLDEN_CSV = """t_ns,dir,type,addr,len,tag,status,note

910,up,MRd,0x123400140,16,0x21,-,fetch SQE slot 5

48420,up,MWr,0x200000F00,64,-,-,data bytes 256-511 (PRP1 page)

SUSPECT_CSV = """t_ns,dir,type,addr,len,tag,status,note

870,up,MRd,0x123400140,16,0x2a,-,fetch SQE slot 5

48610,up,MWr,0x200002100,64,-,-,data bytes 768-1023 (PRP2 page)
49050,up,MWr,0x123410050,4,-,-,CQE to CQ1 slot 5
49270,up,MWr,0xFEE00000,1,-,-,MSI-X vector 1
"""

# Region table used for normalization: (name, base, size). Values follow the
# course's synthetic lab system (notes: BAR0 0xFE60_0000, SQ1, CQ1, data buffer).
REGIONS = [
    ("BAR0", 0xFE600000, 0x4000),
    ("SQ1", 0x123400000, 0x10000),
    ("CQ1", 0x123410000, 0x4000),
    ("DATA", 0x200000000, 0x100000),
    ("MSIX", 0xFEE00000, 0x100000),
]


def load(text):
    """Parse CSV text into a list of dicts (one per event)."""
    return list(csv.DictReader(io.StringIO(text)))


def region(addr_text):
    """Return 'NAME+0xOFFSET' for an address, or the raw text if unknown."""
    if addr_text in ("", "-"):
        return "-"
    a = int(addr_text, 16)
    for name, base, size in REGIONS:
        if base <= a < base + size:
            return f"{name}+0x{a - base:X}"
    return f"UNMAPPED:{addr_text}"


def normalize(events):
    """Deterministic normalization: keep what must match, drop what may vary.

    - absolute timestamps   -> dropped from the key (kept for reporting)
    - addresses             -> region-relative (survives a different IOVA base)
    - PCIe tags             -> symbolic T0, T1 ... in order of first use
    """
    tag_map, out = {}, []
    for i, e in enumerate(events):
        tag = e["tag"]
        if tag != "-":
            tag = tag_map.setdefault(tag.lower(), f"T{len(tag_map)}")
        key = (e["dir"], e["type"], region(e["addr"]), e["len"], tag, e["status"])
        out.append({"idx": i, "t_ns": int(e["t_ns"]), "key": key, "note": e["note"]})
    return out


def fmt(ev):
    d, typ, reg, ln, tag, st = ev["key"]
    return f"#{ev['idx'] + 1:<2} {d:<4} {typ:<4} {reg:<18} len={ln:<3} tag={tag:<3} st={st:<2} ({ev['note']})"


def diff(golden, suspect):
    g, s = normalize(golden), normalize(suspect)
    sm = difflib.SequenceMatcher(a=[e["key"] for e in g], b=[e["key"] for e in s], autojunk=False)
    first = None
    print("Aligned comparison (golden | suspect):")
    for op, i1, i2, j1, j2 in sm.get_opcodes():
        if op == "equal":
            for gi, sj in zip(range(i1, i2), range(j1, j2)):
                print(f"  =  {fmt(g[gi])}")
            continue
        if first is None:
            first = (op, i1, j1)
        for gi in range(i1, i2):
            print(f"  -  {fmt(g[gi])}")
        for sj in range(j1, j2):
            print(f"  +  {fmt(s[sj])}")
    print()
    if first is None:
        print("No divergence: suspect matches the golden trace after normalization.")
        return
    op, i, j = first
    print("FIRST DIVERGENCE")
    print(f"  kind     : {op}")
    if i < len(g):
        print(f"  expected : {fmt(g[i])}")
    else:
        print("  expected : (end of golden trace)")
    if j < len(s):
        print(f"  observed : {fmt(s[j])}")
    else:
        print("  observed : (no corresponding event in the captured interval)")
    print("  note     : the first divergence is where investigation starts;")
    print("             it is not automatically the root cause.")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    diff(load(GOLDEN_CSV), load(SUSPECT_CSV))

  -  #10 down MWr  BAR0+0x100C        len=1   tag=-   st=-  (CQ1 head doorbell = 6)

FIRST DIVERGENCE
  kind     : replace
  expected : #6  up   MWr  DATA+0x1000        len=64  tag=-   st=-  (data bytes 512-767 (PRP2 page))
  observed : #6  up   MWr  DATA+0x2000        len=64  tag=-   st=-  (data bytes 512-767 (PRP2 page))
  note     : the first divergence is where investigation starts;
             it is not automatically the root cause.

Reading the result

  • Normalisation worked: the suspect's tag (0x2a vs 0x21) and timings differ, yet rows 1–5 align as equal.
  • First divergence: the third data write goes to DATA+0x2000 instead of DATA+0x1000. That is the PRP2 page.
  • Not the root cause yet: the device wrote where PRP2 told it to. The preceding cause is the SQE's PRP2 value, which the suspect capture did not export (row 3's note: "payload not exported"). Hypotheses: the host put the wrong PRP2 in the SQE; the device read PRP2 wrongly; an address mapping changed. The next evidence is the SQE payload (re-export the capture with payloads, or host-side tracing).
  • The last line: the golden trace has a CQ1 head doorbell; the suspect does not. Before calling that a second divergence, check coverage: the suspect capture may simply have ended earlier. This is negative evidence and needs a coverage statement.

Choosing an alignment key

Sequence alignment on a single stream works for one command at a time. For many interleaved commands, first group by correlation ID (SQ + CID + slot), then align groups by command content (opcode, SLBA, NLB), then align events inside each group. Without grouping, a harmless change in interleaving looks like a massive divergence.

Safe lab Standard-library Python on synthetic data.

Objective

Extend trace_diff.py to check timing tolerances and to report coverage-limited absences separately.

Skills

Hardware required

None.

Software required

Python 3.8+.

Input

trace_diff.py.

Expected output

The same first divergence, plus a timing check per aligned pair, plus a separate line: "trailing golden events not visible in suspect capture (check coverage)".

Background

Golden traces and diffing sections.

Step 1

Add a SUSPECT_END_NS constant (the suspect capture's end time). If the golden's trailing events would fall after it (using golden relative timing), classify them as "outside suspect coverage" rather than divergences.

Step 2

For each "equal" pair, compute the time since the first event in each trace and flag differences above 20 percent.

Step 3

Change the suspect so that the SQE fetch occurs 5 µs after the doorbell, and check the timing flag.

Observation

Timing differences are reported but do not stop the alignment.

Questions

  1. Why should timing be checked separately from the event key?
  2. Why compute times relative to the first event?

Challenge

Group events by command before aligning, so that two interleaved commands in different order do not produce a divergence.

Hint

Assign each event to a group using the SQ slot from the fetch address and, for data writes, the PRP regions from the SQE payload. Then compare groups as units.

Solution

Timing belongs outside the key because legitimate runs vary; putting time into the key would make every row "different". Relative times remove the arbitrary capture start. For Step 1, compute golden offsets t - g[0]['t_ns'] and suspect coverage SUSPECT_END_NS - s[0]['t_ns']; golden events whose offset exceeds the suspect's coverage go to a "not visible in suspect capture" list. For the challenge: build groups = {slot: [events]} from fetch addresses, map data writes to the slot whose PRP region contains the address, then align groups by (opcode, SLBA, NLB) and diff within each group.

What a senior engineer notices

Whether the "divergence" survives normalisation. Most raw differences are noise; the program's value is that it removes noise deterministically.

Failure variants

  • Region table wrong (buffer outside DATA): every address becomes UNMAPPED and everything diverges.
  • Comparing traces from different MPS settings.

Extension

Read the two CSVs from files given on the command line, and export your own analyzer's CSV into this schema.

Quiz

  1. Which label must every invented trace carry?

    • "Illustrative"
    • "SYNTHETIC TEACHING TRACE — NOT A REAL DEVICE CAPTURE"
    • "Excerpted"
    • No label is needed if the numbers are plausible
    Answer and reasoning

    The full label, in the title and the file. "Illustrative" in a footnote does not travel with the trace.

  2. Which of these may legitimately differ between a golden trace and a good run?

    • CQE status
    • The order of events within one command
    • Tag values
    • The region each data write targets
    Answer and reasoning

    Tags are allocated dynamically; they differ run to run. The others define the command's correct behaviour.

  3. Why normalise addresses to "region + offset" before diffing?

    • Because IOVAs and allocations differ between good runs
    • To hide confidential addresses
    • Because the analyzer requires it
    • To reduce file size
    Answer and reasoning

    Raw addresses change between equally correct runs; region-relative addresses keep the meaning. (Redaction is a separate concern.)

  4. At which analyzer level do you first need NVMe knowledge rather than PCIe knowledge?

    • Level 1
    • Level 3
    • Level 4
    • Level 9
    Answer and reasoning

    Levels 0–3 are pure PCIe (table, requester/completer, fields, request/completion). Level 4 maps TLPs to NVMe transactions.

  5. List the five properties of a golden trace.

    Model answer

    Versioned, reproducible, labelled, configuration-documented, known-good.

  6. Why must deterministic normalisation precede LLM reasoning?

    Model answer

    It produces reproducible, auditable, exactly computed events that fit in a prompt; the model can then be required to cite event IDs, and it cannot invent packets that are not in the event list. LLMs are unreliable at bit/hex arithmetic and vary between runs.

  7. The diff's first divergence is a data write to the wrong page. Is that the root cause?

    Model answer

    Not necessarily. It is where investigation starts. The device writes where the PRP entries tell it; the preceding cause is likely the PRP value in the SQE or list, which must be checked in the payload or on the host.

  8. What must be recorded about a real trace?

    Model answer

    Source, capture tool, versions where known, whether it is public, limitations, and what confidential information was removed or must be protected.

  9. Why group events by command before aligning traces with many outstanding commands?

    Model answer

    Commands complete in different orders between good runs; aligning a single interleaved stream turns harmless reordering into false divergences. Grouping by correlation ID and aligning per group isolates real differences.

Interview questions

  1. (Beginner) What is the difference between a synthetic and a real trace?

    Model answer

    A synthetic trace is invented for teaching or test design and shows what should or could happen; a real trace is captured from hardware and shows what one device did under one configuration. Each must be labelled; only real traces are evidence about a device.

  2. (Beginner) What is a golden trace for?

    Model answer

    It is the reviewed, known-good reference for a workload and configuration, used to detect regressions by comparison.

  3. (Intermediate) How would you diff two traces that use different tags and IOVAs?

    Model answer

    Normalise first: map addresses to regions with offsets, replace tags by symbolic order of use, drop absolute timestamps from the comparison key, group by command, then align groups and events; report the first divergence and check timing within tolerance separately.

  4. (Intermediate) What would you remove from a customer capture before sharing it with a vendor?

    Model answer

    Anything the agreements do not allow: user data payloads not needed for the analysis, identifiers of the customer's systems, and any confidential vendor-specific content from other parties. I would replace values consistently, record the redaction, and label the file as excerpted.

  5. (Advanced) A golden trace was captured with MPS 256; the failing system uses MPS 128. How do you compare?

    Model answer

    Either capture a golden trace for MPS 128, or compare at a level that is invariant to MPS: bytes per region, ordering of regions, CQE status. Raw TLP counts will differ legitimately.

  6. (Advanced) Your diff shows a missing trailing event in the bad trace. How do you report it?

    Model answer

    As "not visible in the suspect capture", with the suspect capture's end time and coverage. It becomes a divergence only if the capture covered the expected time and direction without filters.

  7. (Senior) How would you organise golden traces for a product line?

    Model answer

    A versioned library keyed by workload and configuration, each with a manifest (provenance, capture settings, config, expected normalised events, tolerances, may-differ/must-match rules, reviewer). Automated normalisation and diff in CI for firmware and driver releases. Updates only through reviewed changes tied to intended behaviour changes.

  8. (Senior) How do you keep synthetic training traces from being mistaken for evidence?

    Model answer

    Label in title and file, use a documented imaginary system, avoid real names and file headers, keep them in a separate repository from real captures, and reject any report that cites a synthetic trace as evidence.

Teach-back challenge

Explain trace diffing to a child, a junior engineer and a senior engineer.

Model explanations

Child: "Put last year's good photo next to this year's and find the very first thing that looks different."

Junior: "Turn both captures into the same event format, replace things that always change (tags, addresses, timestamps) with stable names, line the events up, and look at the first row that does not match. Then ask what caused that row."

Senior: "Diffing is only as good as normalisation and the golden trace's configuration match. We group by command to tolerate reordering, compare invariants per group, check timing within tolerances, and treat trailing absences under coverage rules. The first divergence starts a backward search; it is not the root cause."

Summary and mastery gate

Label synthetic traces unmistakably; document and protect real ones. Grow skill through the eleven levels. Normalise deterministically before any automated or LLM reasoning. Keep versioned, reproducible, labelled, configuration-documented, known-good golden traces, and diff against them layer by layer to find the first divergence.

Do not move on until you can:

  • label a synthetic trace and write a real-trace provenance record
  • list what must be redacted from a real capture
  • work a trace through levels 0–10
  • normalise raw rows into events, groups and a timeline
  • explain why normalisation precedes LLM reasoning
  • write a golden-trace manifest with may-differ and must-match rules
  • run and extend trace_diff.py and interpret its first divergence
